OCTOBER 27, 2021
WORKSHOP
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in a workshop with the
following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Phil McCroan,
and Commissioners David C. Rich, Ward McDaniel, and Patrick Ferrell.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Deputy Clerk Leanna Roberts,
County Administrator Michael L. Hammond, County Assistant Administrator Clay
Smallwood, III, Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Emergency Management
Director/Fire Coordinator Matt Herring, Grant Administrator Kaci Rhodes, Office of
Management & Budget Director Sherry Herring, Mosquito Control Director Austin Horton,
Public Works Director Mark Cothran, and Assistant Public Works Director Raymond Hart.
Chairman Quinn called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m., E.T.
COUNTY DRAINAGE ISSUES
County Administrator Hammond stated that this workshop is a follow up to a meeting held
a few weeks ago with Deseret Ranches, Northwest Florida Water Management District
and D.E.P. (Florida Department of Environmental Protection), reporting there were
discussions for a long-term goal of getting Wetappo Creek cleaned out and the drainage
issues at Pleasant Rest area and behind St. Joe Beach/Gulf Aire. He discussed Deseret
Ranches’ plans to develop a cattle farm on property that was once timberlands, the loss
of millions of trees due to Hurricane Michael that soaked up the stormwater, and the high
amount of rainfall this year. County Administrator Hammond discussed his concerns
regarding U.S. Highway 22 being under water five times this year, stating the County
needs a long-term solution. Assistant County Administrator Smallwood discussed the
work Public Works completed in the Pleasant Rest area, stating the County has done all
they can for this area within their capability and authority. He discussed options for funding
from D.E.M., the possibility of submitting a regional plan for stormwater, and short-term
solutions to divert stormwater runoff coming from Deseret Ranches’ property. Paul Thorb,
of NWFWMD appeared before the Board to discuss the conditions of Gulf County and
working with the County and other agencies to find solutions. He stated there are funding
sources available to help with stormwater assessment and planning. Mr. Thorb suggested
that the County reach out to Apalachee Regional Planning Council (A.R.P.C.) for funding
services. Commissioner Farrell inquired whether NWFWMD would assist in the
assessment, Mr. Thorb stated that the assessment is conducted by the local government
but will work with Gulf County to find funding to accomplish the assessment. Discussion
followed regarding funding and an assessment plan. County Administrator Hammond
stated that the Board will need to decide what areas within the County to do the study. A
D.E.P. representative appearing by phone stated that they are here to support and listen
to see where the problem is and happy to assist. Michael Archibald, of Deseret Cattle and

Timber appeared before the Board to thank the Board for inviting them to be a part of the
discussion, stating that water comes down from Calhoun County and Gulf County
becomes a sponge. He discussed the various swamps within the County and is willing to
work with the County regarding this matter. Public Works Director Cothran discussed a
short-term solution on Pleasant Rest Road and the joint venture with Deseret Ranches
on Road CC 20. County Administrator Hammond discussed getting the party in to discuss
the scope of work, requesting feed-back from the Board on what areas that need a study.
Commissioner McDaniel stated that he will bring this matter before the A.R.P.C.,
discussing his concerns regarding the drainage issues within the County. Commissioner
McCroan discussed contacting D.E.P. regarding the major ditch that runs to the Buffer
Preserve behind Jones Homestead, reporting the County does not have access to this
ditch, and it is creating problems in Oak Grove and several other areas.
There being no further discussion, and upon motion by Commissioner McDaniel, second
by Commissioner McCroan, and unanimous vote, the workshop did then adjourn at 4:25
p.m., E.T.

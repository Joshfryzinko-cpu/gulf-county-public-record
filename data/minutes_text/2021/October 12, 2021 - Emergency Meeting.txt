LOCAL STATE OF EMERGENCY – RED TIDE FISH KILL
County Administrator Hammond discussed reaching out to FWC on how Gulf County can
be proactive and prevent dead fish on the beaches; noting he received no answer. He
stated that he directed the County Attorney to draft a proposed resolution and a Local
State of Emergency to be proactive; requesting support of the Board once the County
Attorney reads it aloud for their consideration. County Attorney Novak presented a
proposed Local State of Emergency <copy provided to the Clerk for the record> for the
October 2021 Red Tide Event, reading the highpoints for the Board to consider.
Commissioner Farrell inquired if there were any alternatives from FWC. County
Administrator Hammond stated that FWC’s goal was to pay for it after the fact and help
Gulf County obtain funds from D.E.P. to clean up the beaches. He reported that Gulf
County wants to be proactive and FWC does not have a proactive solution at this time.
County Attorney Novak discussed the Board passing a similar Declaration and Resolution
in 2016; stating the difference from 2016 to now is that there is a worldwide pandemic,
people with respiratory problems throughout Gulf County, public complaints coughing
from Red Tide with combating COVID-19, public getting flu shoots going into the fall
season, and one of the largest tourist attractions weekends in Gulf County for T.D.C. After
discussion, County Administrator Hammond recommended the Board pass this Local
State of Emergency. Commissioner McCroan motioned to approve the Local State of
Emergency. Commissioner McDaniel seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for
public comment. Eugene Raffield, of Raffield’s Fisheries appeared before the Board to
thank the Board for holding an emergency meeting to address the Red Tide in Gulf

County; stating Gulf County needs a pilot program for Red Tide. He stated that fisheries
can collect the distressed bait fish before reaching the shore and sell them for bait, further
discussing his concerns regarding the combination of COVID-19 and Red Tide. Mr.
Raffield discussed the net ban (Constitutional Amendment 93) and the use of bigger nets
for a governmental purpose, stating the State needs to put it in writing telling us what is
meant by governmental purpose of nets. County Administrator Hammond stated that he
just received a response from FWC, reading aloud their response. Following discussion,
Attorney Pat Floyd appeared before the Board on behalf of Mark and Clint Moore, the
fishermen, and the public to discuss the need for a plan that would give County
governments the authority, ability, and duty to do those things, those governmental
purposes, which is the protection and providing for the health, safety, and welfare of its
property and its citizens. He reported the local fleet of fisherman are ready to help the
Board with the Red Tide, requesting the Board to declare this a governmental purpose
and emergency. Chairman Quinn called for additional public comment. There being none,
the motion passed unanimously.
County Attorney Novak then presented a proposed resolution <copy provided to the Clerk
for the record>, reading it aloud for the Board to consider. Commissioner McDaniel called
for a clarification of the 70 (seventy) feet. Eugene Raffield, of Raffield’s Fisheries
appeared before the Board to state that years ago when that was written Raffield’s
Fisheries had the largest boats and they didn’t have anything that exceeded 70 (seventy)
feet. County Attorney Novak stated that Gulf County is asking for a commonsense
approach, just like the scallop pilot program and the oyster harvest. He stated that this
proposed resolution is tailor-made for Gulf County. Following discussion, County
Administrator Hammond stated each fisherman that is going to participate will need to
register and receive a letter from the County. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McCroan,
County Attorney Novak reported that the fishermen are licensed through the State, but
when the County implements this Home Rule, this proposed resolution will hopefully give
the fishermen some protection. Chairman Quinn stated there is a proposed resolution on
the floor read by County Attorney Novak, calling for a motion to move forward.
Commissioner McCroan so moved. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There was no public comment. County
Administrator Hammond requested to add, that any authorized fisherman under this order
would need a letter from the County qualifying them to participate in this program. County
Attorney Novak stated that under the proposed resolution the County Administrator has
the authority to implement, therefore there is no need to include the language to the
proposed resolution. Chairman Quinn inquired if Commissioner McCroan had any
objections to adding it. Commissioner McCroan had no objection. Attorney Floyd
appeared before the Board to state that the fishermen want to make sure they are doing
this under the authority of a governmental purpose of Gulf County from the Board of
County Commissioners and that the Board will be sharing this information with FWC.
County Administrator Hammond stated that the teeth in this is that it is an emergency
order by the Board under Home Rule for emergency purpose. He reported that this
emergency order is only valid for seven days, unless extended. Chairman Quinn called
for additional public comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously, as
follows:

ADMINISTRATIVE REPORT & UPDATE
County Administrator Hammond reported that the County has a generator down and
needs to be repaired, not to exceed $36,000.00; noting his spending authority.
Administrator Hammond advised the Board that a new generator would cost around
$250,000.00. He stated he needs permission from the Board to repair the generator
located inside the County Courthouse, under an emergency. Chairman Quinn called for
a motion to move forward in getting it fixed. Commissioner Rich so moved to fix the
generator. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously.
County Administrator Hammond discussed holding a workshop with the Constitutional
Officers or have more education at the front door (of the Gulf County Courthouse); stating
that he receives calls daily with complaints about masks in the Courthouse. He stated
that the Board’s policy currently is the people at the front door cannot require a mask
because there is no mask policy, but individual Constitutional Officers can require them
in their offices. Chairman Quinn discussed reaching out to the Constitutional Officers and
sit down with them and see how they feel about it before the Board makes a decision.
County Administrator Hammond stated that he will send them all a letter inviting them to
attend the next regular meeting to discuss it. Chairman Quinn had no objections.
County Administrator Hammond reported that Scared Heart’s (Sacred Heart Health
System) parent company, Ascension, is requiring vaccine mandates or their people will
be terminated or threatened to terminate. He stated that the County is scheduled to give
them $2.5 million this year, discussing the option of asking for a letter to explain this
action. County Administrator Hammond stated that the County has a shortage of nurses
and workers, discussing the Biden administration and his concerns regarding residents
being fired when the hospital received a subsidy of $2.5 million from the Gulf County
taxpayer. Commissioners McDaniel and McCroan discussed their concerns regarding

this matter. County Administrator Hammond stated that he will write a letter to their
Director and see if he will come to the next regular meeting to discuss it.
Grant Director Rhodes requested Board approval to submit a letter to E.D.A. committing
the County for matching funds for the floating dry dock project, and authorizing the
Chairman to sign, contingent upon Administration and County Attorney’s review.
Commissioner McDaniel so moved. Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment, the motion
passed unanimously.

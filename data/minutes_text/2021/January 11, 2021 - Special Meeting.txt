JANUARY 11, 2021
SPECIAL MEETING
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in special session with
the following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Phil
McCroan, and Commissioners David C. Rich, Ward McDaniel, and Patrick Ferrell.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Clerk Rebecca L. Norris, Clerk
Director of Finance & Management Rhonda Woodward, Deputy Clerk Leanna Roberts,
County Administrator Michael L. Hammond, Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Code
Enforcement Officer Trevor Seay, Gulf County E.D.C. Director Jim McKnight, Emergency
Management Director/Fire Coordinator Matt Herring, E9-1-1 Coordinator Ben Guthrie,
County Engineer Clay Smallwood, III, Grant Administrator Kaci Rhodes, Human
Resource Director Brett Lowry, Office of Management & Budget Director Sherry Herring,
Mosquito Control Director Austin Horton, Assistant Public Works Director Raymond Hart,
Sheriff Mike Harrison, and Veterans' Service Office Director & S.H.I.P. Director Joe Paul.
Sheriff Harrison called the meeting to order at 4:30 p.m., E.T.
Chairman Quinn opened the meeting with prayer, and led in the Pledge of Allegiance to
the Flag.

H.H.R.P.
County Administrator Hammond presented the Procedures and Plan for the Program
Parameters for the $1.820 million (H.H.R.P. funding); requesting direction but offering the
following recommendations: 1) do not handle this program (H.H.R.P.) in-house anymore
with the County doing the lottery and drawing, then turning it over to an administrator to
run it; 2) solicit request for qualifications (RFQ’s/sealed bids) to select someone to
administrator this program going forward; 3) District III and District V to get the moderate
money (disproportionately underfunded), with staff needing direction on whether the
Board wants to split the balance by District or put it all in one pot and; 4) in addition to
what is on the presented Procedure and Plan, all non-funded applicants in the original
rounds will have to reapply and recertify going forward. Upon inquiry by Commissioner
McDaniel regarding the amount of qualified applicants remaining on the original list,
S.H.I.P. Director Paul reported he does not have a number, but currently there is a need
of $33,839.00 to cap out the very-low income and $349.00 for low income category which
is still short of the thirty percent (30%) set-a-side; taking the funds from the moderate
income. Following discussion, County Administrator Hammond suggested that when the
County finishes out the low, very low and disabled (income categories) percentages, and
if any moderate is leftover, then it be transferred to the new pot. He further recommended
that the Board open it to applicants Countywide, if the money is not met in Districts III and
V. Commissioner McCroan had no objection. Following further discussion regarding the
low income applications, County Administrator Hammond asked for a motion of the Board
to allow staff to take one applicant from each of Districts I, II and IV, and then hold a
drawing to determine the winner. He stated that staff will update the Board at the next
meeting if the money is received ($1.820 million Round 2 Funding) and start the
advertisement to receive sealed bids (RFQ’s). County Administrator Hammond also
stated that part of the recommendation is when Gulf County receives the (new) money,
then staff will advertise (for sealed bids/RFQs). Commissioner McCroan discussed
drawing the other two names just in case the first drawn applicant does not qualify, then
the second name will be awarded the funding (old money * Round 1 Funding). The Board
had no objection and County Administrator Hammond agreed. Chairman Quinn asked if
this recommendation needs to be included in the motion. County Administrator Hammond
stated yes, together with his list of recommendations, but requested clarification on how
the Board wants to do the low and very low (income categories) in Districts I, II and IV
going forward (splitting evenly (1/3 each), or put all names in a Countywide pot.
Commissioner Rich suggested to start it out evenly and see how it ends up. Chairman
Quinn stated that if it can’t be spent in one place, then the Board can move it to another
District. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to include: 1) drawing from the old money,
three (3) low income applicants; noting if the first (1st) drawn applicant doesn’t quality,
they’ll go to the next name drawn; 2) go out for RFQ’s (sealed bids) for the administration
of the new H.H.R.P. money; 3) accept the new Plan recommendations as presented by
County Administrator Hammond and S.H.I.P. Director Paul and; 4) split the new money
for the low and very-low (income categories). Commissioner McDaniel made the motion.
Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public

comment and emails. County Administrator Hammond called for clarification that the
motion would include that all the non-funded applicants, after the County does this latest
drawing, will have to reapply. Chairman Quinn and Commissioner McDaniel had no
objection; agreeing applicants will have to recertify. There being no public comment, the
motion passed unanimously.
CARES ACT BUSINESS PLAN
County Administrator Hammond reported that the U.S. President (Trump) has signed the
Bill for the extension of the CARES Act money, but the State has not caught up with the
new plan; stating that all this will be contingent upon the County getting the State’s
blessing to assure Gulf County will receive reimbursement. He recommended the Board
extend the deadline for the business plan applications until January 31, 2021 (must be
received by January 31, 2021) and that the Board request, if the County can get
reimbursement from the State for these leased employees, to include non-direct
employers leased employees as long as they met all the other qualifications. Chairman
Quinn called for a motion to move forward with the recommendation. Commissioner
Farrell made the motion. Commissioner Rich seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn
called for public comment and emails. There being none, the motion passed unanimously.

E.D.C. Director McKnight appeared before the Board to provide a status update regarding
economic development; stating that he is working on three (3) major proposals along the
Canal (Intercostal Waterway). He reported that Skyborne received a name change to
UAB.
Chris Gouras, of Gouras Associates appeared before the Board to discuss the five (5)
C.D.B.G. Disaster Recovery project applications submitted to D.E.O. (Department of
Economic Opportunity) for approval; stating that the next round of funding is the
Downtown Revitalization Program, in the amount of $60 million with the application
deadline of February 16, 2021. He reported that D.E.O. inquired about whether Gulf
County needed an application submission extension; stating that he asked for a sixty (60)
day extension. Mr. Gouras requested Board approval to allow the Chairman or the County
Administrator to submit a letter requesting this extension as well. Discussion followed
regarding funding and possible projects. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to apply;
sending a letter for a sixty (60) day extension. Commissioner McCroan so moved.
Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment
and emails. There being none, the motion passed unanimously. Commissioner McDaniel
discussed his concerns regarding the time invested and the need for these projects to get
going. County Administrator Hammond stated that in the event Gulf County is not granted
the extension, the Board needs to be prepared to make decisions moving forward by the
next regular meeting. He stated that it will be very helpful if the County could get the
extension so that if projects from the first round are not funded, they could be added to
the second round of funding. Mr. Gouras reported that if the extension is not granted, then
they will be working toward scheduling the required public hearing, in which they’ll take
citizen input, either by email postings, like last time, or hold a hearing.
County Administrator Hammond reported that they have started (Legislative) committee
hearings and it will be a horrible budget year in Tallahassee due to COVID-19; stating
that Gulf County still needs the waiver of match (F.E.M.A. * Hurricane Michael). He stated
that the biggest part Gulf County has hanging is the dune restoration project with a $1
million match. County Administrator Hammond reported the County’s overall match will
be out $2 million, but the County has paid and is not in the hole at all. He encouraged the
Board to keep the heat on should they see or speak to their State Representatives.
County Administrator Hammond stated that he needs to meet with each member of the
Board individually before the next meeting to come up with a recommendation, due to the
political changes in Washington DC, to change the County’s Lobbyists’ scope of work;
reporting there is no need to continue C.B.R.A. (Coastal Barrier Resources Act) with a
democrat President and administration.

Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn of whether staff had sent Representative (Jason) Shoaf
a letter (F.E.M.A. match waiver), County Administrator Hammond stated he is not sure if
a letter has been submitted, but noted Representative Shoaf is aware. He stated that the
Governor (DeSantis) alone with make this decision.
County Administrator Hammond requested approval for funds to the Property Appraiser

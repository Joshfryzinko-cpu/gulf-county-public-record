ADMINISTRATIVE REPORT & UPDATE
County Administrator Hammond requested the Board to review page 23 in the
Administration’s packet <provided to the Clerk for the record>. E.D.C. Director McKnight
appeared before the Board to discuss the recently adopted Resolution by the Gulf County
Health Trust Board (on page 23). He requested permission from the Board to use some
of the half cent sales tax, in the amount of $100,000.00 for five (5) years to support a
kidney care and dialysis facility. Following discussion, Commissioner McDaniel stated
that the County needs to put pressure on Franklin County to support this venture, as
Franklin County residents will benefit from this facility as well. E.D.C. Director McKnight
reported this subject is on Franklin County Commissioners’ agenda for December 21,
2021. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McDaniel regarding how long it will take for the
facility to be up and running, E.D.C. Director McKnight stated he has not had that
conversation, but Fresenius has already reached out to hire someone to be the manager.
Chairman Quinn discussed the need for a dialysis facility in Gulf County, thanking E.D.C.
Director McKnight for his work on this project. County Attorney Novak then read a
proposed resolution by title for the Board to consider, discussing highlighted areas within
the proposed resolutions. County Administrator Hammond reported that Fresenius will be
more than what Gulf County had before, stating there will be a kidney disease doctor and
a network trying to keep patients off dialysis, in addition to caring for patients that are on
dialysis. He requested the Board pass this proposed resolution and authorize the official
request to Franklin County Commission to commit ¼ of the cost. County Attorney Novak

discussed the need for a motion to approve the proposed resolution and directing
Administration to move forward and work with Franklin County in a cooperative effort to
request funds from them. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to move forward.
Commissioner McCroan so moved. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment, the motion
passed unanimously, as follows:

From pages 20-21 of the packet, OMB Director Herring discussed the H.H.R.P. Round 1
status update, requesting Board to approve that all applicants who have applied for both
rounds and are offered Round 1 funding must take these funds. If the applicant refuses,
then the applicant will be removed from both Round 1 and Round 2 as ineligible for that
refusal. She stated that Round 2 does have a higher dollar amount; however, until Round
1 is spent, the County cannot spend a dollar of Round 2 Funding. OMB Director Herring
reported that this will include purchase assistance and rehabs. She stated her second
request within this is that the County require all applicants who applied for Round 2 but
are moved to Round 1 for purchase assistance be required to take Round 1 Funding once
offered. If the applicant refuses Round 1 Funding, then they will be removed from both
rounds (Round 1 and Round 2) of funding and marked as ineligible. OMB Director Herring
stated that the last part of her request is in District IV, one applicant, Sherry Bolden,
requesting to move her from the low-income category to moderate in Round 1. She stated
this is three requests in one. County Administrator Hammond discussed his concerns
regarding the State requirements for Gulf County, stating that the rates are so low that
applicants basically have to be in poverty to qualify. He stated the County is having a hard
time closing this out (Round 1 Funding), suggesting the Board may want to raise the

number to keep applicants from changing Rounds. OMB Director Herring reported if the
Board changes the numbers to match Round 2, applicants will be kicked out of funding.
Following discussion, Commissioner McCroan recommended that if an applicant was
awarded in Round 2 and was moved to Round 1, then the applicant should be awarded
the same amount of funding as awarded in Round 2. Upon inquiry by Commissioner
McDaniel OMB Director Herring stated that changing or increasing the low category
numbers would satisfy some of the push-back received and would be okay, funding wise,
even on the purchase assistance with low (income category). She stated that if the Board
changes moderates, not all applicants will be funded. County Administrator Hammond
discussed changing the low-income applicants to the same funding amount across the
board, dropping the mandatory part for the moderate applicants. After discussion, OMB
Director Herring requested clarification, for those applicants that were originally in Round
1, asking if the Board is increasing their amounts also in low-income category. The Board
had no objection. OMB Director Herring stated that staff will change the amounts for
Round 1 low-income category to match those of Round 2 Funding and moderate, if there
is a space in Round 1, staff will offer to applicant in Round 2 but is not required to take
the lesser amount but will have to wait until Round 2 Funding spending is open.
Commissioner McCroan clarified that no applicant will be required to take a reduce
amount. County Administrator Hammond that moderate income could be moved to Round
1 with lesser funding amount, but this is voluntary. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Rich,
County Administrator Hammond reported that the Round 1 Funding deadline for spending
is June 30, 2022. Following additional discussion, Chairman Quinn called for a motion to
raise the low (income category) number to $40,000.00. Commissioner McCroan so
moved. County Administrator Hammond stated for clarity, $40,000.00 on rehab and
$60,000.00 on purchase assistance. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to raise the
purchase assistance on the low to $40,000.00 and on rehab to $60,000.00. OMB Director
Herring stated that $60,00.00 is for purchase assistance and $40,00.00 is for rehab.
County Administrator Hammond state that on the moderate, there is not a forced deal,
but they could voluntarily take the lesser amount for Round 1 funding. OMB Director
Herring stated that her third request was moving Sherry Bolden. Chairman Quinn stated
the Board will move Ms. Bolden from low to moderate if the motion passes. Chairman
Quinn called for a motion move forward with those recommendations. Commissioner
McCroan stated he will include all of that in his motion. Commissioner Rich seconded the
motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment, the
motion passed unanimously.

County Administrator Hammond discussed the County getting out of the garbage
business and going to a private company for collection, hauling, and disposal; reporting
the City of Port St. Joe elected to remain in the garbage business, as the City had their
own trucks, and recycling program. He stated the City of Port St. Joe, and the County
jointly built that Transfer Station on Industrial Road and the County received grant funds
to build the Recycling Center adjacent to the Transfer Station. County Administrator
Hammond reported when the County got out of the garbage business, the County agreed
to allow the City of Port St. Joe to have the Transfer Station, paying the County
$75,000.00 and a fire truck and the County transferred the property to the City of Port St.
Joe, but the County retained the equity above $150,000.00. He stated the County gave
the City of Port St. Joe a long-term lease/use of the recycle building, which was not
recorded in the Official Records. County Administrator Hammond stated as time passed,
the City of Port St. Joe got out of the garbage business and this building was leased for
a long time to Waste Pro, but technically this building is still the County’s. He proposed,
if the City of Port St. Joe and the County wanted to split the rental of that building going
forward, then staff will put it on the commercial market for leasing. Commissioner Farrell
made a motion to move forward on talking to the City of Port St. Joe to see if some income

GULF COUNTY, FLORIDA RESOLUTION 2021-16 IN RESPONSE TO CITY OF PORT
ST. JOE ORDINANCE 584
County Attorney Novak discussed Gulf County Resolution No. 2021-16; presenting a
copy of City Ordinance 584 for the Board members to review <copy not provided to the
Clerk for the record>. He discussed the timeline regarding City Ordinance 584; stating
that today’s meeting is to discuss the dispute and ideally find some resolve before it goes
under Judicial evaluation. Chairman Quinn opened the meeting to City Mayor Buzzett.
City Mayor Buzzett stating that the City Board is here today to try and negotiate something
in good faith with Gulf County; reporting the City Board is open to the concern the County
had regarding density. Following discussion regarding density, Chairman Quinn opened
the floor to the City Commissioners. City Commissioner Ashbrook discussed capping in
the 200 range; suggesting a 50-unit apartment complex and have individual lots for
homeowners that will quality for programs and the City will give them the land. Discussion
followed regarding agreeing on 172, affordable housing, and waiver of impact fees. City
Mayor Buzzett stated the City of Port St. Joe needs to get through the process with D.E.O.
(Department of Economic Opportunity); reporting the final reading and adoption is
scheduled for Tuesday. He stated he does not have a problem signing anything that says
after the City gets through the process for this piece of property, reporting the City of Port
St. Joe will change or create a PUD to set the maximum at 172 for this piece of property.
City Commissioners Hoffman and Langston stated they would support 172; reporting that
he would like to see more of townhouses and individual units. Upon inquiry by

Commissioner Farrell, City Mayor Buzzett stated that it looks like it will be a cap at 172.
County Administrator Hammond reported that how the County got to the 172 is by taking
the City’s density of 7 units per acre on the uplands and agreed on 20, which is 140, and
taking the County density on wetlands that would give another 32. He stated the MOU
(Memorandum of Understanding) would be for that annexed property and would be 172.
County Administrator Hammond stated that he does not think the County needs to be a
part of the PUD, with just the City of Port St. Joe agreeing to 172 on the whole thing (piece
of property) and then the County is out of the mix if that is doable.
City Commissioner Ashbrook reported that a citizen attended the last City Commission
meeting presented concerns regarding County owned property that was given for the
purpose of affordable housing and is being treated as private enterprise, digging sand
pits; asking for clarification regarding the parcel behind Honeyville Park. County
Administrator Hammond reported that St. Joe Company gave this piece of property to the
County and at some juncture the County will do some potential affordable housing on this
site. He reported that the County did purchase some acres around this area and not all is
set aside for affordable housing. After discussion by City Commissioner Ashbrook,
County Administrator Hammond reported that the County has permission from St. Joe
Company to excavate the sand and it is being placed at the Cape (San Blas).
City Mayor Buzzett discussed drafting a Memorandum of Understanding regarding the
172; inquiring if the County will drop the challenge to the annexation. Chairman Quinn
and Commissioner McCroan said yes. County Attorney Novak stated that is accurate,
that the sole purpose of today’s meeting was to try and resolve that dispute. He stated
that if it is the will of both the County and the City of Port St. Joe, then the County will
draft a Memorandum of Understanding for execution.
GULF COUNTY WATER SERVICE DISTRICT AREA ORDINANCE
Assistant County Administrator Smallwood discussed getting into the water business
(purchase Lighthouse Utilities/LUCI), creating a capital improvement project list for this
system, and reaching out to various funding sources to complete this project. He
discussed the need to find new water sources for projected demand within the next five
(5) years. Assistant County Administrator Smallwood reported that the County has
property in White City and the plan is to put a new plant in White City to supply the south
end (of the County); stating there are advantage to this: 1. off the coastline, getting away
from saltwater intrusion problems; 2. out of the storm surge zone; and 3. getting out of
the 100-year flood plain. He stated when the County got into the water business, it was
known that a source had to be established, it was not to get into the water business to
bankrupt the City (of Port St. Joe) or cause financial hardship, or cause City (of Port St.
Joe) workers to lose any jobs. Assistant County Administrator Smallwood did say the
County is committed to providing quality water at a reasonable rate and discussed the
willingness to help the City of Port St. Joe also. City Mayor Buzzett discussed talking to
City Public Works, City Water Department and City Manger to run, with assistant from the
County, a larger trunk line from the water plant to Jones Homestead, providing all the
pressure the County needs. He discussed looking at the rate structure; stating the City
has very good water, passing all the quality tests required; reporting the City can provide

plenty of pressure for the County’s water usage. City Commissioner Hoffman discussed
the City’s rates; stating the only way the City of Port St. Joe can hold the line is with future
customers; therefore, needing assurance from the County regarding the details in this
plan. Discussion followed regarding water rates, water quality, and service areas.
Chairman Quinn stated the County does not want to put the City of Port St. Joe out of
business nor City employees to lose their jobs; asking how the County can help assist the
City (of Port St. Joe) to get the rate debt down. County Commissioner McCroan stated
that none of the City Commissioners present were on the Board when the water system
change; discussing helping the City (of Port St. Joe) get back on the aquifer. Discussion
followed regarding quality water, testing results, and working together. City Mayor Buzzett
stated the County’s proposed ordinance needs to define the City of Port St. Joe’s service
area. Chairman Quinn inquired about how the County can help the City of Port St. Joe to
get the debt paid down and what the plan is going forward to get the water rates down.
City Manager Anderson stated the rates have leveled out; reporting that getting more
customers online will reduce the rates. He discussed using Pot (Triumph-RESTORE Act)
money to get the customers from the Beaches online, noting that placing a booster plant
at Jones Homestead to tie back into the water plant is another option for a rate reduction,
and the requirements for testing in using the aquifer. Commissioner McDaniel stated the
City (of Port St. Joe) is running treated water that is seeping (seepage) from the ground,
not from the Chipola pump station; inquiring about how the City is doing to help itself and
what departments are being run/paid out of the water fees. Following discussion, City
Attorney McCahill requested that the County define the service areas within the County’s
proposed ordinance. After discussion, County Administrator Hammond reported that the
water is eating residents’ pipes under their house and eating pipes that the City (of Port
St. Joe) are having to replace, stating the people do not like the water, not the taste, the
smell, or the color. He stated that if we do nothing, in five (5) years from now, those water
bills will be 15% higher or more and will continue to go up. County Administrator
Hammond also stated that when the Cape rates are half what they are in White City, with
a water plant producing aquifer water, there is no way that the County can take the heat
from residents wanting aquifer water that is half the price. He discussed the cost of the
City of Port St. Joe; stating the only way the County sees lowing the rates is to get back
on the aquifer to lower the cost to treat the water. County Attorney Novak discussed the
advertisement of the proposed ordinance and read specific sections within the proposed
ordinance regarding the City’s water service areas. He stated that the proposed ordinance
references a Memorandum of Understating that was recorded in the Official Records that
identities the City’s water service area, discussing this recorded Memorandum of
Understanding. After discussion by City Attorney McCahill, County Attorney Novak stated
that he could attach the Memorandum of Understanding to the back of the proposed
ordinance as an exhibit. City Mayor Buzzett thanked County Attorney Novak for the
explanation; stating with what County Attorney Novak said, there is no need to attach the
Memorandum of Understanding as an exhibit to the proposed ordinance. After inquiry by
City Mayor Buzzett, City Attorney McCahill requested that the Memorandum of
Understanding be attached as an exhibit to the proposed ordinance. City Commissioner
Hoffman discussed his concerns regarding the service area, the aquifer, and the
Memorandum of Understanding; requesting the County provide a detailed service area in

the proposed ordinance. Discussion followed regarding the water rates, water service
area, the Memorandum of Understanding, and the City’s mileage rate.
Chairman Quinn asked if the City of Port St. Joe would be willing to sit down and have
discussion regarding future annexations. City Mayor Buzzett stated that he has no issue
sitting down and talking about any topic. He thanked the County for holding this meeting;
requesting the City and County meeting again to work this matter out. City Commissioners
Ashbrook and Brett discussed doing a study to see what it would take for the City to get
back on the aquifer; looking at finding ways to reduce the water rates for customers.
There being no further discussion, Chairman Quinn adjourned the meeting at 10:35 a.m.,

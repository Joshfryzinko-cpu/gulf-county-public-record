SEPTEMBER 28, 2021
REGULAR MEETING
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in regular session with
the following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Phil
McCroan, and Commissioners David C. Rich, Ward McDaniel, and Patrick Ferrell.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Clerk Rebecca L. Norris, Director
of Finance & Management Director Amy Ludlam, Deputy Clerk Leanna Roberts, County
Administrator Michael L. Hammond, County Assistant Administrator Clay Smallwood, III,
Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Building Official Brad Bailey, Gulf County E.D.C.
Director Jim McKnight, Emergency Management Director/Fire Coordinator Matt Herring,
Gulf County E.M.S. Director Jody Daniels, Grant Administrator Kaci Rhodes, Office of
Management & Budget Director Sherry Herring, Mosquito Control Director Austin Horton,
County Planner Lee Collinsworth, Assistant Public Works Director Raymond Hart, and
Sheriff Mike Harrison.
Sheriff Harrison called the meeting to order at 9:00 a.m., E.T.
Chairman Quinn opened the meeting with prayer and led in the Pledge of Allegiance to
the Flag.
CONSENT AGENDA
Chairman Quinn called for public comment regarding the Consent Agenda. Eugene
Behage, member of the Salinas Park Committee and Vice President of Coastal
Community Association appeared before the Board to discuss concerns regarding the
County’s limited funds that will be allocated to refurbish Salinas Park, asking what is in
the agreement. He stated that the Coastal Community Association conducted a survey;
reporting that tennis and basketball courts were the least important <copy of survey was
provided to the Clerk for the record> according to the survey. Mr. Behage discussed other
courts available to the public in nearby areas, requesting that funds be exclusively for
trees, shrubs, and vegetation planting. Commissioner McCroan reported that F.R.D.A.P.
grant funds cannot be used for plants or shrubs, stating that the basketball and tennis
courts rated higher than the pickle ball court for this grant. Upon inquiry by Mr. Behage
regarding a parking lot for Fire Department, Commissioner McCroan stated that the
F.R.D.A.P. grant is strictly for recreation. Grant Director Rhodes stated that the parking
lot will be included in this grant application, considered as a support facility and it is ranked
high. She stated that this application will consist of parking, tennis, and basketball courts.
Patrick Foy, of Indian Pass appeared before the Board to ask if a dog park could be
included, requesting this be considered at Salians Park. Chairman Quinn called for a
motion to extend Mr. Foy’s time to speak. Commissioner McCroan so moved.
Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion, and it passed unanimously. Mr. Foy reported

that a dog park was ranked high on the survey; noting he understands grant requirements.
Chairman Quinn called for additional public comment regarding the Consent Agenda.
There was no further public comment. Mr. Foy appeared back before the Board to inquire
if this grant would allow for a dog park. Grant Director Rhodes stated that the dog park is
not one of the elements that ranks for this F.R.D.A.P. grant. Clerk Norris reported that
staff sent out an amended Consent Agenda; noting that there were a few items that
needed to be corrected which was item 11 that had a typographical error, noting this
should have been S.C.R.A.P. grant not S.C.O.P., and item 7, the completion date needed
to be removed. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to accept the Consent Agenda with
those changes that the Clerk mentioned. Commissioner McDaniel made the motion.
Commissioner Rich seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment.
There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously, as follows:

MEETING – STORMWATER DRAINAGE
Assistant County Administrator Smallwood discussed a meeting held with Deseret
Ranches and Northwest Florida Water Management District that addressed the drainage
issues since Hurricane Michael; stating they are working together to create a plan to
correct it. He stated the County is asking NWFWMD to assist with a plan, remove the
storm debris from Wetappo Creek, and hold Deseret Ranches accountable for what they
are doing. He stated that Deseret Ranches is a good partner; reporting that Deseret
Ranches purchased the pipe for Road 20 and Public Works will install it. Assistant County
Administrator Smallwood stated they plan to hold a workshop in October to present a plan
to the Board to correct this matter.

RESOLUTION – VACCINE MANDATE
County Administrator Hammond discussed the (U.S. President) Biden Vaccine Mandate;
reporting that Gulf County falls under the category of over 100 employees. He stated that
unless the Board directs him otherwise, he will not require Gulf County employees to be
vaccinated. After discussion, County Administrator Hammond requested support of the
Board by resolution opposing mandates. Chairman Quinn discussed his concerns
regarding this matter, stating that it should be a personal choice. County Administrator
Hammond stated that he will be violating an Executive Order; requesting the Board to
support his decision not to enforce it. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Farrell, County
Administrator Hammond stated that the government program giving an employee 80
hours, has expired and the County is no longer giving leave to individuals that get COVID,
but the employee can use their personal leave time. Following discussion by members of
the Board, Commissioner Rich made a motion to support County Administrator Hammond
in a Resolution to make it not mandatory and oppose the Federal mandate. Commissioner
McCroan seconded the motion. After discussion by Chairman Quinn, he then called for
public comment. There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously.

LEGISLATIVE REQUEST – LOCAL BILL T.D.C. FUND
County Administrator Hammond stated that now would be the time, if the Board desires,
to submit a Legislative request regarding a local bill on the T.D.C. public safety funding.
Chairman Quinn called for a recommendation. County Administrator Hammond
recommended the Board ask the County’s Legislative Delegation, Representative Shoaf
and Senator Ausley, to push a bill similar to the Counties West of Gulf County, with
different limits, that would allow a percentage to be used for public safety, law
enforcement, E.M.S. and fire. Discussion followed regarding the pennies and spending
requirements. Sheriff Harrison appeared before the Board to thank members of the Board
for their support; stating the Sheriff’s Office can take care of the locals under the current

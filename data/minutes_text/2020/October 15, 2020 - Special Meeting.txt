OCTOBER 15, 2020
SPECIAL MEETING
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in special session with
the following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Phil
McCroan, and Commissioners David C. Rich, Ward McDaniel, and Jimmy Rogers.
Others present were: Clerk Rebecca L. Norris, Deputy Clerk Leanna Roberts, County
Administrator Michael L. Hammond, County Assistant Administrator Warren Yeager, Jr.,
Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Code Enforcement Officer Trevor Seay, Emergency
Management/Fire Coordinator Matt Herring, County Engineer Clay Smallwood, III, Grant
Administrator Kaci Rhodes, Office of Management & Budget Director Sherry Herring,
Mosquito Control Director Austin Horton, Public Works Director Mark Cothran, and Sheriff
Mike Harrison.
Sheriff Harrison called the meeting to order at 11:00 a.m., E.T.
Chairman Quinn led in the Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag.
2020 BOND ISSUE
County Administrator Hammond reported that Mark Mustian, with Nabors, Giblin and
Nickerson, PA is attending to present a document for the Board to consider; stating that
staff is pleased with the bond rating (for the purchase of Lighthouse Utilities-LUCI). Mark
Mustian, with Nabors, Giblin and Nickerson, PA appeared before the Board to discuss
the proposed resolution which will delegate to the Chairman the right to sign the bond
purchase agreement. He reported that the projected closing date is two (2) weeks from
today. He discussed highlights within the proposed resolution and the requirements of the
bonds; reporting that parameters within this proposed resolutions authorizes borrowing
up to $4.5 million, with an interest rate not to exceed 2.75%, and the County has to be
able to pay the bond back within seven year date. Commissioner McCroan inquired about
the timeline in being able to drop another well down. County Administrator Hammond
discussed receiving and opening bids (Bid #1920-47 * LUCI I Plant Restoration and Water
System Improvements at Money Bayou Main) on the 27th (October) and even if the Board
awarded it, the contractor would not be mobilized by that time. He stated that he does not
see this as being a foreseeable problem and no work will be completed on this property
until it is in County hands. Discussion followed regarding advertisement to receive sealed
bids. County Administrator Hammond stated that there is a maximum in the proposed
bond resolution of 2.75% but will not know the actual rate until closing. Mr. Mustian
requested the Board adopt the proposed bond resolution; stating that the Clerk has the
final version. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to adopt that resolution with the
changes. Commissioner McCroan so moved. Commissioner McDaniel seconded the

motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment and emails. There being none, the
motion passed unanimously, as follows:

ADMINISTRATIVE REPORT & UPDATE
Upon discussion by County Administrator Hammond, Public Works Director Cothran
appeared before the Board to discuss meeting with FWC (Fish and Wildlife Commission)
regarding the acquisition of the Saul’s Creek Road within a year, but in order for them to
stay on task with a project scheduled for spring of 2021, they are asking if the County
would be interested in entering into a letter of agreement so FWC could start their project
and then at that point start maintaining the road. He discussed making a motion to move
forward with this letter of agreement, contingent upon Chairman review and then County
Attorney Novak and FWC’s attorney will work out the agreement. Chairman Quinn called
for a motion to send that letter. Commissioner McDaniel so moved for the letter.
Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment and emails. There being none, the motion passed unanimously.

Public Works Cothran provided an update regarding the cleanup of Salinas Park. He
discussed the concrete pile that has been collected from the storm (Hurricane Michael)
and the multiple uses and being a benefit to the County once the concrete is crushed up.
Public Works Cothran requested permission to obtain quotes from contractors to have
the concrete crushed; stating that a portion of his road material budget could go towards
this cost. Commissioner McCroan thanked Public Works for the work done at Salinas
Park. Discussion followed regarding the cleanup at Salinas Park and the dead trees.
County Administrator Hammond requested the Board allow him to work with Public Works
regarding the concrete, under his spending authority, without bidding it. He discussed
paying for the crushing fee from the tipping fee that was set aside from the disaster
(Hurricane Michael). After discussion, County Administrator Hammond requested a
motion of the Board to recognize that he can do it under his spending authority, noting
this is disaster material (Hurricane Michael). Commissioner McCroan so moved.
Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment
and emails. There being none, the motion passed unanimously. Chairman Quinn inquired
whether staff needs to contact someone with a log truck to remove the trees from Salinas
Park. County Administrator Hammond stated that staff has it worked out, if the County
cannot find someone to get them for free, then the County has a backup plan.
County Administrator Hammond discussed a grant for removal of derelict vessels for the
Donna Kay and a sailboat, and now a barge. County Engineer Smallwood appeared
before the Board to report that the County has received three (3) letters from FWC (Fish
and Wildlife Commission) for the Donna Kay (shrimp boat), a sailboat that is at Indian
Pass, and now a barge in the Gulf County Canal. He reported that sealed bids (Bid #2021-
02 * 2020 Derelict Vessel Removal) are being advertised and will be opened during the
regular November meeting. He reported that once bids are received, then the County will
apply for the funds from FWC.
County Administrator Hammond reported that the barges showed up on time with the
woodchips; stating that part of their request was for the County to extend that road
(Industrial Road) several hundred feet to they can go directly from the scales at the old
Arizona Chemical site under the underpass to the mill site. He requested Board approval;
stating he does not have a price yet, but County Engineer Smallwood will work on it.
County Administrator Hammond reported that the deal with St. Joe Company has expired,
but will ask if they will be willing to pay for half (50%). He requested Board approval to
move forward with this project, with or without St. Joe Company. Upon inquiry by
Chairman Quinn regarding the need for a motion, County Administrator Hammond stated
there is no need for a motion; reporting that staff will keep it under his bid amount, but if
the Board has no objection, then staff will move forward. Chairman Quinn had no
objection.
County Administrator Hammond discussed the announcement that Washington County,
Mexico Beach, and others are getting F.E.M.A reimbursement; stating that some of that
is mixed with debris removal that the State was already paying for on behalf of Gulf
County. He reported that Gulf County still has out more than $35 million of projects and
the road number is unknown. County Administrator Hammond stated that the County has

not received word on the cost share from the Governor’s Office; stating that the waiver is
worth $2 million (5%).
County Administrator Hammond discussed the confusion with D.E.O. (Department of
Economic Opportunity) regarding the C.D.B.G.D.R. process which requires certification
that it was storm damage (Hurricane Michael) in order to obtain reimbursement, also
requiring that the County did not get any F.E.M.A. or insurance money, which would make
it impossible for County government. He reported that staff has a conference call on the
26th with the new director of D.E.O. to try and get further clarification. County Administrator
Hammond reported that the County has until November 30, 2020 to get a project list
submitted and will be asking the Board to rank them once a list is compiled. Discussion
followed regarding trimming the list to a maximum of five (5) items and prioritizing them.
Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn, County Administrator Hammond discussed the plan,
waivers, and how he felt the money should be split in the damaged areas; noting the
economic revitalization element.
There being no further business and upon motion by Commissioner McDaniel, second by
Commissioner McCroan, and unanimous vote, the meeting did then adjourn at 11:29
a.m., E.T.

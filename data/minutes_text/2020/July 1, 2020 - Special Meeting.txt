PUBLIC HEARING – BUSINESS LICENSE REGULATIONS
Pursuant to advertisement to hold a Public Hearing to consider a proposed ordinance,
County Attorney Novak read the proposed ordinance by title regarding business license
regulations. Upon direction by Chairman Quinn, County Attorney Novak then read the
proposed ordinance in its entirety into the record. County Administrator Hammond stated
that this proposed ordinance only affects the unincorporated areas of Gulf County, not
areas in the City of Port St. Joe or the City of Wewahitchka. He reported that businesses
already licensed by the State will only need to provide their State license and their address
for doing business; stating there will be no required fee. County Administrator Hammond
discussed vacation rentals; requesting to add an administrative re-inspection fee to the
proposed ordinance for rentals that fail the initial inspection. After discussion regarding
the fees, County Administrator Hammond stated that the Building Department will handle
the business licenses and T.D.C. will handle the vacation rentals. Chairman Quinn
opened the Public Hearing for comment from the public; calling for public comment or
emails. Upon Deputy Administrator Summers reporting there was one (1) email, County
Attorney Novak then read an email from Brendan Murphy, of Cape San Blas regarding
his support of the proposed ordinance <copy provided to the Clerk for the record>. After
discussion, Chairman Quinn called for additional comment by email. There being none,
the Chairman then closed comment to the public. Commissioner McCroan motioned to
move forward with the business license regulations (ordinance). Commissioner McDaniel
seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment and emails. There being
none, the motion passed unanimously, as follows:

ORDINANCE NO. 2020-04
AN ORDINANCE OF GULF COUNTY, FLORIDA REQUIRING ANY
PERSON OR ENTITY THAT ENDEAVORS TO CARRY ON, CONDUCT
OR ENGAGE IN ANY BUSINESS TRADE, OCCUPATION OR
PROFESSION WITHIN GULF COUNTY IS REQUIRED TO OBTAIN AN
ANNUALLY RENEWED BUSINESS LICENSE (INCLUSIVE OF
VACATION RENTALS) AND TIMELY FILED AND RENEWED
THEREAFTER; PROVIDING FOR ADMINISTRATIVE OVERSIGHT AND
REGULATORY AUTHORITY; FOR SAID POLICIES AND PROCEDURE
TO BE FURTHER AMENDED, BE CODIFIED AND BECOME PART OF
GULF COUNTY MUNICODE; PROVIDING FOR REPEALER,
SEVERABILITY AND MODIFICATIONS THAT MAY ARISE FROM
CONSIDERATION AT PUBLIC HEARINGS; AND PROVIDING FOR AN
EFFECTIVE DATE.
*Complete Ordinance is on file with Clerk*
(End)
County Attorney Novak stated that section four (4) is where the language is regarding the
DBPR (Department of Business and Professional Regulations-state business license)
exemption for fees is located.

CARES ACT
County Administrator Hammond discussed the CARES Act; requesting the Board redo
the County’s economic stimulus program for businesses. E.D.C. Director McKnight
appeared before the Board to present and discuss the proposed Gulf County CARES Act
business reinvestment plan <copy provided to the Clerk for the record>. County
Administrator Hammond stated that staff is seeking two (2) things today: 1) approval of
the plan, and if approved, staff will then submit the plan for preapproval from D.E.M.
(Department of Emergency Management); and 2) permission to advertise to receive
sealed bids for a RFQ’s (Request for Qualifications) to manage this program. Upon inquiry
by Commissioner McCroan regarding this funding, County Administrator Hammond
reported that the County received an initial 25% allotment (due to COVID-19), but the
75% is on a reimbursement basis. He reported that staff would like to purchase an
ambulance, with the remaining (CARES Act) funds, if the Board is in agreement; stating
he believes this falls under the criteria (to use CARES Act funds for the purchase). County
Administrator Hammond again requested approval from the Board to bless this plan, send
it to D.E.M., advertise for sealed bids for an RFQ to administer it, and ask for an
ambulance from this program. After discussion, Chairman Quinn stated if the Board
approves the (Gulf County CARES Act Business Reinvestment) Plan, then he will call for
a motion to send it to D.E.M. Commissioner Rogers so moved. Commissioner McCroan
seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment or emails. Deputy
Administrator Summers reported there was an email and read it aloud from Vince Bishop,
of Port St. Joe inquiring about the two FTE requirements. Discussion followed regarding
the employee requirement. Chairman Quinn called for any additional emails. There were
none. County Attorney Novak stated that this Plan is to benefit all applicants and the
members of the Board that own businesses; stating for the record this investment plan is
for the entire community, not a special unique benefit that the Board is passing for
themselves, therefore there is not a requirement for any of the members of the Board to
abstain. Chairman Quinn called for additional emails. There being none, the motion to
approve the plan passed unanimously. Chairman Quinn then called for a motion to

advertise to receive sealed bids for RFQ’s to implement this program. Commissioner
McDaniel so moved to go out for RFQs. Commissioner Rich seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment or emails. There being none, the motion then
passed unanimously. Upon Chairman Quinn calling for a motion, County Administrator
Hammond recommended the Board submit a request to D.E.M. to approve to purchase
an ambulance for Gulf County E.M.S. out of the CARES (Act) funds. Commissioner
McCroan made the motion. Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn
called for public comment or emails. There were none. Commissioner Rich inquired if the
ambulance will go on the North end (of the County). County Administrator Hammond
stated the ambulance would go wherever the E.M.S. Director chooses. After members of
the Board discussed the ambulance going to the South end, the motion then passed
unanimously.
SAUL’S CREEK ROAD
County Administrator Hammond reported that the County has been approached by the
State to take over ownership and maintenance of Saul’s Creek Road; stating that the
State owns property on both sides of the road. He also reported that the County has a
public boat ramp at the end of this road and there are a few private property owners, but
the State is going to guarantee to the County that this road will be left open to the public
and to the boat ramp, but will take over the cost maintenance and what-not going forward.
County Administrator Hammond requested permission from the Board to pursue this;
getting the paperwork together and advertise; stating that there will be a reverter (clause)
in the event the State ceases to have it open for the public. He requested the Board bless
the idea, then staff will proceed and bring an agreement back before the Board for
consideration. Commissioner McDaniel made the motion to move forward. Commissioner
Rogers seconded the motion. After discussion by Commissioner McDaniel, Chairman
Quinn called for public comment or emails. There being none, the motion passed
unanimously.
HIGHLAND VIEW BEACH
County Administrator Hammond presented a map of Highland View Beach <copy
provided to the Clerk for the record>; discussing the County and City’s (of Port St. Joe)
portions of this beach. He reported that the Sheriff’s Office is receiving complaints of
public driving on this beach; requesting direction from the Board on whether beach driving
will or will not be allowed and the need to be consistent with the City of Port St. Joe.
Commissioner Rogers inquired about the City of Port St. Joe’s stance on the matter.
County Administrator Hammond stated that he doesn’t think the City has indicated that
they want to close it off, but no communications have been made. Commissioners Rogers
and McCroan discussed their support in keeping this beach open, as long as the City is
in agreement. County Administrator Hammond inquired if the Board wants to add this to
the County’s Ordinance (Beach Driving). Sheriff Harrison appeared before the Board to
report that the Beach Driving Ordinance only covers areas from Indian Pass to the State
Park; requesting that the Board change the Ordinance to include the (Highland View
Beach) area. Chairman Quinn inquired if the County needs to reach out to the City
Commission to come up with an idea before the County moves forward. County
Administrator Hammond stated if the Board will tell him the Board’s position, then the

County Attorney can contact the City (of Port St. Joe) and proceed. Discussion followed
regarding working with the City of Port St. Joe. Members of the Board discussed being in
support of leaving this beach open. Chairman Quinn called for a motion. Commissioner
Rogers made the motion. Chairman Quinn inquired from County Attorney Novak as to
how the motion needs to go. County Attorney Novak stated it would be two fold, the will
of the Board is to keep it open and instruct Administration and the County Attorney to
work with the City to accomplish it, and can include in the motion the desire of the Board
regarding the public access to the beach. He discussed starting with a policy until an
ordinance can be adopted. Chairman Quinn inquired of Commissioner Rogers on whether
he was good (with this motion). Commissioner Rogers had no objections. Sheriff Harrison
appeared before the Board to inquire if the public will be required to have a beach driving
permit to access this portion of the beach. Chairman Quinn said yes. County
Administrator Hammond suggested that it not be enforced until the Ordinance can be
changed, but to go ahead and post it and get the City to agree to allow the County the
enforcement arm for that. Sheriff Harrison requested assistance in getting beach access
signage posted once it is opened up. Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion. After
discussion, Chairman Quinn called for emails and public comment. There being none, the
motion then passed unanimously.

County Administrator Hammond discussed a mask order (COVID-19) from the Chief
Judge and the renovations within the Courthouse due to the waterline rapture. He then
discussed his concerns regarding the new regulations from the Court System;
recommending to the Board that masks be optional for County employees; noting that
Constitutional Officers control their offices and their employees, and the general public
follow the Chief Judge’s order. After discussion, Clerk Norris appeared before the Board
to report that the Chief Judge did try to get it where it was not mandatory; noting
Constitutional Officers (in the Courthouse) can take off their masks once they get into
their designated offices, it is only mandatory (to wear a mask) in the offices when assisting
the public. She reported that masks are mandatory in all common areas, hallways, public
restrooms, the courtroom and entering the facility. Chairman Quinn stated he supports
the wearing of masks in the common areas and allowing the Constitutional Officers to set
the rules within their offices. Commissioner McCroan discussed receiving phone calls
regarding the public standing outside in the heat; asking if there is another option. Upon
inquiry by Commissioner Rich regarding whether mask will be handed out, Clerk Norris
stated that the public are to bring their own mask, but if someone does not have one, a
mask will be provided as long as funds (and supply) allow. After further discussion
regarding the wearing of masks, Clerk Norris stated there is not much that can be done
at this time regarding the elderly standing in the sun due the construction going on within
the Courthouse; reporting chairs have been provided outside in the shade. County
Administrator Hammond requested the Board allow County employees the option to wear
masks inside the Courthouse. Chairman Quinn stated he thinks the County is good; the
public comes in and they have to wear a mask; further stating that Constitutional Officers

address their employees; going with what the Judge recommended. County Administrator
Hammond stated unless the Board tells him no, then he is going to tell County employees
that it is optional to wear a mask in the Courthouse. E.D.C. Director McKnight appeared
before the Board to share his ideas regarding wearing masks.

the project (construction of the new Transfer Station at Five Points Landfill). He requested
the Board approve a change order to pay Highland Wake Construction, LLC $60,000.00.

Commissioner McCroan motioned to approve. Commissioner McDaniel seconded the
motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment or emails. There was none. County
Administrator Hammond asked to include in the motion to allow him or the Chairman to

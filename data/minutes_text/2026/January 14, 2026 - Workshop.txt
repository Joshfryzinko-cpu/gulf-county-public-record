JANUARY 14, 2026
WORKSHOP
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in a workshop with the
following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Phil McCroan,
and Commissioners Christopher McLemore, Lloyd Jack Husband, III, and Randy
Pridgeon.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Chief Deputy Clerk Leanna
Cooper, Deputy Clerk Casi Campbell, County Administrator Michael L. Hammond,
County Assistant Administrator Clay Smallwood, III, Deputy Administrator Kari Summers,
Deputy Administrator Silvia Williams, Building Official Brad Bailey, Gulf County E.D.C.
Director Jim McKnight, Emergency Management Director/Fire Coordinator Matt Herring,
G.I.S. Coordinator Clay Kennedy, Office of Management & Budget Director Sherry
Herring, Mosquito Control Director Austin Horton, County Planner Doug Crane, Public
Works Director Mark Cothran, Assistant Public Works Director Raymond Hart, and T.D.C.
Executive Director Blake Rish.
Chairman Quinn called the meeting to order at 9:04 a.m., E.T.
CITY AND COUNTY ISSUES
County Assistant Administrator Smallwood presented the Board with a road work list that
will be funded by D.O.T. (Department of Transportation) by fiscal years for Board review
<copy provided to the Clerk for the record>, noting the County has applied but not
received funding for Country Club Road and Freeman Rouse Road & Dead Lakes Drive.
He stated by the end of this month the Board will need to provide a road list for upcoming
grant applications to D.O.T. Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns regarding
road work on Long Avenue. Assistant County Administrator Smallwood reported Long
Avenue is in the design phase with construction funds being released July 2027.
Commissioner McCroan confirmed the Long Avenue project is from Madison Street to
U.S. Highway 98. County Administrator Hammond stated that when D.O.T. used the
Country Club Road as a bypass after Hurricane Michael, it was damaged by State
workers, and he will be requesting funding from the new D.O.T. secretary. He discussed
Lake Grove Road being a good road to add to the (D.O.T.) funding list, requesting the
Board submit a list by the end of the month. Commissioner McLemore inquired about the
completion of Dalkeith Road, noting the first section ends at Mace Martin Road. Assistant
County Administrator Smallwood noted the design phase from Lower Dalkeith Road to
the end of payment is in fiscal year 2027, noting this project is one design but two
construction phases. Discussion followed regarding the Dalkeith Road Paving Project.
Commissioner Husband requested staff review County Road 5 Bridge, noting he has
received complaints of potholes. County Assistant Administrator Smallwood stated he will
review the condition of the road and see how to make the repairs. He then continued to

discuss the road list from page 1, noting the second page is the road list funded from the
road bond (bank loan). Assistant County Administrator Smallwood reported these roads
will be ready for advertisement in February 2026 after Dewberry completes the bid
specifications. County Administrator Hammond noted the two roads listed in red are
change orders. Discussion followed. Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns
regarding the drainage conditions on White Sand Road before paving. Commissioner
Pridgeon verified with County Administrator Hammond when meeting with D.O.T. staff he
will also discuss the Back Beach Road Project. E.D.C. Director McKnight appeared before
the Board to discuss the Back Beach Road Project. Discussion followed on summer
beach traffic and how this alternate route would help with the traffic flow.
County Administrator Hammond noted a recent press release by Governor Ron DeSantis.
Assistant County Administrator Smallwood reported the County received news regarding
moving the south transmission (water) main off the coast, noting the line will run from
Jones Homestead to Dead Man’s Curve. He stated this is a $7.7 million project. Upon
inquiry by Commissioner McCroan, County Administrator Hammond stated the goal is
when the White City Water Plant and the Jones Homestead Booster Station come online,
then the County will tie-in the next well, and LUCI I and LUCI II will be booster and
emergency areas only.
Assistant County Administrator Smallwood provided the Board with a sewer project
update, reporting the County received an interlocal agreement with the City of
Wewahitchka and is in communications with the City of Port St. Joe regarding a possible
interlocal. County Administrator Hammond discussed his concerns regarding deadline for
the City of Port St. Joe to decide to joint with the County on this project. He discussed the
County’s desire to run a sewer line to the (Howard Creek) Workcamp with a commitment
from D.O.C. (Department of Corrections) to reopen this facility. Commissioner Pridgeon
discussed using reuse water from the County Wastewater Treatment Plant for not only
the golf course but for ballfields at the school. Commissioner McCroan discussed
communicating with the City of Port St. Joe regarding moving the City’s Wastewater
Treatment Plant off the Canal (Intercoastal Waterway) during their upcoming workshop
with the City.
Commissioner McLemore discussed his concerns regarding the need for enough
workcrews for each district, noting the County received an additional workcrew last year.
He discussed working on various boat landings and parks in his district, noting that more
work is needed. Commissioner McLemore inquired of any updates regarding the Old Gulf
County Courthouse. Commissioner Husband discussed submitting an registry application
to the National Historical Registry and applying for grants to renovate the Old Gulf County
Courthouse in Wewahitchka. Members of the Board discussed their support on this
project.
Commissioner McCroan stated the County is moving forward with the Coast Guard
property, noting Representative Jimmy Patronis is sponsoring a bill that will allow the
County to acquire this property. County Administrator Hammond discussed the drafting of
the bill, noting the County is requesting the property on the Gulf-side, including the road

going into the old Coast Guard barracks and the old Lighthouse property. Commissioner
McCroan discussed the Erosion Control Breakwaters Project, development of a new
County park in Jones Homestead, and the commitment to a revitalize the school soccer
field. Chairman Quinn discussed supporting the soccer field project.
Commissioner Husband welcomed Blake Rish as the new T.D.C. Director. He discussed
the parks and recreational budget, requesting T.D.C. Director Rish to perform a parks
inventory that would allow the Board to prioritize needs and use grant funding accordingly.
Members of the Board discussed grant funds used at parks and boat ramps/docks. T.D.C.
Director Rish stated an inventory has been completed and he will provide a copy to the
Board. Commissioner Husband discussed using the green space at the Overstreet Fire
Department to expand the Overstreet Park. He stated his goal is to get parents interested
in going to the park, discussing incentive ideas. T.D.C. Director Rish discussed staff
reviewing ideas to encourage parents to visit parks and will report back to the Board.
Upon inquiry by Commissioner Pridgeon, Commissioner Husband discussed the County
workforce summer program, noting he is in support of continuing this program each year.
Chairman Quinn directed T.D.C. Director Rish to review the workout station at the
Washington Gym Recreational Center for the parents’ incentive program.
Commissioner Pridgeon discussed rental inspections and rental property owners paying
bed taxes. T.D.C. Director Rish discussed working with the County and the Gulf County
Tax Collector to assist with the inspections and collection. Commissioner Pridgeon
discussed adding the elevator key location information to the inspection paperwork.
Discussion followed regarding a proposed action plan for rental elevator keys. County
Administrator Hammond discussed the County wanted to make it a requirement that
rental elevator keys be placed in a central location, noting majority of WindMark is rental
property and the City of Port St. Joe will have to implement this rule. Discussion followed.
Commissioner Pridgeon discussed trash services with BCC Waste Solutions. Members
of the Board discussed the current waste service provider and keeping the cost down for
the customers. Commissioner Pridgeon then discussed his concerns regarding spring
break with the recent changes in Bay County and the advertisements in WindMark with
six-seater golf carts and sleeps 24, noting Gulf County is a family environment.
Commissioner McCroan discussed problems last year’s with college students digging
massive holes on the beaches, supporting the Board communicating with Sheriff
Harrison. Discussion followed. T.D.C. Director Rish noted the marketing campaign targets
the older age group. Discussion followed regarding considering no alcohol on the beach
during spring break. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed the living shoreline at Highland
View. Upon request by Commissioner Pridgeon, OMB Director Herring provided an
update regarding the fire academy program. Commissioner Pridgeon noted the County
has received grant funds to purchase equipment and will be applying for Triumph funds
for a paid instructor. Discussion followed. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed making Bird
Island a bird sanctuary. Members of the Board expressed support in making Bird Island
a bird sanctuary. County Administrator Hammond discussed the location and positive
outcomes with creating the bird sanctuary. Discussion followed. Commissioner Pridgeon
discussed ready-made bathrooms at the City of Parker (in Bay County), noting the golf
course needs restrooms. County Administrator Hammond discussed the ready-made

bathroom units are on State contract, recommended the Board start with three units, two
at the golf course and one at Dunes Drive. Discussion followed regarding the ready-made
units and their possible locations. County Administrator Hammond stated he will bring a
price point before the Board at the next regular meeting. Commissioner Pridgeon
discussed the library usage, inquiring about the funding and the Saturday closure.
Commissioner Pridgeon discussed closing a day in the week to allow for opening hours
on Saturdays. County Administrator Hammond discussed the benefits of opening on
Saturdays and afternoons for the children, stated he will have a recommendation for the
Board at the next meeting to consider regarding the library operations. Commissioner
Pridgeon discussed funding and cost of living expenses. OMB Director Herring discussed
the funds provided to the library. Discussion followed regarding funding and operational
hours that will benefit the residents. County Administrator Hammond reported that
recently both libraries were closed on Saturdays, but now Port St. Joe is open on the
second and fourth Saturday from 10:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m., ET and the first and third
Saturday from 9:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m., CT in Wewahitchka even though the County
provided funding for the libraries to be open on Saturdays. Discussion followed.
Commissioner Pridgeon discussed touring the dry dock maritime academy in Mobile,
Alabama and the proposed project site (old Arizionia Chemical property) for the County
Maritime Academy. Members of the Board expressed support of the proposed maritime
academy project. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed Federal and State funds available
to fund this project, noting this training facility will be an asset to Gulf County. E.D.C.
Director McKnight discussed the training facility and funding sources. Commissioner
Pridgeon discussed the land for the training site. County Administrator Hammond
discussed teaming with the Port Authority on the site for the maritime academy and the
floating dry dock, discussing his concerns regarding the reconstruction of the Port
Authority board. Discussion followed. Port Authority Treasurer Jessica Rish appeared
before the Board to state she is in support of the maritime academy but undecided on
selling the property (old Arizonia Chemical site) to the County. She discussed her
concerns regarding the refiguration of the Port Authority board. Discussion followed.
Chairman Quinn stated if the bill passes then the Port board members will change. County
Attorney Novak discussed the binding agreement the County has with the Port Authority
regarding this property. County Administrator Hammond discussed his concerns
regarding the commitment from the Port Authority for a site for the Maritime Academy,
noting the exact site location is undetermined at this time. Guerry Magidson, Port
Authority Chairman appeared before the Board to discuss the Port’s assets and debts,
noting the Port Authority is in support of the dry dock and the academy. Discussion
followed. Chairman Quinn discussed working with the Port Authority to gain control of the
site before the Port board changes members. County Attorney Novak discussed bringing
a recommendation back before the Board on the matter. After discussion by
Commissioner Pridgeon, Commissioner Husband inquired if the Board purchased the
property that there be a stipulation if the bill does not pass and the Port Authority board
does not change, then the County will not obtain ownership of the property, it will remain
with the current Port Authority. County Attorney Novak discussed drafting a contract with
conditions based on the outcome of the bill passing. Discussion followed. Upon inquiry
by Port Authority Attorney Tom Gibson, Commissioner Pridgeon discussed the phases to
the proposed maritime academy site.

Public Works Director Cothran provided a status update regarding various projects Public
Works is working to complete and projects completed last year. Commissioner Pridgeon
thanked Public Works and T.D.C. staff for completing these projects, noting Board
members would not be able to provide a new project list this year if it was not for the hard
work of staff to complete last year’s project list. Public Works Director Cothran noted that
a canopy will soon be installed at the fueling depot.
There being no further business, and upon motion by Commissioner McCroan, second
by Commissioner Pridgeon, and a unanimous vote, the meeting did then adjourn at 10.59
a.m., E.T.
SANDY QUINN, JR.

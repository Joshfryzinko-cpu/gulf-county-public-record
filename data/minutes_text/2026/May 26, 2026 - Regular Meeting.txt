GULF COUNTY VOTING DISTRICTS
Frank Dykes, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to discuss his concerns regarding
the State redistricting in Louisiana, noting that Gulf County has a minority district due to
MCA862035RV Nathan Peters vs Gulf County, Florida by the same voter’s right act 1965.
He discussed the court ruling of single member district, noting that the authorization of
the Board may be in jeopardy due to public voting. Mr. Dykes stated if the Supreme Court
rules that single-member districts cannot be created, but Gulf County has one, and
members of the Board were elected it brings doubt to Board members’ authority. Upon
motion by Commissioner McCroan, second by Commissioner Pridgeon, and unanimous
vote, the Board extended Mr. Dykes time to speak. Mr. Dykes continued to discuss the
Supreme Court’s ruling. County Administrator Hammond discussed the history and the
difference between Gulf County and Louisianna is that Gulf County has a stipulated
agreement between parties. He reported that Gulf County currently does not have a
minority/majority district. Commissioner McCroan discussed voting districts, noting in the
past voters voted for all districts but the candidate was required to live in that represented
district. Mr. Dykes continued to discuss his concerns regarding the court mandated district
lines. Discussion followed. County Attorney Novak stated that everything that the Board
does is valid and enforceable, noting members of this Board are duly elected Officials of
Gulf County under the laws of Florida and the Federal Consent Decree. He discussed the
Federal Consent Decree, noting there is no minority/majority district which was
established ten (10) years ago. Discussion followed. Commissioner McCroan inquired
what would be the mechanism if the County wanted to go back to at-large (Countywide)
voting. County Attorney Novak discussed the Board petitioning the court and the other
option is the interested parties from the original petition. County Administrator Hammond
noted that the hired attorney from Tallahassee (Coppins, Monroe, Adkins, Dincman &
Spellman, P.A.) stated that it would have been better for Gulf County in 1986 to go to
court and lost; then it could have been overturned. He stated this is no different than
settling out of court with a settlement agreement and forty years later wanting to change
the agreed upon dollar amount. Commissioner McCroan discussed the changes over time
and the new Supreme Court ruling. County Attorney Novak noted he will review the ruling,
work with Attorney Spellman, and bring a recommendation back to the Board. County
Administrator Hammond stated that these are two separate issues, noting Gulf County is
not gerrymandering, and the Supreme Court was ruling on gerrymandering. Discussion
followed.

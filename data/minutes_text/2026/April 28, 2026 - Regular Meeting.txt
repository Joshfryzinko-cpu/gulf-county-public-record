RESOLUTION – REMNANT DEFENDANT OPIOID SETTLEMENT AGREEMENT
County Administrator Hammond directed the Board to pages 1 and 2 of the Administrative
Update and Report packet <copy provided to the Clerk for the record>. County Attorney
Novak discussed the proposed resolution on pages 1-2 as the County continued
participation to the opioid litigation settlement, requesting a vote of the Board to adopt.
Chairman Quinn noted that once the Resolution is adopted, staff will submit the
appropriate documentation as requested. He then called for a motion to approve this
Resolution and authorize his signature. Commissioner McCroan so moved.
Commissioner Husband seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously, as follows:
RESOLUTION NO. 2026-22
WHEREAS, the Gulf County Board of County Commissioner (the
“Subdivision”) has been involved in litigation and/or potential claims relating
to the opioid epidemic; and

WHEREAS, a proposed nationwide settlement known as the
Remnant Defendant Settlement Agreement (the “Agreement”) has been
negotiated to resolve claims against certain opioid-related defendants; and
WHEREAS, the Agreement provides for monetary payments and
other relief to participating political subdivisions, with allocations determined
pursuant to the Plan of Allocations set forth in Exhibit E of the Agreement;
and
WHEREAS, the Agreement contemplates participation by political
subdivisions through execution of required participation documents and
releases, and provides for the administration and distribution of settlement
funds by a Notice and Claims Administrator (see § VI(B)(1)(D)); and
WHEREAS, participation in the Agreement is intended to secure
funding for opioid remediation and related purposes for the benefit of the
Subdivision and its residents;
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE BOARD OF
COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF THE GULF COUNTY BOARD OF
COUNTY COMMISSIONERS:
1. Approval of Settlement Participation.
The Subdivision hereby approves participation in the Remnant
Defendant Settlement Agreement and elects to become a Participating
Subdivision, subject to the terms and conditions set forth therein.
2. Authorization of Execution.
The Chairman is hereby authorized to execute all necessary documents
required for participation in the Agreement, including any Participation
Agreement, Release, or related certificates.
3. Acknowledgment of Allocation and Use of Funds.
The Subdivision acknowledges that any settlement funds received will
be allocated pursuant to Exhibit E and administered in accordance with
the Agreement, including any applicable State-Subdivision Agreement
governing distribution and use of funds (§I(VV)).
4. Compliance with Terms.
The Subdivision agrees to comply with all applicable terms of the
Agreement, including requirements related to claims submission,
release of claims, and use of funds for approved opioid remediation
purposes.
5. Effective Date.
This Resolution shall take effect immediately upon adoption.
(end)

PLANNING ASSISTANCE TO STATES STUDY – ST. JOSEPH BAY
Keith Neel, of Bay Savers appeared before the Board to discuss the results from the
installed flow meters, noting water flow is being diverted from the Apalachicola River
through Lake Wimico and into St. Joe Bay and East Bay. He stated that Bay Savers seeks
to engage the Army Corps of Engineers to mitigate this unwanted flow diversion and ask
the County to take the next step in planning an assistance States study by submitting a
letter of intent to the Corps of Engineers. Mr. Neel discussed the cost shares between the
County and the Corps of Engineers and highlights of next steps once the study is
complete. Upon motion by Commissioner McCroan, second by Commissioner Pridgeon,
and unanimous vote, the Board extended Mr. Neel’s time to speak. Mr. Neel requested,
on behalf of Bay Savers, that the County sign the Gulf County PAS (Planning Assistance
to States) Study letter of intent and submit the letter to the Corps of Engineers.
Commissioner Husband discussed the efforts and possible funding sources. Mr. Neel
stated that Representative Jason Shoaf has submitted appropriation to cover the cost,
noting that it has made it through the first round. He also noted that City of Port St. Joe
has shown interested in contributing financially, reporting that he will be also reaching out
to Franklin County for financial support. Discussion followed. Commissioner Pridgeon
inquired if the study is just flow are will there be detailed testing of the water content. Mr.

Neel stated the testing will be the nutrients in the water, sedimentation, etc., in addition to
the flow. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Pridgeon, Mr. Neel stated Bay Savers is asking
the Board to sign the letter of intent for the study. Discussion followed. Members of the
Board inquired when would the County be obligated to pay the $150,000.00. Mr. Neel
stated when the Corps funds their portion is when the County will be obligated to sign the
agreement and fifteen days to supply the County’s financial obligation. Commissioner
Husband made a motion to approve the letter of intent to the Corps of Engineers for the
Section 22 study to get the funding started, noting this does not tie the County’s hands to
any funding out of the County’s budget. Commissioner Pridgeon seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Mr. Neel stated that East Bay is affected as
well in Bay County. Chairman Quinn called for any additional public comment. There
being none, the motion passed unanimously.

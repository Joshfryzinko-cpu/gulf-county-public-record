FEBRUARY 2, 2017
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
WORKSHOP

following members present: Chairman Ward McDaniel, Vice Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr.,

Others present: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Clerk Rebecca L. Norris, Deputy Clerk
Leanna Roberts, Chief Administrator Don Butler, Assistant Administrator Michael L.
Hammond, Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Building Official George Knight, Central
Services Director Lynn Lanier, Gulf County E.D.C. Director Chris Holley, Gulf County
E.D.C. Staff Assistant Lianna Sagins, G.I.S. Coordinator Jessica Susich, County Planner

Chairman McDaniel called the meeting to order at 9:02 a.m., E.T.
COBRA
Chief Administrator Butler discussed the history of COBRA (Coastal Barrier Resource
Act); reporting that it was put in place in 1982 in Gulf County. He provided a thirty (30)
year timeline regarding COBRA. After discussion, Chief Administrator Butler provided a
recap of the highpoints; stating that as long as Gulf County relies on someone’s
interpretation, Gulf County is going to lose. He stated that Gulf County needs someone
to make a decision. Chief Administrator Butler discussed two options: 1) do nothing; 2)
take a route other than the administrative route that the County has been trying to take.
County Attorney Novak discussed past efforts regarding COBRA; stating that Staff needs
direction from the Board as to how to move forward. He reported that the Board can do
nothing or a recommendation can be presented to the Board of alternative approaches,
which is a third party interpretation of the facts. County Attorney Novak stated that Staff
would like to present budget requests, alternative paths, a timeline, an alternative to
administrative review to the Board at the next meeting for consideration. Upon request by
Commissioner McCroan, RESTORE Act Coordinator Yeager appeared before the Board
to discuss COBRA; stating that the Board has two options: 1) County does nothing
because we have exhausted everything administratively or 2) the County will have to go
through some type of judicial review with this program. He reported that it will benefit Gulf
County if the COBRA is removed. After discussion by County Attorney Novak, Chairman
McDaniel called for public comment. Pat Hardman, President of Coastal Community
Association appeared before the Board to request that the Board move forward with
judicial review or litigation; discussing her concerns regarding COBRA.

RESTORE
RESTORE Act Coordinator Yeager appeared before the Board to present a handout for
the Board to review <copy provided to the Clerk for the record> of possible projects for
Pot III; reporting that the Board will soon need to make a determination of what projects
will be funded with Pot III (also called Consortium Pot) monies. He requested that the
Board provide him with direction as to what projects the Board would like to see assigned
to Pot III. RESTORE Act Coordinator Yeager reported that the deadline to submit project
detail to U.S. Treasury is April, 2017; also reporting that projects from Pot I can be used
for Pot III. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Whitfield, RESTORE Act Coordinator Yeager
reported that there is $12.8 million for each County in Pot III, some will be used in paying
the consultants, but the County can count on approximately a little over $11 million that
will come over a 15 (fifteen) year period. He reported that they are still trying to get
approval from the U.S. Treasury to get bonding on this money. Commissioner Whitfield
asked if the County can change out the projects each year or will they have to plan
projects for the entire $11 million. RESTORE Act Coordinator Yeager stated that there
has to be one (1) State expenditure plan and while it could be amended, there will be
issues if one (1) County changes their plan. He reported that this is why the Consortium
is trying to encourage the Counties to do large projects and to provide to the consultants
more than their $12.8 million. Chairman McDaniel stated that the County’s goal is to take
the $12.8 million and turn it into $50 million through grants.
HABITAT CONSERVATION PLAN
Chief Administrator Butler discussed HCP (Habitat Conservation Plan); presenting a
handout to the Board for review <copy provided to the Clerk for the record>. He reported
that he thinks the County can handle the additional work due to the HCP with current Staff
instead of hiring a biologist and the associated cost. Chief Administrator Butler stated that
the proposed HCP will be presented to the Board on February 21st for consideration. After
discussion by Chief Administrator Butler, County Attorney Novak stated that the County
is in the final stages; reporting that it is Federal Law and it is not going to change. He
discussed the process if the Board adopts the HCP. Chairman McDaniel thanked Kristi
Yanchis, of U.S. Fish and Wildlife for working with Gulf County on the drafting of the HCP.
He reported that the permits will be obtained locally. Assistant Administrator Hammond
discussed his concerns regarding the proposed HCP; reporting that this plan is going to
be very expensive to the property owners on the Cape. Upon inquiry by Commissioner
McCroan, County Attorney Novak reported that with this project Gulf County is unique
due to the density and the number of parcels involved. He stated that the proposed plan
that will be presented to the Board has been tailored to Gulf County. After discussion by
County Attorney Novak, Chairman McDaniel called for public comment. Kristi Yanchis, of
U.S. Fish and Wildlife appeared before the Board to discuss the proposed HCP. Pat
Hardman, President of Coastal Community Association appeared before the Board to
discuss her concerns regarding the HCP and the process to get to the final proposed
plan; stating that the Board needs to adopt this plan. Chief Administrator Butler reported
that the final draft will be presented to the Board on February 21st for consideration.

2017 SCALLOP SEASON
T.D.C. Executive Director Adams appeared before the Board to report that FWC has
approved the 2017 scallop season to run from July 25th through September 10th. She
stated that FWC is recommending another caging project for Gulf County.

ROAD ACCEPTANCE – SPRUCE AVENUE
Chief Administrator Butler discussed multiple Limited Maintenance Agreements on
Spruce Avenue in Creekwood Subdivision; reporting that the County has gone in
numerous times and worked on this road. He stated that the cheapest thing for the County
to do is go in and put a thin layer of asphalt on a portion of this roadway (Spruce Avenue).
Chief Administrator Butler reported that he received a bid this morning, obtaining two price
options: 1. from County Road 386 (Overstreet Road) to the intersection of Fork Drive, in
the amount of $18,876.40, or 2. the entire roadway (Spruce Avenue) for a total cost of
$33,790.30. For the Board’s consideration, he stated the County could do the first block
(to the intersection of Fork Drive); solving a lot of the problem, but by doing the entire
roadway, it will really solve the problem. Chief Administrator Butler reported this will be
paid from District III’s Road Bond money and Secondary Road and Bridge Funds, if
approved by the Board. After discussion by Chairman McDaniel, Chief Administrator
Butler stated his number one (1) recommendation would be to pave up to Fork Drive for
$18,876.40; noting in the long run the County would be better off paving the entire stretch
of roadway (Spruce Avenue). Commissioner Rogers discussed the road problems on
Spruce Avenue stating that he is in agreement with Chief Administrator Butler’s first
recommendation which is to pave up to Fork Drive. Commissioner McCroan inquired as
to who owns the road. Chief Administrator Butler reported that the owner is deceased,
and there is no one claiming the roadway. Discussion followed. Chief Administrator Butler
discussed holding a discussion another day; stating that he is not for the County taking
on additional roads, but with that being said, there are going to be some roads that it
would only make sense for the County to accept as a County road. He said it would be a
good topic to hold a workshop on to discuss what really needs to be done; reporting that
there are roads within the County where the owners have passed on. Commissioner
McCroan inquired if the County could claim (Spruce Avenue) as a County road. County
Attorney Novak stated that there are three (3) criteria: 1. they predate your current
development regulations; 2. the County has a history and precedent set where the County

has established Limited Maintenance Agreements; or 3. the County looks at the cost
benefit. After discussion by County Attorney Novak, Chairman McDaniel reported that this
road (Spruce Avenue) is paved, but is full of potholes. Commissioner Rogers motioned
to accept Chief Administrator Butler’s recommendation to pave (Spruce Avenue) down to
the first intersection (Fork Drive). Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion.
Chairman McDaniel called for public comment. There being no public comment, the
motion passed unanimously (4-0).

Allegiance to the Flag.
HOUSING
S.H.I.P. Director Joe Paul appeared before the Board to announce the Hurricane Michael
Loan Program <material provided to the Clerk for the record>. He explained that this
program is for low income homebuyers; providing $15,000.00 down payment assistance.
S.H.I.P. Director Paul reported that the second program, which is the HOP program
(Homeownership Pool) is available to profit and non-profit developers; stating the only
requirement is that they must be a HOP member.
Chairman Quinn discussed the H.H.R.P. (Hurricane Housing Recovery Program);
suggesting that each District receive $1 million; allowing each District to be represented
with the money and the funding being split evenly between the very low, low, and
moderate-income levels. He recommended that: (A) very low-income will be a grant,
requiring no mortgage or proof of insurance; with a grant maximum of $20,000.00 for
rehabilitation, and limited to $40,000.00 for the purchase of a mobile or modular home;
(B) low-income would require a ten (10) year mortgage, forgiven just like the S.H.I.P.
rules, with a $25,000.00 maximum for rehabilitation and a maximum down payment
assistance of $50,000.00, with insurance being required; (C) moderate-income would

require a ten (10) year mortgage, with the first five (5) years not being forgiven, and the
next (5) five years forgiven at 20% (twenty percent) per year. He stated that only down
payment assistance will be considered for this program category; providing a maximum
of $60,000.00 or 50%, whichever is less, with insurance being required. Additionally,
Chairman Quinn recommended that; (a) no one with a current S.H.I.P. loan will be eligible
if they are in the low or moderate-income categories; (b) the application deadline be
extended until 5:00 p.m., E.T. on September 30, 2019; (c) applicants must be a Gulf
County resident on October 10, 2018 to qualify; (d) all applications must be screened and
separated by District and by very low, low and moderate income levels; (e) all qualified
applications will be eligible for a random drawing in an open County Commission meeting
in October, 2019; (f) all applications will be given a number and once drawn, a list will be
generated and final screening will be completed; (g) following the drawing applicants will
be contacted; allowing applicants 30 (thirty) days to finish submitting their qualifications;
and finally (h) when an applicant fails to meet the criteria within the 30 (thirty) days, with
an official notice, they will be disqualified and the next applicant in line will be considered
until all funds are disbursed. After discussion by members of the Board, County
Administrator Hammond reported that there are two (2) more programs, DAP and
C.D.B.G., to follow; stating that after the Board makes a decision regarding this matter,
the L.H.A.P, (Local Housing Assistance Plan) needs to be amended to reflect this change.
He discussed considering the use of a portion of the S.H.I.P. money to subsidize the very
low (income) that are short (on funding). After discussion by Commissioner McDaniel,
County Administrator Hammond stated that staff needs a couple of motions: 1) need a
motion to accept the Chairman’s proposed plan; and 2) need a motion for the Chairman
to sign the agreement with Florida Housing. S.H.I.P. Director Paul appeared before the
Board to discuss the motion allowing the Chairman to sign the H.H.R.P. (Hurricane
Housing Recovery Program) agreement based on the Chairman’s review and those
strategies added. He requested the Board revoke the $80,000.00 for purchase assistance
and put it back at $30,000.00 for S.H.I.P. County Administrator Hammond suggested that
at the next meeting, or one in October, that the L.H.A.P. be amended to reflect this
change; putting all the strategies in (the plan) going forward. County Administrator
Hammond directed S.H.I.P. Director Paul, before the next meeting, to provide the Board
with information regarding the option to allocate and set aside current year S.H.I.P. funds
that could assist with some of these strategies, specifically the very low income. Chairman
Quinn asked if some of the D.A.P. money could be put into the S.H.I.P. County
Administrator Hammond stated that staff will have to wait until the rules are provided (from
Florida Housing) to make that determination. After discussion by County Administrator
Hammond, Chairman Quinn called for a motion to approve the H.H.R.P. program.
Commissioner McCroan so moved. Commissioner McDaniel seconded the motion. After
discussion, Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment,
the motion passed unanimously. Chairman Quinn then called for a motion to allow him to
sign the H.H.R.P. agreement. Commissioner Rogers so moved. Commissioner Rich
seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public
comment, the motion passed unanimously. Commissioner McDaniel inquired of the
projection of next year’s funding for S.H.I.P. S.H.I.P. Director Paul reported that the
standard S.H.I.P. money is $350,000.00 per year; discussing the possibility of pairing

S.H.I.P. funds with H.H.R.P. money, but noting that there will be two different mortgage
agreements.
EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT GRANT / N.R.C.S. GRANT
County Administrator Hammond requested permission from the Board to apply for an
Emergency Management Grant to be used to pay the match amount required by the
N.R.C.S. grant; reporting the N.R.C.S. grant is a $5.3 million grant that will fund cleanup
of the Simons Bayou area, and the backside of Highland View that has houses in canals
and ditches (due to Hurricane Michael). He requested permission to advertise for this.
County Administrator Hammond recommended to take $1.2 million (from the Emergency
Management Grant) and apply it as the match for the N.R.C.S. Grant. Commissioner Rich
so moved. Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. Upon inquiry by Norris Clerk regarding the motion, County Administrator
Hammond stated the County would be applying for a Department of Emergency
Management Grant under a new program; recommending applying for this grant as the
match funds for the N.R.C.S. grant that the County received preliminary approval for but
does not have the money for the match requirement. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously. County
Administrator Hammond reported that AshBritt will be doing the work for us on this project
and will possibly be sub-contracting it out; suggesting that the County use their own
inspectors this time. He reported that the County has the potential to take our own wood
debris and that from State agencies but will keep the Board posted on this matter.
TRANSFER STATION
County Administrator Hammond requested permission from the Board to award this to
the low-bidder, Highland Group, if he can get the number satisfactory to proceed with the
Transfer Station (Five Points Landfill). Commissioner McDaniel so moved. Commissioner
McCroan seconded the motion. Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn regarding the source of
funding to pay for this project, County Administrator Hammond reported that the majority
of the funds are coming from a State grant, in the amount of $1 million. He reported that
the County purchased the building, the scales, and the scale house; noting the concrete
work is too large of a project to do in-house. County Administrator Hammond reported
that once in operation, the County will lease it to the new garbage hauler for the County
(BCC Waste Solutions * Bid #1819-05), which will generate money. Chairman Quinn
called for public comment. There being no public comment, the motion passed
unanimously.

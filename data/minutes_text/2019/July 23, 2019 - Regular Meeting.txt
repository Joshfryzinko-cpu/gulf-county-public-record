REPORT – ST. JOE BAY / GULF COUNTY CANAL
Dusty May, of Bay Savers appeared before the Board to discuss Bay Savers and provide
the Board an update report regarding the economic conditions of the St. Joseph Bay. He
discussed the water shed and water sources within Gulf County. Commissioner McDaniel
motioned to extend time. Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion. Mr. May
discussed the run-off problems and saltwater intrusion; reporting that a lot of the natural
grass is gone, becoming marsh. He discussed building a lock, south of the ‘T’, and the
benefits of this lock for Gulf County. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McCroan, Mr. May
stated that the Wetappo run-off will not affect the Gulf County Canal all that much and
can be controlled by existing run-off legislation.
UPDATE – GULF CORRECTIONAL INSTITUTE
Scott Payne, GCI Warden appeared before the Board to provide an update regarding the
correctional facility; reporting they began receiving inmates July 1, 2019 with the current
population now being 255. He stated that the Annex is still under a reconstruction phase.
Commissioner McDaniel thanked Mr. Payne for the update and requested he notify the
Board if the County can help. Mr. Payne discussed his concerns regarding the need for
support from the Legislator; reporting that staff needs a pay raise. He requested that the
County review the bridge leading to the prison; reporting it is bad going out on the
transport buses. Commissioner McDaniel stated that this bridge is top of the list. County
Administrator Hammond reported that the structure of the bridge is fine, it is the top deck
that needs to be replaced and the County has submitted a grant application for funding
of this project.

UPDATE – RADIO COMMUNICATION SYSTEM
E-9-1-1 Director Guthrie appeared before the Board to provide an update regarding the
County’s Emergency Radio System; reporting that space has been secured on a tower
at the North end of the County and he is working on obtaining buildings at each tower site
(North and South ends of the County). He stated that because the County is changing
locations with the equipment, all has to be approved by F.C.C. (Federal Communications
Commission); reporting that the applications were submitted and now waiting on approval
before any equipment can be purchased. County Administrator Hammond stated that he
has given E-9-1-1 Director Guthrie approval to move forward on all these things;
discussing his concerns regarding the price tag. E-9-1-1 Director Guthrie reported that

the repeaters and coaxes to accommodate both ends of the County will run approximately
$65,000.00-$70,000.00, which includes installation. County Administrator Hammond
stated the County will reuse the generator on the South end of the County, but uncertain
if the generator on the North end can be used. County Commissioner McDaniel discussed
his concerns regarding this matter; encouraging E-9-1-1 Director Guthrie to stay on top
of this.

CODE ENFORCEMENT
Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns regarding an increase in code violations,
especially in the Oak Grove area, since the County went to one RV (recreational vehicle)
per parcel; stating the Board needs to put some teeth in this. Upon inquiry by Chairman
Quinn, County Administrator Hammond discussed the code enforcement process;
suggesting a change in the Ordinance to include jail time and cutting the power off. After
discussion by County Attorney Novak, County Administrator Hammond recommended
the Board amend the Building Ordinance or the Code Enforcement Ordinance. After
discussion by Commissioner Rogers, County Administrator Hammond stated that if it is
the Board’s desire, then staff will draft an ordinance change and bring it before the Board
and the next meeting. Members of the Board were in agreement.
TRASH SERVICE
Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns regarding complaints, mostly in the
tourist corridor, that several households don’t have garbage service and are using
everyone else’s trash cans; asking the Board their thoughts on going to mandatory
garbage service in the tourist corridor. After discussion by Chairman Quinn, Assistant
Administrator Yeager reported that some owners don’t purchase enough cans, and some
don’t have a can at all. County Administrator Hammond stated that he believes the Board
should do mandatory trash service in the tourist corridor. After discussion by Assistant
County Administrator Yeager, Jason Tunnell, President of BCC Waste Solutions
appeared before the Board to discuss the proposed mandatory trash service in the tourist
corridor and RFID chips being placed on the cans within the tourist corridor. After further
discussion, County Attorney Novak reported that the County has already picked up over
400 Waste Pro trash cans. Mr. Tunnell reported that there are still commercial Waste Pro
dumpsters that have not been picked up by Waste Pro. County Administrator Hammond
stated that the problem in the tourist corridor is not enough cans and not enough
frequency of pickup.

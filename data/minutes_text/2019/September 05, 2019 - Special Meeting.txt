DISASTER HOUSING/HOUSING PLAN/S.H.I.P.
S.H.I.P. Director Paul appeared before the Board to present documents <copy provided
to the Clerk for the record> and discuss the current housing plan. He requested Board
approval to increase the purchase assistance to a maximum of $80,000.00. Upon inquiry
by Chairman Quinn regarding prior discussion on the County providing up to $80,000.00
for the H.H.R.P. (Hurricane Housing Recovery Program), County Administrator
Hammond stated this was the County’s initial proposal for the moderate-income level
(workforce housing), but under this proposal it could extend to the low and moderate
income. S.H.I.P. Director Paul discussed the needed credit score in order to receive a
bank loan; stating that with the H.H.R.P. funding and increasing to $80,000.00, it will allow
the County to go into the median income, which is $60,050.00 annually for a single
person. He reported that this change would only require a technical revision which could
be emailed to Florida Housing for approval, followed by the presentation of the revised
L.H.A.P. (Local Housing Assistance Program) to the Board for ratification. Upon inquiry
by Chairman Quinn, S.H.I.P. Director Paul clarified that this funding will be for purchase
assistance or for building a home; noting the recipients can use their own contractors.

County Administrator Hammond affirmed that the presented proposal is up to $80,000.00,
not 50% or up to $80,000.00. Discussion followed regarding the proposal and bank
approvals for a loan. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McDaniel, S.H.I.P. Director Paul
stated that he is requesting approval from the Board to do a technical revision to the
County’s current L.H.A.P.; increasing purchase assistance from $30,000.00 to a
maximum of $80,000.00. Commissioner McDaniel made the motion to cap it at
$80,000.00. Commissioner Rich seconded the motion. Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn,
S.H.I.P. Director Paul reported that this can come out of either S.H.I.P. or H.H.R.P. funds.
Chairman Quinn called of public comment. There being no public comment, the motion
passed unanimously.
S.H.I.P. Director Paul then discussed the two (2) additional recommended strategies (1.
demolition reconstruction and, 2. Emergency Repair) under the H.H.R.P. funding
agreement; reporting this will not change the L.H.A.P., with the strategies only being in
force while H.H.R.P. is in force. He also advised that this H.H.R.P. includes loan
forgiveness upon the recipient’s death, but noted that is not the case for the L.H.A.P.
S.H.I.P. Director Paul requested that the Board ratify this Housing Agreement as
presented; giving S.H.I.P. staff permission to administer the program. Chairman Quinn
discussed concerns regarding applicant qualifications; suggesting all qualified applicants
be invited to the board room and a lottery drawing be conducted; omitting the point
system. He stated that he is going to recommend a third party administer this program for
transparency purposes. Discussion followed regarding the administration of the program,
eligibility, the point system, and ongoing review for compliance. Upon inquiry by
Commissioner McDaniel regarding a deadline on approving this agreement, S.H.I.P.
Director Paul reported that the money will start coming in October or November and this
agreement needs to be in place, approved by Florida Housing, before the County will
receive any money. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McDaniel regarding who would
administer the program if they outsourced it, S.H.I.P. Director Paul stated the only party
who has expressed interest is A.R.P.C. (Apalachee Regional Planning Council). County
Administrator Hammond reported that A.R.P.C. is administering this program in other
Counties that do not have the staffing. He discussed his concerns regarding staffing,
having S.H.I.P. rules that are followed, and the need for review and tracking compliance.
Commissioner McCroan asked if A.R.P.C. would track the file for the life of the loan or
just administer the funds; stating this is something that needs to be looked at. Chairman
Quinn stated that he is not ready to make a decision on this today. After further discussion
about the program and options for how recipients are chosen, County Administrator
Hammond discussed scheduling another workshop before the regular meeting in
September to discuss this matter further.
S.H.I.P. Director Paul recommended the Board reject Bid #1819-45, S.H.I.P. Project for
Bertha Quinn, due to no bids being received; noting this is the second time this project
has been up for bid which no bids were received. Additionally, He requested permission
to rebid this project next S.H.I.P. year (State fiscal year) after contractors have been
approved. Commissioner McDaniel motioned to reject. Commissioner McCroan
seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There was no public

comment. Chairman Quinn called for any objection to the rejecting of the bid and
rebidding the project. With no objections, the motion passed unanimously.
S.H.I.P. Director Paul requested the Board award Bid #1819-46, S.H.I.P. Project for
Jennifer Bailey, to Raymond Driesbach, LLC in the amount of $40,000.00; noting
Driesbach was the only bidder. He reported the total bid was for $37,944.00, and with the
closing cost it will not go over the $40,000.00. Commissioner McCroan motioned to
accept. Commissioner Rich seconded the motion. Upon inquiry by Commissioner
McDaniel regarding Mr. Driesbach, S.H.I.P. Director Paul positively affirmed his work and
participation as a contractor in the program. Chairman Quinn called for public comment.
There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously.
S.H.I.P. Director Paul requested direction from the Board; reporting that his office has
advertised and will begin receiving applications on Monday. He recommended these
applications be stacked in a box until the Board decides on a plan/policy (on the
H.H.R.P.). Following discussion about documenting the timeliness of applications,
S.H.I.P. Director Paul stated that they will stack completed applications into a box until
the Board gives direction. Chairman Quinn was in agreement. County Administrator
Hammond asked for a short recess.

Board and request other project funding; noting the Board is under no obligation to fund

away’ the 10th Street Park, the City (of Port St. Joe) decided not to do the 10th Street Park.
He requested Board direction on either writing a letter to the City (of Port St. Joe) or call
City Manager Jim Anderson; inquiring if his interpretation is correct in that the County did
not vote to give the City of Port St. Joe $800,000.00 to spend, that the vote was to do a
project on 10th Street Park that is now been cancelled. The Board agreed that the funds

the City’s Resolution 6A regarding the 10th Street Park; reporting that the Board was

County Administrator Hammond reported that the County paved Gulf Aire Drive less than
a month ago and a week after it was paved, the City (of Port St. Joe) cut the brand-new
road. He stated that the County has a policy that does not allow any utility, including the
City, to cut a road, yet this has become common place. County Administrator Hammond
requested direction from the Board regarding him reaching out to the City or the Chairman
writing a letter; stating this policy needs to be enforced. He stated that his understanding
is that the City is not going to cap the road. Public Works Director Cothran reported that
he and County Engineer Clay Smallwood identified yesterday twenty-nine (29) different
places where roads had been cut in order to work on some kind of water or sewer
infrastructure underneath and then patched back inappropriately. He reported that the
City has been notified of the 29 (twenty-nine) locations and we are waiting on the City’s
decision regarding repairs and using their remaining road bond funds; recommending that
now would be a great time to correctly fix the patches considering that Roberts and
Roberts, Inc. is working within the County on the Road Bond (projects). Public Works
Director Cothran stated that he is in agreement with County Administrator Hammond
regarding enforcement of this policy. Commissioner McCroan directed Public Works
Director Cothran or County Administrator Hammond to contact City Manager Jim
Anderson to see if this can get worked out. County Administrator Hammond discussed
the need for the Board to say “do not cut our roads anymore”. Commissioner McCroan
agreed, and discussed his concerns regarding cut roads in Oak Grove. Upon inquiry by
County Administrator Hammond, Chairman Quinn was in agreement (with Commissioner
McCroan’s direction). Commissioner Rich asked if a motion was needed. Chairman Quinn
stated that County Administrator Hammond is just going to have a conversation. County

Administrator Hammond stated that if it is the will of the Board to tell them not to cut our
roads anymore, he will do that and then have a conversation about fixing the ones
previously cut. He discussed concerns regarding the cost of paving roads and damage to
the roads due to cutting; stating he will discuss this matter with City Manager Anderson.
Commissioner McDaniel discussed his concerns; requesting that staff find out who told
the operator to cut the road. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McDaniel regarding who will
be paying for the repairs, County Administrator Hammond stated that he does not intend
to pay for it; that he will ask the City (of Port St. Joe) to fix what they cut.
County Attorney Novak reported that the BCC (Waste Solutions, LLC) agreement (Bid

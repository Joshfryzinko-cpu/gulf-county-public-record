MARCH 25, 2024
WORKSHOP
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met in workshop session with the
following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Phil McCroan,
and Commissioners David C. Rich, Jack Husband, and Patrick Farrell.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Director of Finance & Management
Leo Bebeau, Deputy Clerk Leanna Cooper, County Administrator Michael L. Hammond,
County Assistant Administrator Clay Smallwood, III, Deputy Administrator Kari Summers,
Building Official Brad Bailey, Grant Administrator Kaci Rhodes, Grant Administrator Erin
Kennedy, Office of Management & Budget Director Sherry Herring, County Planner Doug
Crane, and Sheriff Mike Harrison.
Sheriff Harrison called the meeting to order at 5:00 p.m., E.T.
Chairman Quinn led in the Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag.
INDIAN PASS SEPTIC CONVERSION PILOT PROGRAM
County Administrator Hammond discussed providing a presentation by OnSyte
Performance and then accepting questions. Herschel Vinyard, of OnSyte Performance
discussed the septic tanks leaking into the Lagoon. He reported there are currently thirty
Counties within the State that are under an order from Florida Department of
Environmental Protection (D.E.P.) regarding septic tanks, noting Gulf County is not yet
subject to this order, the closest is Wakulla County. Jeff Littlejohn, OnSyte Performance
Vice President then discussed the engineering side of the technology serving Counties
within Florida. He stated property owners have options, as this is not a mandatory
program, noting property owners may keep their current septic tank, pick another system
that meets State requirements, or use the County picked program. Chairman Quinn called
for public comment. Rob Fisher, of Indian Pass appeared before the Board to discuss his
concerns regarding this program and the conflicting information received, reporting that
the Florida Department of Agriculture is stating the Indian Lagoon is better now than in
1981, that the State has approved more than one service provider and component
provider, and residents of Apopka were not happy once OnSyte Performance’s system
was installed. Mr. Littlejohn stated there are various categories of wastewater treatment,
noting this program is being permitted under distributed wastewater system as opposed
to advanced treatment units or a performance base treatment system. He discussed the
difference between distributed and performance base. Mr. Vinyard stated OnSyte is the
only distributed wastewater treatment company that is licensed in the State of Florida.
Chairman Quinn called for additional public comment. Mary Freytag, of Indian Pass
appeared before the Board to discuss the concerns from Apopka being noise, vibration
and what shows up in your yard. Mr. Littlejohn stated there are thirty units deployed in

Apopka and they have not heard any complaints of vibration, noise, or smell. Mr. Littlejohn
reported that with this program, the homeowner will have the old septic tank removed and
a new unit will be installed underground, noting inside the unit is three pumps, a blower
motor, and sensors and beside the home is a computer and cellular device that connects
to the internet. He discussed an operator that monitors each unit continually and the size
of the pumps. Ms. Freytag inquired if medications would disturb the bacteria system. Mr.
Littlejohn stated there have been disruptions of the treatment process, discussing an
incident where a homeowner discarded oil and cooking grease. Discussion followed
regarding various items that should not be flushed or put down the drain. Mr. Littlejohn
noted that medications are not significant enough to disturb the wastewater process even
in an assisted living facility where there is a lot of medication. Ms. Freytag inquired about
the price for customers. Mr. Littlejohn stated the range is from $45.00-$55.00, but OnSyte
does not set rates. Discussion followed regarding pricing and the possible annual
increase. County Administrator Hammond stated in the proposal OnSyte will not be doing
the billing, it will be billed through the Gulf County Water Department and the County
would have complete control of setting the rates, noting the County does not intend to
make money, but to break-even. Upon inquiry by Ms. Freytag, Mr. Littlejohn stated this
program is community lead not vendor lead. County Administrator Hammond reported
there will not be a set price as some property owners will need their drain-fill replaced,
some may need an electric upgrade, or with the size of the property one might be able to
team with their neighbor, noting this program is lock and key and everyone has the same
flat fee. Ms. Freytag discussed concerns regarding the number of users currently using
this system, noting she does not feel comfortable in giving up her septic system.
Commissioner McCroan inquired of the buy-in amount to deploy this program. Mr.
Littlejohn stated there is no buy-in amount. Commissioner McCroan noted this program
is voluntary. Discussion followed regarding the City of Apopka coming on to this septic
system and water improvements after an RV Park in Gilchrist County got online. Ms.
Freytag inquired about power outages and cell phone service. Mr. Littlejohn reported the
system does not use cell towers, but commercial bandwidth and these units are designed
to operate without power like a septic tank. Ms. Freytag inquired if property owners will
be signing an easement. Mr. Littlejohn stated that would be a decision of the County,
discussing the option of a license versus an easement. Elliott Barnett, of Indian Pass
appeared before the Board to inquire who the Board relied on as their technical consultant
for this program. County Administrator Hammond stated he and Assistant County
Administrator/County Engineer Smallwood have been working on this project for two
years and the County has a couple of consultants, noting the Northwest Florida Water
Management District (N.W.F.W.M.D.) and FL D.E.P. has signed off on this program. Mr.
Barnett inquired if there is a study that shows a direct correlation between Indian Pass
septic tanks and red tide or increased nitrates in brown water. Mr. Vinyard stated OnSyte
is regulated just like a wastewater treatment plant and water quality samples are
submitted monthly. Mr. Barnett inquired if there had been a study to see if there are
nitrates in the water wells in Indian Pass versus septic tanks. Commissioner Farrell
discussed information available online, noting the County and City are members of Rural
Water Associations. Discussion followed regarding monitoring and an assessment plan.
Upon inquiry by Mr. Barnett, Mr. Littlejohn reported, depending on the failure, the
response time is from 24 to 96 hours. <Commissioner Farrell left the meeting at 6:08 p.m.,

E.T.> John Schrader, of Indian Pass appeared before the Board to inquire if OnSyte would
be willing to offer an email address for submitting additional questions. Mr. Littlejohn said
yes. Mr. Schrader discussed the study from FL Department Agriculture and asked if
OnSyte was familiar with this study. Mr. Littlejohn stated he is not familiar. Mr. Schrader
discussed his concerns regarding this program and future ‘what ifs’. County Administrator
Hammond stated the County will not be out of it because this system will be owned by
the County and under the County’s permit; OnSyte is just the contractor. Mark Evans, of
Indian Pass appeared before the Board to inquire if the drain-field is bad, if it would be
replaced. County Administrator Hammond stated if you sign up for this program and
whatever is needed to satisfy OnSyte, the County will replace it (using the grant funds
provided by the State for this pilot program). Colleen Reilly, of Indian Pass appeared
before the Board discussed implementing a roll-out date to help residents in the decision
making. Commissioner McCroan inquired if there are any stipulations on the grant
regarding timeframe. County Administrator Hammond stated if the County voted in the
next month to move forward, then the County will be at least six months out from obtaining
all the permits, noting there will be plenty of time to signup, the County has enough funds
for 20% if everyone signed up today, as this is Phase I of a multi-phase project. Rob
Fisher, of Indian Pass appeared before the Board to ask County Attorney Novak of
whether the State could mandate residences with newly septic tanks to convert to this
system. County Attorney Novak discussed Legislative actions in the past, noting it will be
mandated and there will be a roll-out. Discussion followed regarding the State providing
funding. Robyn Rennick, Cottages of Indian Summer Association Manager appeared
before the Board to inquire if the subdivision will need to tie into the common drain-field,
if elected to use this system. Mr. Littlejohn stated OnSyte can accommodate. Mark Evans,
of Indian Pass appeared before the Board to discuss his concerns regarding a lesser
utility bill during his off-season. Mr. Littlejohn stated with the monitoring, OnSyte will know
if residents are not using the units and the units will attempt to stay alive but will starve in
about eight weeks then go into a dormant mode, noting there is no contract that
accommodates a feature of only billing for a few months out of the year (for seasonal
residents). County Administrator Hammond stated he would not recommend seasonal
billing to the Board as the County has a flat fee, there is a maintenance cost, and the
equipment is still being maintained. Discussion followed regarding the wattage, setbacks,
LDRs and CCCL permitting. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Rich, Mr. Littlejohn discussed
the weather proofing of the computer module that is affixed to the side of the house.
Chairman Quinn inquired if Mr. Vinyard would be willing to come back for another
workshop. Mr. Vinyard and Littlejohn said yes. Barbara Radcliff, of Indian Pass appeared
before the Board and inquired about how soon the Board will vote on this matter and if
there be future workshops. County Administrator Hammond stated his hope would be to
start in six months and the County will have further discussions, noting nothing can
happen until the County receives the State permit. Mr. Vinyard noted residents can submit
questions via email in the interim. Ms. Radcliff inquired if the Board is in favor of this
program. Commissioner McCroan stated he would like to see another workshop before
moving forward. Chairman Quinn stated he was in agreement. Discussion followed
regarding a contract, fees, and annual increases. County Administrator Hammond
recommended to the Board that the grant funds not be used for new construction, noting
they can get the new system, but would have to pay for it. He stated the first come, first

serve is to get old septic systems offline not new construction. Rob Fisher, of Indian Pass
appeared before the Board to encourage the audience to appear before the Board to ask
questions. <Commissioner Rich left the meeting at 6:39 p.m., ET.> Mr. Fisher then
provided a recap of today’s discussion and his concerns regarding the number of units
currently being serviced and this program being new technology. <Commissioner Rich
returned to the meeting at 6:39 p.m., ET> He then discussed his concerns regarding
Indian Pass being a remote area.
There being no further discussion, upon motion by Commissioner Husband, second by
Commissioner McCroan, and unanimous vote, the meeting did then adjourn at 6:43 p.m.,
ET.

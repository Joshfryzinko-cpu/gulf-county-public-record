CONSENT AGENDA
Chairman Quinn called for public comment regarding the Consent Agenda. Deborah
Mays, of Gulf County Citizens Coalition of a Healthy Future appeared before the Board
to discuss their concerns regarding the dredging project associated with the Dewberry
proposal (item 16 * task order * pages 58-61) in the Consent Agenda. She requested the
Board perform additional testing, allocating funds for additional core samples to test for
dioxins. Ms. Mays also discussed a letter that was previously sent to the Board <copy not
provided to the Clerk for the record> requesting the Board oppose the well oil dredging in
Wewahitchka, inquiring if a member of the Board will discuss and vote on this matter
during today’s meeting. County Administrator Hammond stated this is not new money
<Chairman Quinn left the meeting at 9:08 a.m., E.T.>, noting this is 2017 appropriation
funding. He reported the County does not have enough money to do the dredging
<Chairman Quinn returned to the meeting at 9:09 a.m., E.T.> with the remaining funds.
County Administrator Hammond noted this proposal (from Dewberry) is to permit the site
behind Highland View to store the spoil. Following discussion, Chairman Quinn discussed
looking into this matter further regarding the well oil dredging proposal in Wewahitchka.
County Administrator Hammond noted Calhoun County is in favor of this oil dredging
project. County Administrator Hammond discussed contacting Calhoun County to inquire
about the County’s pros and cons and report back to the Board. Ms. Mays reported that
Franklin County is opposed and submitted a letter in opposition. Chairman Quinn directed

County Administrator Hammond to contact Franklin County as well for their input.
Chairman Quinn called for additional public comment regarding the Consent Agenda.
John Ehrman, of Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to discuss meeting with
D.E.P. (Department of Environmental Protection) to discuss concerns regarding the core
samples that were taken from the sediment for this dredging project (referring to
Dewberry’s task order on item #16). He noted D.E.P. would be interested in obtaining the
results of the additional sample testing if the County completes them. Mr. Ehrman
requested that the Board make sure the County knows what toxin elements are in the
spoils before dredging. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McCroan regarding D.E.P.’s
interest in any results and whether they have funding for additional test samples, Assistant
County Administrator Smallwood noted that D.E.P. has not reached out to the County
requesting additional sampling but if they do, he will ask them for funding. Chairman
Quinn noted that he is not opposed to the additional core sample testing if the County
could find an agency to fund the testing. He then called for additional public comment.
There were no additional public comment. Commissioner Husband requested to pull
pages 19-21 (item 3 * agreement with Apalachicola Riverkeepers) for discussion, noting
he will also need to abstain due to item #16; as Dewberry is one of his clients. He then
discussed his concerns regarding the letter requesting the Board to enter into an
agreement with the Apalachicola Riverkeepers, noting the County does not own Lister
Landing, only leasing this parcel. County Administrator Hammond stated this location will
not be at the landing, but to the left side toward the River Ditch, facing the river. He noted
the County technically does not own it but hopes to close on this property soon and, if
needed prior to the County taking ownership, then the County will execute an amended
lease agreement with the two current landowners. Discussion followed regarding details
of the agreement, noting the County will benefit by receiving 20,000 yards of sand.
Chairman Quinn called for a motion to accept the Consent Agenda as printed with
Commissioner Husband abstaining from item 16. Commissioner Farrell so moved.
Commissioner Rich seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment.
Upon request for clarification by Deputy Clerk Cooper, Commissioner Husband stated
pages 19-21 were put back into the Consent Agenda for approval. Chairman Quinn called
for public comment. There being none, the motion passed 4-0, with Commissioner
Husband abstaining <FORM 8B on file with Clerk>, as follows:

ADMINISTRATIVE UPDATE & REPORT
STORMWATER UPDATE
From the Administrative Update and Report Packet <copy provided to the Clerk for the
record>, Public Works Director Cothran provided a stormwater update to the Board. He
discussed the issue with beavers on the North end of the County, along with the land
change from timber to cattle farms. Public Works Director Cothran reported there still is
ditches clogged due to Hurricane Michael which are not County owned property nor the
County’s jurisdiction, noting staff is working to obtain limited maintenance agreements

with property owners. Commissioner Rich discussed touring the ditches at Five Acre
Farms, noting the County does need to ask Deseret Ranches if they will clean out several
miles of their ditch behind Five Acre Farms. Commissioner Husband discussed the recent
rainfall being a 100-year event. Commissioner Rich stated the size of pipes (installed) in
the past may not be sufficient moving forward due to the recent land use changes,
development, and change of landscapes since the hurricane.
FWC UPDATE – OYSTER HARVEST
County Attorney Novak discussed the timeline regarding the ban on oyster harvesting in
the Apalachicola Bay and Indian Lagoon, discussing the lawsuit filed by the County
regarding of the closure of Indian Lagoon to oyster harvest. He reported that there is 30
million dollars approved for a pilot program in the Apalachicola Bay to study the impacts
and begin to rebuild the oyster habitat. County Attorney Novak stated this is the fourth
year of a five-year ban in the Apalachicola Bay and Indian Lagoon, noting through this
pilot program there are over seventy sites throughout the Apalachicola Bay for monitoring
the recovery of the oyster population, but there are no pilot programs in Gulf County
(Indian Lagoon). He discussed working toward getting the Indian Lagoon removed from
the oyster ban while FWC’s (Fish & Wildlife Commission) only focus is the Apalachicola
Bay. County Attorney Novak stated with Board approval, staff will get on the Commission’s
agenda to present Gulf County’s request to remove Indian Lagoon from the court system
and the ban. Commissioner McCroan inquired when and what time the Commission
meeting will occur. County Attorney Novak stated oysters are on their September agenda.
Commissioner Farrell discussed his concerns regarding the need to be removed from this
ban.
CAPITAL IMPROVEMENT SCHEDULE
Grant Administrator Rhodes presented pages 1-5 of the admin packet to the Board for

hearing.
PURCHASE REQUEST – BUCKET TRUCK / GULF COAST ELECTRIC COOP
County Administrator Hammond referenced page 6 of the packet, requesting to purchase
a bucket truck from Gulf Coast Electric Cooperative. Mosquito Control Director Horton
reported the County reached an agreement with Gulf Coast Electrical to purchase a
bucket truck, in the amount of $25,000.00 (to be paid from Capital Projects Fund). Upon
inquiry by Commissioner Husband about the County’s purpose or use for this equipment,
Mosquito Control Director Horton discussed the reach capability of this bucket truck.
County Administrator Hammond stated staff can cut palm fronds, use it at the golf course,
trim trees, do a lot of things with the reach, and get on roofs. Chairman Quinn called for
a motion to move forward with purchasing this bucket truck. Commissioner Rich so
moved. Commissioner Husband seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. There been no public comment, the motion passed unanimously.

SRF EXTENSION REQUEST
OMB Director Herring discussed page 7, a memorandum requesting the Board ratify the
SRF loan agreement extension request, noting the County submitted an extension
request due to some of the close-out work that was not able to be completed before this
agreement expired. She stated a six-month extension is offered; the new end date would
be October 2024. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to move forward. Commissioner
McCroan motioned. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called
for public comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously.
LHAP CHANGES
County Administrator Hammond stated page 8 is regarding LHAP Changes, noting the
Board granted him authority to approve this but he wanted to make the Board aware of
the first line change. He stated the asset requirement change adds a non-homestead

noted all of these elements that are in for these changes are due to situations staff has
encountered. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to make these changes. Commissioner

AGREEMENT ACCEPTANCE – NWF HEALTH NETWORK
Referencing page 7, OMB Director Herring reported that staff received an agreement
regarding the opioid litigation, noting this is the first year of funding which is allowable to
be rolled over. She requested Board approval to accept this agreement. Chairman Quinn
inquired about the County’s use for this funding. OMB Director Herring stated the intent
is to help purchase an ambulance for Gulf County E.M.S., noting the dollar amount will
not cover the entire purchase but the plan is to use this year’s funding and year two and
year three until the County can pay themselves back. Chairman Quinn called for a motion
to accept this agreement. Commissioner Farrell so moved. Commissioner McCroan
seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being none, the
motion passed unanimously.

BEACH ORDINANCE – BLOCKING BEACH ACCESS
Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns regarding the public blocking beach
access, noting he has received a lot of complaints. He stated the public will block the
shoreline where vehicles cannot drive on the beach. Commissioner McCroan also
reported he received calls regarding people coming out of the houses videoing drivers as
they enter the beach and try to intimidate the drivers. County Administrator Hammond
reported signs have been ordered and T.D.C. has posted, but the County will need an
Ordinance change to provide law enforcement the opportunity to give a warning, then
fine. He discussed the need for access not just for driving on the beach but access for
emergency vehicles. County Administrator Hammond stated if the Board has no
objection, then staff will advertise to hold a public hearing at the next Board meeting.
Commissioner McCroan had no objections. County Administrator Hammond requested a
motion to advertise for an Ordinance change. Commissioner McCroan made the motion.
Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment.
John Ehrman, of Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to inquire for clarification that
the County already had an existing Ordinance that addressed moving of tents from the
beach at sunset to sunrise. County Administrator Hammond stated this matter is different
from the Leave No Trace (Ordinance #2016-01) as it is part of the Beach Driving

Ordinance (#2023-11) that prohibits people blocking access, meaning some are putting
their chairs from the edge of the dune to the water to keep people from crossing. Mr.
Ehrman stated there is still a problem with tents and chairs being left on the beach
overnight. He inquired why this is not being enforced, noting in Barrier Dunes it is a
problem. County Administrator Hammond stated it is being enforced, noting the way the
Ordinance (#2016-01) is written, if you are there, then you can have it overnight, it’s just
unattended items that are to be removed. He discussed Barrier Dunes density, noting
there are 199 units and with a narrow beach, space is limited. Chairman Quinn called for
any additional public comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously.

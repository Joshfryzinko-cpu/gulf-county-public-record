STATUS REPORT – MK RANCH PROJECT
Billy Webster, of Ducks Unlimited, Inc. appeared before the Board with Jon Creamer of
FWC. Mr. Creamer introduced Mr. Webster, noting FWC partners with Ducks Unlimited
on various projects including the MK Ranch Project. Mr. Webster then discussed the
history and restoration project at MK Ranch using a presentation slideshow <not provided
to the Clerk for the record>, noting the property is in Howard Creek. Upon motion by
Commissioner Farrell, second by Commissioner Husband, and unanimous 3-0 vote, the
Board extended Mr. Webster’s time to speak. He stated the restoration project is to allow
the water from the Apalachicola River to flow across the property toward the Lake Wimico
area then back through the Jackson River. Mr. Webster reported that NFWF (National
Fish & Wildlife Foundation) has provided the grant funds for this restoration project, noting
the bid was bid out in 2023 and construction began in 2024. He reported that the Sauls
Creek Road will remain open to the public, noting the three low water crossings will be
upgraded and a fourth will be installed. Commissioner Farrell inquired of the current
access to the property and if the property would be gated. Mr. Creamer reported Sauls
Creek Road will terminate where it terminates currently, will remain open, and the public
will be able to access that portion of the land, noting a section will be taken out. Discussion
followed regarding the water level, placing trees in ditches, and blocking ditches.
Commissioner Farrell discussed over 500 signatures to a petition <not provided to the
Clerk for the record> to keep Sauls Creek Road open all the way through Browns Creek,
noting from today’s presentation the road will remain open, although they will not be able
to access some of the ditches (which was used by boaters to access the river), but are
able to access the land on both sides of the road if the land is not too wet. Discussion
followed regarding the water flow, low water crossings, and the projected land smoothing.
County Administrator Hammond discussed the negotiations between FWC and the
County regarding Sauls Creek Road, stating that the County has not received a
development order for this project nor any project plans. He discussed his concerns
regarding this project and hunting on Sauls Creek Road, noting the County has not
blessed filling ditches and putting debris in ditches. County Administrator Hammond
stated the County did negotiate with FWC in good faith to install box culverts and improve
the road, noting the low water crossings save the road from being blown out with the river
floods. He stated that if they want to place another low water crossing in on the County’s
portion of the road, then this needs to be discussed further for County approval, noting
anything that improves access to the public he would be good with. Chairman Quinn
called for public comment. Bradford Williams, of Howard Creek appeared before the
Board to discuss his concerns regarding this restoration project, noting he is not in support
of this project. George “Buddy” Cooper, of Wewahitchka appeared before the Board to
discuss his concerns regarding tupelo trees being removed, ditches being filled with
orange clay, and cutting off access to dog hunters by boat. Chairman Quinn called for
additional public comment. There was no further public comment. County Administrator
Hammond requested that Mr. Webster provide the County with accurate information. Mr.
Webster then pointed on the map to the information requested regarding the restoration
project. County Administrator Hammond noted the negotiations regarding Sauls Creek
Road is dead as the County and FWC could not agree, requesting Mr. Webster provide a
proposal regarding the lower water crossing for the Board to vote on as this is a County
road. He discussed the concerns of the community regarding access to hunting and the

County’s concern about what it is going to do to the County’s road (Sauls Creek Road)
once the ditches are blocked. Catherine Ricketts, of Port St. Joe appeared before the
Board to discuss her concerns regarding the water flow at MK Ranch, noting her support
of this restoration project. Debrah Mays, of Indian Pass discuss her concerns regarding
the lack of information provided regarding this project, asking the Board to provide a public
information session. Commissioner Husband discussed his concerns regarding the
County not receiving a development order, noting this is not a County project. Robert
Branch, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to discuss the conditions of Sauls
Creek Road and concerns of having public access to this County road. Mr. Webster
discussed the studies done with the hydraulic model in order to obtain the necessary
permits. Randy Pridgeon, of WindMark appeared before the Board to inquire if FWC could
go back on this project and take into account the concerns of the public regarding hunting
and fishing. Mr. Creamer stated access and hunting rights of the property are not being
taken away. Mr. Pridgeon stated his request is not about taking it away but the way this
project is going to affect the hunting and fishing, requesting if there is a way through the
engineering the property that could be preserved verses flooding this area to enable
access for hunting. Mr. Creamer reported hunters can access the river from Browns
Landing, noting that not blocking the lower ditches will change the hydrology model.
Following discussion, Chirsty McElroy, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to
discuss her concerns regarding this restoration project, requesting FWC share the project
data and information to the public.
STATUS REPORT – CHIPOLA PROJECT
Ken Jones, Consultant for the Apalachicola Riverkeepers appeared before the Board to
provide a presentation <copy not provided to the Clerk for the record> update on the
Spider’s Cut Slough Restoration Project. He noted that a meeting was held with
concerned citizens regarding this project and as a result, he was asked to make a
presentation to the Board by Commissioner Rich. Mr. Jones reported that this is a NFWF
project, noting these funds are private and the work will be performed on private lands.
He discussed the slough restoration project at Douglas Slough and Spider’s Cut, noting
the goal is to reduce the trend of the loss of the tupelo and other swamp species. Mr.
Jones stated the Chipola River will not be impacted by this restoration project. He
discussed the dredged sand will possibly be used by the County as fill and building
material, noting if the County does not want the sand, then it will be deposited on FWC
property a few miles up from Lister Landing. Mr. Jones discussed the benefits of this
restoration project. Commissioner Husband inquired about where the FWC disposal site
is located. Mr. Jones stated the location is off of CR 381 (Dalkeith Road), a mile east of
Lister Landing, which is an old borrow pit. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Husband
regarding the means of transportation of the sand removal, Mr. Jones reported the means
will be left open for the contractor, noting it will be a small amount of material with each
movement. He discussed a few options available regarding the sand removal process.
Following discussion, County Administrator Hammond inquired whether this would be a
one-time dredge. Mr. Jones stated that it is anticipated once all the work is complete, the
slough will naturally remain open. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Ronald
Mayhann, of Wewahitchka appeared before the Board to discuss his concerns regarding
this restoration project, asking the Board to stop this project. He reported that a committee

was formed a few weeks ago consisting of 300 members, Chipola River Conservation

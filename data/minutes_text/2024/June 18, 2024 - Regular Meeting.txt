POLICY CHANGE – LAND DEVELOPMENT REGULATIONS
Building Official Bailey presented page 1 of the Administrative Update and Report Packet
<copy of the packet was provided to the Clerk for the record>, recommending the Board
change the height limit for building construction from its current 60 feet to 50 feet, noting
it would keep construction to three habitable floors with a parking slab underneath.
Building Official Bailey then presented Chairman Quinn a revised recommendation <copy
provided to the Clerk for the record>, noting the change is to add a case-by-case hardship
maximum floor area height of 27 foot for the variance process. Commissioner Husband
discussed adding the hardship in the policy change, noting this would be by variance only.
Following discussion, Commissioner McCroan inquired whether this change is exempt
from industrial. County Administrator Hammond stated industrial is exempt. Chairman
Quinn then called for a motion to move forward. Commissioner McCroan so moved.
Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment.
There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously. County Administrator
Hammond noted this is just a policy change and will be enforced starting July 1, 2024. He
stated the next time the LDR (Land Development Regulations) is updated this addendum
will be included.

INCENTIVE PAY – VOLUNTEER FIREFIGHTERS
Fire Coordinator Herring then discussed incentive pay for volunteer firefighters, noting
there are 120 volunteers Countywide. He presented a proposal to the Board for
consideration, found on page 7 of the packet. Upon direction of Chairman Quinn, Fire
Coordinator Herring discussed the importance of the volunteer firefighters to obtain
certification in Firefighter I and Firefighter II. After discussion, Commissioner Husband
made a motion to eliminate the impact fee study and do this in the interim, rescinding that
and approving this. Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn, County Attorney Novak stated
Commissioner Husband’s motion would be to simply withdraw the resolution that the
Board passed last month to start the process, the study, and the fees that are required by
law to start the process for that impact fee and the second component would be to
recommend this policy change. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion.
Commissioner McCroan asked for clarification on the motion and Commissioner Farrell
stated the motion is just to rescind. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McCroan,
Commissioner Husband acknowledged the motion is to rescind. Chairman Quinn called
for public comment. Randy Pridgeon, of WindMark Beach appeared before the Board to
discuss this proposal, noting the Board needs to look at providing a stipend to the fire
chiefs. Chairman Quinn called for additional public comment. There being no further
public comment, the motion passed unanimously. Commissioner Husband then motioned
to approve Fire Coordinator Herring’s proposal. Commissioner Farrell seconded the
motion for discussion. After discussion regarding being paid quarterly and internal
training, County Administrator Hammond requested that the start date of July 1, 2024, be
included in the motion. Following discussion, Chairman Quinn inquired if Commissioner
Husband had any objection to adding the start date of July 1, 2024, to his motion.
Commissioner Husband agreed to include in his motion the state date of July 1, 2024,
and the first pay date to be October 1, 2024. Fire Coordinator Herring discussed including
a clause that states volunteer firefighter needs to be in good standing with the County,
meaning they are following policy and procedure nor a problem for their chief. County
Administrator Hammond discussed the stipend upon receiving State certification and the
34 volunteers currently eligible can be paid in the next few weeks. Commissioner

Husband inquired if the Board had any objection to allowing the fire chief and Fire
Coordinator Herring to agree if the volunteer firefighter is in good standing. The Board
had no objection. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Christy McElroy, of Port St.
Joe appeared before the Board to discuss a 911 call regarding a tree on a powerline next
door to her home. She stated fire departments need volunteers that are willing to listen
and investigate. Chairman Quinn called for additional public comment. There being none,
the motion passed unanimously. Fire Coordinator Herring noted the incident Ms. McElroy
discussed was in the city limits of Port St. Joe.

QUASI-JUDICIAL HEARING – REVIEW & CONSIDERATION OF P.D.R.B.
APPLICATIONS
PLANNED DEVELOPMENT PROJECT – PTW, LLC
Planner Crane presented a planned development project (Salt Pines) with PTW, LLC *
Parcels ID #03019-000R and #03040-002R for a multi-family residential, recreational and
open space. Chairman Quinn called anyone wishing to speak on this issue to step
forward. Clay Murphy, of Southeastern Consulting Engineers, Inc. stepped forward.
Chairman Quinn inquired if Mr. Murphy had any objections to waiving Quasi-Judicial. Mr.
Murphy had no objection. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to waive. Commissioner
McCroan so moved. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called
for public comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously. Mr. Murphy
discussed the planned development project request. Planner Crane reported the P.D.R.B.
voted 5-0 to approve this planned development project. He also reported that this property
was zoned agriculture, and the Board granted a land use change a few months back,
noting with the current LDR (Land Development Regulations) changes, it would change
the density allocation down to two units per acre. Planner Crane stated one of the two
parcels is located in the City of Port St. Joe and the bigger parcel is in County jurisdiction.
He reported the City of Port St. Joe is allowing a density rate of 15 units per acre on the
City parcel and the County will be two units. Planner Crane discussed the wetlands that
will be preserved and will be part of the planned development project and the future plat.
He stated the developer is asking for a density of 6 units an acre, noting it does not meet
the County specifications, but is under what the City of Port St. Joe will allow. Planner
Crane noted the P.D.R.B. discussed past subdivisions that crossed between the City and
the County, a precedent has been met middle ground, noting there is arazzo directly
across the street from this development and is at 6 units per acre. Commissioner
McCroan discussed his concerns regarding this density request and traffic, noting he is
not in support of anything more than 4 units per acre. County Administrator Hammond

noted when the developer approached the County, stating the City of Port St. Joe had
offered annexation into the City where the developer would get 15 units an acre. He
further discussing his concerns regarding the density within the City of Port St. Joe.
Following discussion, Mr. Murphy stated that if the developer were to fill it, two units an
acre plus the 15 units in the City of Port St. Joe, then it would be 97.76, noting the reason
for 6 units is due to the clustering as the development is sitting at 13 acres of the 37.
Commissioner McCroan stated residents in this neighborhood are not in support of this
development, discussing the decision to keep the density at 4 units per acre. Following
further discussion, Chairman Quinn called for a motion to waive the formal reading.
Commissioner Farrell so moved. The motion failed for a second. Planner Crane then read
the formal reading regarding the planning development project for Parcels ID #03019-
000R and #03040-002R. Commissioner Rich moved to approve. Commissioner Farrell
seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Christy McElroy, of Port
St. Joe appeared before the Board to discuss her concerns regarding this request, asking
the Board to vote no. Chairman Quinn called for additional public comment. There was
no further public comment. The motion then failed 2-2, with Chairman Quinn and
Commissioner McCroan voting no and Commissioner Husband abstaining due to his
business relationship with this project <FORM 8B on file with Clerk>.

PUBLIC HEARING – TOURIST DEVELOPMENT TAX EXTENSION
County Attorney Novak noted this proposed ordinance is for the County T.D.C. 5th penny,
stating the County has gone through all the statutory steps to pass a Resolution and
advertised to hold Public Hearings. He then read the proposed ordinance by title
regarding the 1% tourist development 5th penny extension countywide referendum for
Board consideration, along with the ballot language. Chairman Quinn opened the Public
Hearing, calling for public comment. Christy McElroy, of Port St. Joe appeared before the
Board to discuss the 5th penny being used to purchase the golf club (St. Joseph Bay
Country Club Golf Course), noting in 2014 the 5th penny was to be used for a sports
complex, The Field of Dreams. She inquired if the extension of the 5th penny would be
used for parks and recreation, noting the funds are needed for growth and a sports
complex, The Field of Dreams. Chairman Quinn noted if The Field of Dreams is built, he
is sure some of the money will go towards it but will also go towards all the parks within
the County. County Attorney Novak reported historically the members of the Board made
the decision, but the law has changed, and the Board must now ask the public through a
referendum. Chairman Quinn called for any additional public comment. There being none,
the motion passed unanimously, as follows:

POLICY – COUNTY FIRE DEPARTMENT
Lisa Pridgeon, of WindMark appeared before the Board to inquire about responding to
fire calls, noting that she is a volunteer firefighter for Beaches Fire Department. She
discussed a recent fire call on the Cape (Simmons Bayou brush fire behind Hunt’s Oyster
Bar) responding to assist as needed, having arrived on scene in her personal vehicle.
Mrs. Pridgeon stated that after the call she was notified by her fire chief that firefighters
are only allowed to assist in fire calls that they have been dispatched to. County
Administrator Hammond stated that Fire Coordinator Herring can answer Mrs. Pridgeon’s
questions, noting there are reasons this rule is in place, one being insurance. Fire
Coordinator Herring appeared before the Board to state it is County policy to not self-
deploy and part of the reason is, even though you are certified, if you go in your POV
(personal owned vehicle) or you are not paged, then you would not be covered under the
County’s insurance. He noted if you get in a car accident or directing traffic and get run-
over, then there is no line of death benefit to your family. County Administrator Hammond
discussed an incident in the past where a firefighter from another department filed a claim.

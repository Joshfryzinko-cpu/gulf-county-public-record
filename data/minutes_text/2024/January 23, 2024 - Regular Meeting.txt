BEAR ACTIVITY
Jon Creamer, of FL Fish & Wildlife Commission appeared before the Board to discuss the
bear activity in Gulf County, noting FWC’s message has always been trash management
but transitioning the focus to public safety. He reported traps have been placed at several
bus stops in addition to a trapping event in town (Port St. Joe). Mr. Creamer noted the
bears are currently denned-up and will be coming back out in March, stating FWC’s
process moving forward will first be public safety followed by education and trash
management. Commissioner Rich thanked Mr. Creamer for their efforts, inquiring how the
Board should report problem bears in neighborhoods that need to be removed. Mr.
Creamer discussed calling the FWC number or the 1-800 hotline. Commissioner Rich
discussed key areas of first focus. Following discussion regarding working with the
County on these key areas, County Administrator Hammond asked Mr. Creamer to
explain the difference between a nuisance bear and a bear coming inside someone’s yard
or garage. Mr. Creamer stated that when bears start to enter higher level structures where
people are, conflict with a bear in a home or carport, then FWC will take a higher level of
action and the bear will be euthanized, but a nuisance bear is those tipping over a trash
can, or coming through town at which these bears will be relocated. He discussed talking
with the community about what they want to see, noting FWC’s focus is conservation of
wildlife and protecting that wildlife, but also striking a balance between wildlife and public
safety. Following discussion regarding providing future update reports, County
Administrator Hammond discussed enforcing code violations to vacation rental
companies regarding trash.
AFFORDABLE HOUSING – KNOWLES AVENUE
E.D.C. Director McKnight appeared before the Board to discuss the need for affordable
housing, presenting a proposal to the Board to consider regarding single-family homes
for moderate income which gives priority to nurses, teachers, police officers, first
responders, city and county employees and other positions to be identified. He discussed
the County creating a community land trust, called Gulf County Community Land Trust,
which would ensure the houses will be affordable forever, noting the purchaser/owner of
the house would not own the land but would own the house. He stated the house could
not be leased and could only be sold for the increase of the consumer price index (CPI),
reporting the first proposed location will be located on Knowles Avenue (Courthouse
Complex) consisting of eleven (11) lots. E.D.C. Director McKnight requested Board
approval to seek public input, create the land trust documents, and state the intent to
designate Knowles Avenue the property for affordable housing. Commissioner Husband

inquired whether this location was in a flood zone and if the County needed to get a LOMA
(letter of map amendment). Following discussion, Chairman Quinn called for a motion to
move forward. Commissioner McCroan so moved. Commissioner Rich seconded the
motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being none, the motion passed
unanimously.
E.D.C. – PROJECT SAFETY
E.D.C. Director McKnight then discussed an E.D.C. project called Project Safety, noting
this business is an existing company that makes safety-related clothing equipment and
presently contracts with the Department of Defense and law enforcement agencies. He
reported this company desires to expand operations to this part of Florida, noting Gulf
County is at the top of a short list under consideration for Project Safety. E.D.C. Director
McKnight reported Project Safety will create 200 jobs initially with the potential to expand
to 300 jobs, requiring 50,000 square foot building, and 20 acres of land for future
expansion. He requested the Board to consider: 1) Gulf County providing 20 acres of
land north of Port St. Joe on U.S. Highway 71 either the old L&P (Louisiana-Pacific) site
or land adjacent to the County Transfer Station (Five Point Landfill); 2) a 50,000 square
foot facility estimated to cost $15 million using Triumph grant funds to build the facility at
65% of the cost and Gulf County to bond the 35% balance; 3) Project Safety will pay the
bond debt service, including interest by leasing the facility for 20 years; and 4) at the end
of the lease term, Gulf County will agree to sale the property to Project Safety for an
estimate of $200,000.00. County Administrator Hammond discussed the preferred site
would be the L&P (Louisiana-Pacific) site and the need to negotiate with St. Joe Company
on the purchase, noting the County will commit to: 1) providing the land whether County
owned or County purchased from St. Joe Company; 2) request the amount of money from
Triumph, 65% portion; and 3) if all falls into place the County would borrow the money
and lease back to Project Safety for the remaining balance of approximately $6-7 million.
Chairman Quinn called for a motion to move forward. Commissioner Farrell so moved.
Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously.

PILOT PROJECT – WATER MANAGEMENT SEPTIC / INDIAN PASS
County Administrator Hammond discussed a proposed pilot project beginning at Indian
Pass that will be a riverine spring protection for areas beyond the scope of public sewer,
noting the County has received $1.6 million from (Northwest FL) Water Management to
start this pilot project. To get started, he reported, the County will need a permit, noting
the County has a willing applicant that will do it at no cost. County Attorney Novak reported
that On Site Performance is the consultant and contractor, noting this company is the only
licensed consultant permitted in the State of Florida to run this pilot program. He stated
staff needs approval from the Board to authorize the Chairman and County staff to sign
off on the initial permit and bring On Site Performance on board to run the pilot program.
County Attorney Novak noted staff will bring an agreement back at the next regular
meeting with a recommendation to allow them to run this program out in Indian Pass,
noting staff has initially vetted this company as a sole-source provider. Upon inquiry by
Commissioner McCroan, County Administrator Hammond discussed the pilot project,
noting this is a voluntary program to get existing septic systems off septic tanks and into
this new on-site treatment system. He stated the conversion would be at no cost to the
person and the goal would be to get everyone at Indian Pass on this computerized
system, noting new construction would have to pay to get online. Upon inquiry by

Commissioner Husband regarding the consultant as a sole-source provider, County
Administrator Hammond stated if there are others then staff will follow the normal
procurement policy procedure and advertise it for thirty (30) days. Following discussion,
Commissioner Farrell moved to approve. Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment, the motion
passed unanimously.

PUBLIC HEARINGS – RESTAURANT PARKING REVIEW
Planner Crane discussed receiving requests to review and evaluate the County’s parking
requirements for restaurants, noting that Gulf County is grossly out-of-line compared to
surrounding communities. He reported the TAC (Technical Advisory Committee) have
recommended changing to one (1) parking space per four (4) seats or one (1) space per
60 square feet of floor space devoted to dining or assembly, whichever resulted in the
greater number plus one (1) space per five (5) employees on the larger shift. Planner
Crane stated that after P.D.R.B. review, P.D.R.B. recommends the last two (2) of the 60
square feet of the assembly area and one (1) per five (5) employees be simplified to gross
square footage. Following discussion, Planner Crane reported the recommendation to the
Board would be one (1) parking space per four (4) seats or one (1) space per 100 square
feet of gross floor area, whichever resulted in a greater number. Discussion followed
regarding the current parking requirements for Gulf County compared to surrounding
Counties. Commissioner Husband stated he is in support of the P.D.R.B.
recommendation, requesting that it be added for existing buildings in the County that are
being converted into a restaurant that the same parking requirements be applied as it
would be for a new restaurant application and DO (development order) application. Upon
discussion by Planner Crane, County Administrator Hammond stated the parking
requirements would be caught by the change of use process. Planner Crane stated if the
Board has no objection, then the process can begin to amend the LDR (Land
Development Regulations) through a proposed ordinance. Commissioner Farrell moved
to approve. Commissioner Husband seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for
public comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously.

QUASI-JUDICIAL HEARING – REVIEW & CONSIDERATION P.D.R.B. APPLICATIONS
VARIANCE REQUEST – INDIAN PASS RESORTS, LLC
Planner Crane presented a variance request from Indian Pass Resorts, LLC * Parcel ID
#03188-000R located at 2817 Indian Pass Road (Indian Pass Campground) for a
variance of the Gulf County LDR (Land Development Regulations) Article 3 Section
3.01.02 to allow additional RV (recreational vehicles) sites to be added to an existing park
in a VE Flood Zone. He discussed receiving an application for a development order of
Indian Pass Campground for some upgrades, including total renovation, redevelopment
of sites, beach renourishment, and large improvements. Planner Crane reported the
development order was reviewed by TAC (Technical Advisory Committee) and concerns
were provided to the developer, noting the Gulf County LDR does not allow RVs in a VE
Flood Zone and the entire park is in a VE Flood Zone. He stated Florida Statute 513
supersedes County Ordinances due to this park (Indian Pass Campground) preexisted
the County’s LDR, but the County’s understanding is that it is only for the number of sites

PROPOSED FIRE TAX
Commissioner Husband requested an update from County Attorney Novak regarding the
potential fire tax. County Attorney Novak discussed working with the FL Department of
Revenue to explore what vehicle can be used for fire assessment. He noted there are
other Municipalities and Counties that have established an assessment that was placed
on a referendum. County Attorney Novak reported the vehicle for Gulf County is the T.D.C.
tax which has been maxed out and will be placed on a referendum in the fall (2024) on
the general election ballot. He discussed the discretionary tax which half of that one penny
is pledged to Scared Heart and the other is for the County’s road bond obligations. County
Attorney Novak stated for the County to go out and look at other discretionary taxes it
would be a consideration for this Board to put a discretionary tax on the referendum in
the general election ballot. He noted there is not an opportunity for the County to put a
flat dollar surcharge on a rental independent of the County, therefore sales tax would be
the vehicle in which the County could come up with the fire protection or the fire
assessment, which would be a vote of the public. County Attorney Novak stated this would

be a consideration of the Board and if the Board wants to move forward, then he will
present a legal memorandum, pass a resolution through ordinance form and then place
it on the ballot election in the fall. County Attorney Novak noted that the School Board has
passed a resolution, and this school tax will be placed on the ballot as well as the T.D.C.
tax. Commissioner Husband discussed in the next Legislative session there will be a bill
proposed for the ability of a vehicle to use, requesting County Attorney Novak review this
further.

PIPING WATER LINE – JONES HOMESTEAD & RAILROAD
Upon inquiry by Commissioner Hammond regarding the water line, Assistant County
Administrator Smallwood confirmed the contractor is starting in two places to install the
water line, one area in Jones Homestead and the other will start near the railroad strip.
County Administrator Hammond stated the piping is going down Jones Homestead Road
to the plant, up U.S. Highway 98 to Twine Drive, down Twine Drive to Madison Street,
and cross the railroad tracks to U.S. Highway 71 close to the L & P (Louisiana Pacific)
site and then to White City. Commissioner McCroan discussed his concern regarding the
paving project at Madison Street in Oak Grove. Assistant County Administrator
Smallwood noted the pipe work will be on the opposite side of the road, but there will be
five or six that will be affected.
MEETING WITH D.E.M.
County Administrator Hammond reported that Good Friday will be the County’s third
makeup call with D.E.M., stating he hopes the County will receive a good answer.

CAPITAL PLANNING
Terry O’Neal, of Panama City appeared before the Board to thank Assistant County
Administrator Smallwood for last month’s presentation and to County staff for the work
that is being done regarding capital planning and team maintenance. Upon motion by
Commissioner Farrell, second by Commissioner McCroan, and unanimous vote, the
Board extended Mr. O’Neal’s time to speak. Mr. O’Neal continued to discuss his
appreciation for the County’s use of funds for capital projects and future growth.

by Commissioner Farrell, and unanimous vote, the meeting did then adjourn at 10:42

REJECT BID #2324-38 – ST. JOSEPH PENINSULA BEACH RENOURISHMENT
Referring to page 16, Assistant County Administrator Smallwood recommended the
Board reject Bid #2324-38 for the St. Joseph Peninsula Beach Renourishment (erosion
control project) due to the budget. County Administrator Hammond reported staff has
requested MRD Associates to obtain an extension, noting the time constraints based on
turtle season is too strenuous and is driving the price. Following discussion, he stated
staff is going to ask the Legislature for more money, noting hopefully by summer 2025 the
County will be able to move forward with this project. After discussion by Commissioner
McCroan, County Administrator Hammond discussed it taking the County 12 months to
complete this project, if the County was to haul the sand. Chairman Quinn called for a
motion to reject the bid. Commissioner McCroan so moved. Commissioner Husband
seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Coastal Community
Association President Pat Hardman appeared before the Board inquiring of other options,
discussing her concerns for the residents on Cape San Blas. Upon inquiry by Ms.

Hardman on what citizens can do to help move this project forward, County Administrator
Hammond stated the County needs the time constraints lifted and the Legislature to give
the County more funds for this project. Ms. Hardman discussed her concerns regarding
the timeline and the desire to assist the County in getting this project moving forward.
Chairman Quinn called for any additional public comment. Mariah Thomas, of Cape San
Blas appeared before the Board to discuss her concerns regarding the erosion and
inquired about the number of bids received for this project. County Administrator
Hammond reported this project has gone out for bid twice, noting the second round only
one bid was received. Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns regarding this
area of the beach, noting if this area was not under CBRA (Coastal Barrier Resources
Act), then the Federal Government would renourish this beach after storms or hurricanes.
Discussion followed regarding the project, funding and permits. Pat Hardman, Coastal
Community Association President returned to the Board to discuss the support of the
State Representatives, noting the holdup is at the Federal level and U.S. Fish and Wildlife.
She asked the Board to provide her with information of how much funding the County
needs from the State. Chairman Quinn called for any additional public comment. Kellie
Mills, of Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to discuss her concerns regarding
tourism at the Cape and the need for this project to be completed in a timely manner. After
further discussion, the motion passed unanimously.
SUNRISE/SUNSET
Attorney Jeremy Anderson, representing Sunrise/Sunset Homeowners Associates
appeared before the Board to discuss contacting an engineer for a plan of action and
exploring moving the structures landward. He continued discussion regarding the
estimated cost to move the structures, D.E.P. approval, the Building Official letter and the
commentary, noting the structure is a retaining wall by D.E.P. standards, not a seawall.
Attorney Anderson stated under the Flood Plain Ordinance it is permitted to install a
retaining wall during construction. Upon motion by Commissioner McCroan, second by
Commissioner Farrell, and unanimous vote, the Board extended Attorney Anderson’s
time to speak. Attorney Anderson continued to discuss the definition of a retaining wall,
requesting Board approval for immediate work, and under the prevision of 4.02.04(G)7(a)
(under the Flood Plain Ordinance) to submit a request for an approval to repair the
retaining wall. He again asked the Board for emergency action to repair the retaining wall,
noting D.E.P. requires approval of the County before the wall can be repaired and
requested Board approval to allow the Building Department to review their application for
the restoration and repair of the retaining wall, and have Code Enforcement not purse
action. Attorney Anderson noted that under the County’s provision, the homeowners will
not need a variance and requested Board permission to allow Ms. Womack to discuss
wave action. County Attorney Novak inquired if Attorney Anderson notified the
neighboring property owners to the north. Attorney Anderson discussed reviewing the
letters to see which meeting the neighbors were noticed for. Discussion followed
regarding the notice requirements. County Administrator Hammond discussed the
difference between a seawall and a retaining wall is that one is not below grade and the
other is above grade, reporting the ones the County has permitted north of this property
is at zero or higher and did not permit a seawall because that is below grade (sea level).
He stated the County will give a zero retaining wall, with no problem and the Building

Department will sign off today giving permission to obtain the required D.E.P. permit.
County Administrator Hammond reported the County will not give permission for a
seawall, unless it is voted by the Board, discussing the after-the-fact permit by D.E.P.
Discussion followed regarding the definition of a seawall and the desire to repair the wall
back to the way it was. Chairman Quinn asked Attorney Anderson what he is asking of
the Board. Attorney Anderson asked permission to allow Ms. Womack to appear before
the Board to discuss the wave action. Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns
regarding setting precedent in allowing seawalls. After further discussion, Planner Crane
reported there is no record of the County ever permitting a retaining wall or seawall for
that area. Stephanie Womack, of Cypress Environmental appeared before the Board to
state that from a permitting perspective, seawalls are typically located on a water body
on a mean high-water line and retaining walls are landward. She reported that after the
2019 County renourishment project, there was about 135 feet from the wall to the wet
sand; calling it a retaining wall, not a seawall, noting the D.E.P. permit was for a retaining
wall. Discussion followed regarding the beach erosion. Upon motion by Commissioner
McCroan, second by Commissioner Husband, and unanimous vote, the Board extended
Ms. Womack’s time to speak. Ms. Womack continued to discuss the after-the-fact D.E.P.
permit, noting the HOA was granted authorization by Gulf County for construction of a
retaining wall based on the local state of emergency declaration of September 13, 2010,
and to allow the retaining wall to remain as a permanent structure seaward of the coastal
construction control line. Discussion followed regarding the after-the-fact D.E.P. permit
and subsequent renourishment projects. Attorney Anderson discussed the wave action.
County Administrator Hammond stated the reason the County sent a letter to have the
wall removed was because the County never authorized installation of this seawall.
Discussion followed regarding the definition of a seawall and obtaining a D.E.P. permit to
repair the structure. Chairman Quinn inquired about what the HOA is asking of the Board.
Attorney Anderson noted because the County is still under an emergency order from the
State of Florida, the request is to allow the homeowners (of Sunrise/Sunset) to do
emergency repairs, minimum as necessary, to secure it as is to prevent damage to the
structure and direct the Building Official not to pursue removal, allowing the homeowners
to go through the process under the code, the flood plain provision, for a retaining wall,
seek approval, then take that letter to D.E.P. for approval to make the repair. County
Attorney Novak noted, out of an abundance of caution, the Board wanted to provide
Attorney Anderson with the opportunity to appear before the Board with a plan as soon
as possible, providing a highlight of the discussion at the October 22, 2024 Board meeting
that allowed Attorney Anderson time to meet with his clients (Sunrise/Sunset HOA) and
form a plan to present to the Board at today’s meeting. He reported under this special
meeting today, the County does not have a formal application or what Attorney Anderson
has articulated in his request, noting that Attorney Anderson has professional certified
engineer appear before the Board to offer opinion and there is a structure and process,
and this needs to go through the Quasi-Judicial process. County Attorney Novak
recommended that Attorney Anderson submit what he is requesting now and place the
item on the November 26th agenda, suggesting that Attorney Anderson submit something
in writing to the Planning Department and have a formal hearing. Discussion followed
regarding the wall. Attorney Anderson inquired if there is something that could be done in
an emergency circumstance, due to the meeting being a couple weeks away, asking the

Board to allow placement of geo-bags. Commissioner Husband stated the Board asked
Attorney Anderson to submit a notice of intent to issue and it would have been reviewed
today and discussed the feasibility of moving the house back (landward). He stated D.E.P.
is the determining factor on their findings of fact and the County does not have the ability
to do that here, noting he does not believe the County has the authority to give permission
to put the wall back without something from D.E.P. based on the wording in the last D.E.P.
permit. Attorney Anderson again requested permission of the Board to allow the
homeowners to place geo-bags behind the wall temporarily until a permanent solution
can be determined. Commissioner McCroan noted that at the last Board meeting,
Commissioner Husband stated that the homeowners could place an above grade
retaining wall behind the existing wall without a permit. Commissioner Husband
commented that the Building and Planning Officials said they would permit it as a
temporary fix. Attorney Anderson discussed asking for a temporary solution that would be
put in place more immediate than installing a wall, like the geo-bags. Commissioner
Husband stated to his knowledge, he is not aware of any field permit that needs a building
permit. Attorney Anderson requested the Board to issue an emergency order to direct
staff to, if deemed appropriate, to issue an NOCC (notice of construction consistence) for
trap bags or similar materials to stabilize the wall. Commissioner Rich inquired about what
Ms. Womack’s recommendation is to the Board and County staff. Ms. Womack stated for
a short-term solution, using the trap bags would be a reasonable solution, noting she’s
unsure of how many bags would be needed. Commissioner Rich discussed his concerns
with the property owners trying to save their home and the County assisting in a short-
term solution while they evaluate a permanent solution. Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn,
County Administrator Hammond stated, unless the Board tells staff different, the Building
Department will not issue any permit to repair that wall because it is considered a violation
of the County Comprehensive Plan and LDR (Land Development Regulations). He noted
bags behind the wall, that does nothing to the wall, that protect the property, staff has no
issue, stating all the property owners will need is a field permit from D.E.P. Commissioner
McCroan requested County Administrator Hammond, Assistant County Administrator
Smallwood and Commissioner Husband to give a recommendation regarding the trap
bags. County Administrator Hammond stated as long as it is not repairing the wall and
just shoring up something, then the County does not have to do anything, noting the
homeowners can do that without any approval from the County. Commissioner McCroan
discussed his concerns regarding Attorney Anderson asking for this letter from the
County. Commissioner Husband stated the issue is the County has not been provided
with a plan, noting the Board does not know if the landowners are going to place bags on
the landward side of the wall and jeopardize more of the wall, that could hurt the owners.
County Attorney Novak requested permission of the Board to work with Planner Crane to
narrow the scope and draft a hold harmless letter, bringing this matter before the Board
under a Quasi-Judicial Hearing setting on November 26, 2024. County Attorney Novak
requested a vote of the Board to authorize himself and the Planner to come up with a
limited scope, indemnifying the County in the decision. Commissioner McCroan clarified
this is no repair to the wall. County Attorney Novak concurred, noting it will specifically
state this is no repair to the wall, but to mitigate the erosion. Commissioner McCroan
made the motion. Commissioner Husband seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called
for public comment. Daniel Sieber, of Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to

discuss homeowners of Sunrise/Sunset being allowed to make temporary repairs,
inquiring if the surrounding neighbors will be allowed the same. County Attorney Novak
noted there is nothing being allowed until the end of the month when the Board will hear
the proposal, encouraging the surrounding neighbors to attend this meeting. Discussion
followed regarding the geo-trap bags. Mairah Thomas, of Cape San Blas appeared again
before the Board to discuss the document from MRD Associations regarding the current
stages of the erosion and the projection of further erosion, noting there are no increase
erosion to either side of Sunrise/Sunset condos. Chairman Quinn called for any additional
public comment. There being no further public comment, the motion passed unanimously.

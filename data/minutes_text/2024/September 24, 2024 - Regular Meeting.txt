CONSENT AGENDA
Chairman Quinn called for public comment regarding the Consent Agenda. Debra Mays,
of Jones Homestead appeared before the Board to discuss the missing appendixes
regarding the airport agreement (under item #2, pages 12-27). County Administrator
Hammond stated the survey has not been completed, noting that is why the legal
description was not included. He reported that the site is approximately 1,000 acres that
borders the Gulf County Canal to the west, just off County Road 382 (Industrial Road)
and U.S. Highway 71 to the north and the Dynamite Dock Road on the north side.
Following discussion and upon inquiry by Ms. Mays regarding Appendix C, County
Administrator Hammond stated the County is not planning to build any hangers, noting
that would be for St. Joe Company or whoever hired to run the airport. He reported the
scope of work the County will be a part of is the road, water, sewer, runway, taxiway, and
FBO (Fixed Base Operator). Randy Pridgeon, of WindMark appeared before the Board
to discuss his concerns regarding the economic development agreement, noting this
agreement will tie the County for five years and incur staff time. He requested the Board
table this agreement until the public can hold a meeting. Chairman Quinn called for any
additional public comment regarding the Consent Agenda. There was no further public
comment. Commissioner McCroan discussed supporting economic development and
concerns regarding the proposed airport agreement with St. Joe Company, noting he

would like to table this agreement for further review. Following discussion, County
Attorney Novak discussed the agreement, noting that if either party steps back from this
project, then the agreement goes away. He discussed further items within the agreement
regarding this matter. Discussion followed regarding the mitigation bank and conservation
easement. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McCroan, County Attorney Novak noted the
land will be conveyed to the County and the County will give it back at a nominal amount
to St. Joe Company, St Joe will build the FBO of 3,000 square feet and hangers, but the
County and the Board will still control the site. Upon request by Commissioner Husband,
Chairman Quinn pulled page 36 for discussion. Commissioner Husband asked if there
was an option to transfer these funds to a different fire department (rescind grant from
Florida Department of Agriculture for the purchase of wildland gear). OMB Director
Herring stated no, not on the County side but staff can make that request to the State.

STORM UPDATE
Emergency Management Director Herring appeared before the Board to report Gulf
County is currently under a tropical storm watch, with a prediction that tropical cyclone #9
will develop into a major hurricane before landfall east of Perry, Florida Thursday night.
He discussed his concerns regarding the predicted 5-10 feet storm surge for Indian Pass,
noting the County should experience tropical storm force winds as early as Thursday
morning with 6-8 inches of rain. County Administrator Hammond noted this first
recommendation to the Board is to declare a local state of emergency. County Attorney
Novak read the proposed local state of emergency regarding Tropical Storm Helene aloud
for the Board to consider. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to accept the Local State
of Emergency. Commissioner McCroan so moved. Commissioner Husband seconded the
motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being none, the motion passed
unanimously. County Administrator Hammond then provided the following
recommendations: 1. non-residents must leave the Cape, Indian Pass and Simmons
Bayou, everything from the Y at C-30 to the Franklin County line by 8:00 a.m. E.T.; 2. this
would include all RVs (recreational vehicles) in the same area; and 3. all remaining RVs
throughout the remaining Coastal Corridor should closely monitor for updates. Upon
inquiry by Chairman Quinn of the recommendation for County business, County
Administrator Hammond requested permission from the Board to allow himself and the
Chairman to tentatively close the Courthouse on Thursday. Following discussion, Gulf
County Health Department Director Sarah Quaranta appeared before the Board to
discuss closing clinical operations and possibly the patient clinic schedule, reporting the
Health Department will not stop operating in terms of disaster response. She discussed
the special needs registry and contacting those patients to confirm their needs. County
Administrator Hammond discussed watching the storm surge and working with the
nursing home if those patients need to be evacuated. Commissioner Rich requested that
Emergency Management Director Herring discuss what the public needs to prepare for
in the next 72-hours. Emergency Management Director Herring discussed making the
decision to stay or leave and if staying, he provided a list of supplies needed to survive
when without power for a few weeks. Discussion followed regarding the storm surge
model and the evacuation due to the St. Joe Bay. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to

carry all those recommendations (presented by County Administrator Hammond).
Commissioner McCroan so moved. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment, the motion
passed unanimously. County Administrator Hammond reported staff has ordered a truck
load of water from the State and 10,000 sandbags. He stated the moby mats will be picked
up and the bathrooms at the Cape, Indian Pass and Troy Deal, flags will be down at
Beacon Hill Veterans’ Memorial Park. Discussion followed regarding areas needing
sandbags. County Administrator Hammond reported the water will be distributed at the
County Fire Departments.

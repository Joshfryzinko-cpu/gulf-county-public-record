SEPTEMBER 16, 2024
FINAL BUDGET HEARING
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in special budget session
with the following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Phil
McCroan, and Commissioners David C. Rich, Jack Husband, and Patrick Farrell.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Clerk Rebecca L. Norris, Director
of Finance & Management Leo Bebeau, Deputy Clerk Leanna Cooper, County
Administrator Michael L. Hammond, County Assistant Administrator Clay Smallwood, III,
Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Emergency Management Director/Fire Coordinator
Matt Herring, Gulf County E.M.S. Director Jody Daniels, G.I.S. Coordinator Clay Kennedy,
Grant Administrator Erin Kennedy, Grant Administrator Laura Beach-Armstrong, Human
Resource Director Kaci Rhodes, Office of Management & Budget Director Sherry
Herring, Mosquito Control Director Austin Horton, Public Works Director Mark Cothran,
Assistant Public Works Director Raymond Hart, Sheriff Mike Harrison, and T.D.C.
Executive Director Silvia Williams.
Chairman Quinn called the meeting to order at 5:01 p.m., E.T.
Chairman Quinn then led in the Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag.
2024-2025 BUDGET OVERVIEW
OMB Director Herring stated the tentative aggravate millage rate is 6.2850, noting this is
5.69% above the current year aggregate rolled back rate of 5.9644. She then reported
that each of the Fire Control Districts are at .5 mills and the tentative M.S.T.U. voted debt
service millage rate are, as follows: 1. Gulfside Beachfront at 0.4897; 2. Gulfside Interior
at 0.4059; and 3. 0.3981 for Bayside, noting these are the maximum millages that the
Board may adopt. OMB Director Herring stated a roll call vote is required at this Hearing
and will be part of the TRIM compliance packet. She provided primary reasons for the ad
valorem tax revenue increase are, as follows: 1. Sheriff’s Office; 2. other four
Constitutional Officers; 3. E.M.S.; 4. insurance cost; 5. Jail; 6. firefighter incentives; 7.
park renovations; 8. City of Port St. Joe Summer Program; 9. PSJ Downtown
Redevelopment; 10. library operations; and 11. pay raises for employees. OMB Director
Herring noted the budget book is posted on the County’s website and was advertised in
the newspaper with the Budget Summary and Notice to Proposed Tax Increase. She
noted on page 4 of the tentative budget book is the balanced totals of each fund and
reminded the Board any increases in expenditures will require an equivalent reduction in
a specified line-item. OMB Director Herring requested approval of the Board for the
following items: 1. net increase of $22,275.00 in General Fund due to cash carry forward
estimates for Emergency Management affecting revenues and transfer out to Capital
Fund; 2. net increase of $29,000.00 for the St. Joseph Fire Control District ($2,000.00 for

St. Joseph Fire Control District, $5,000.00 for Beaches Fire Department, $7,000.00 for
Highland View Fire Department, $9,000.00 for White City Fire Department, and $6,000.00
for South Gulf Fire Department) due to revised carry forward estimates, affecting those
revenues, expenditures and reserves; 3. $9,000.00 increase for Tupelo Fire Control
District for Stone Mill Creek, affecting revenues, expenditures and reserves; 4. Sheriff’s
Office request Cash Carry Forward in the amount of $71,588.00 in T.D.C. Public Safety
Fund of unspent estimates to roll-forward, affecting revenues and expenditures; 5.
increase of $22,275.00 previously discussed also increases the Capital Fund due to the
transfer in; 6. $100,000.00 due to increase Cash Carry Forward estimates on the Water
Fund, affecting revenues and reserves; and 7. make line-time adjustments regarding
elected official salaries, not affecting ad valorem or the total by fund. Chairman Quinn
called for a motion to accept OMB Director Herring recommendation. Commissioner
Farrell so moved. Commissioner Husband seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called
for public comment. Christy McElroy, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to inquire
if this vote is regarding the whole budget or will there be additional discussions. Chairman
Quinn stated there will be more votes to follow. Chairman Quinn called for any additional
public comment. There being no further public comment, the motion passed unanimously.
PUBLIC COMMENT
Chairman Quinn opened the Budget Hearing to the public, calling for public comment.
Robert Branch, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to discuss his concerns
regarding the funds coming into this County, inquiring where the County is finding funds
within the budget to purchase residential property. He provided material regarding
property located on Jones Homestead Road (Parcel ID #03107-001R) for the Board to
review <copy provided to the Clerk for the record>. Discussion followed. Christy McElroy,
of Port St. Joe, representing Panhandle Transparency Partnership, appeared before the
Board to inquire about the budget, as follows: 1. two entries transferred into the Golf
Course; 2. what the professional services are under the (golf) Club that is budgeted over
$100,000.00; 3. whether the County Administrator pulls money for salary from other
departments; 4. where is the revenue from the dirt harvested from the Honeyville Borrow
Pit represented in the budget; 5. why is there a line-item for sports complex and other
parks when the County does not have a Sports Complex; 6. concerns on some of the
County salaries, noting the County Attorney is scheduled to make $400,000.00 this year
and County Commissioners make approximately $71,000.00; 7. budgeted travel expense
for members of the Board. Following discussion regarding funds not used for travel
expenses are transferred to the General Fund to budget next year’s budget, County
Administrator Hammond responded to Ms. McElroy’s inquiries, as follows: 1. the
professional services are the County’s contracted employees at the Golf Course, noting
not full-time nor benefitted employees; 2. the transfers-in, noting there was a green project
last year that was a huge transfer and the rebuild of the structure of irrigation and drainage
is this year. Upon inquiry by Ms. McElroy regarding the expenses to operate the Golf
Course, County Administrator Hammond reported the Club is standing alone, the County
has put no money in it as far as Ad Valorem Tax money from any other source, noting the
membership fee pays for green fees for the members only, the carts are paid separately,
and the greens fees are for non-members. He stated the money the County has put in
has been for the greens and irrigation and for the Club House. County Administrator

Hammond continued to answer Ms. Elroy’s questions, as follows: 3. the County
Commission makes $31,000.00 per year; 4. 10% of his total salary comes from the
Building Department, noting his salary is paid out of multiple budgets. Discussion followed
regarding the delegation of staff departments between County Administrator Hammond
and Assistant County Administrator Smallwood and staffing at the Building Department;
and 5. sand pit money goes into that fund, directing OMB Director Herring to provide the
correct fund. OMB Director Herring stated those operations fall under Fund 123. Ms.
McElory then provided material to Chairman Quinn and Commissioner McCroan to review
<copy provided to the Clerk for the record>. Chairman Quinn called for any additional
public comment. There was no further public comment.
2024-2025 BUDGET OVERVIEW
County Administrator Hammond thanked the Board for lowering taxes for the fourth year
in a row, noting if the Board adopts the presented proposed budget the rate will be set at
5.9 mills. He then recommended to the Board to move forward with the roll call votes and
adoptions. Chairman Quinn inquired if staff was good with the roll-over for the parks’
money. OMB Director Herring noted unless the Board has a change in plan, then staff will
do that end-of-year when the final numbers are posted. She stated it will come back
before the Board for approval as a proposed budget resolution.

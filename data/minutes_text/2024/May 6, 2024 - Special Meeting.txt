RESOLUTION – AIRPORT GRANT
OMB Director Herring presented a proposed resolution regarding the grant agreement for
the County Airport Project <found on page 1 of the Administrative Update and Report
Packet>, reading the proposed resolution by title for the Board to consider. County
Administrator Hammond stated this is current year funding, in the amount of $2.5 million,
noting this is not the latest funding of $1 million which will take effect July 1st. Chairman
Quinn called for a motion to move forward. Commissioner Rich so moved. Commissioner
Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Randy Pidgeon,
of WindMark appeared before the Board to inquire about the Airport, requesting the Board
hold a workshop to discuss the details of this project. County Administrator Hammond
noted this project started four years ago, being a priority after Hurricane Michael, stating
Gulf County is the only County in Northwest Florida that does not have a paved runway.
He discussed the timeline regarding the proposed airport project, noting this project is a
public, private, partnership (P3). Upon inquiry by Mr. Pridgeon, County Administrator
Hammond reported the County will be the conduit for a General Aviation Airport that will
receive the Federal grant, noting the County will own the property but it will be leased to
St. Joe Company. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Christy McElroy, of Port
St. Joe appeared before the Board to discuss her concerns comparing this project to the
building of the Bay County Airport. She discussed putting together an analysis, providing
a copy to the Clerk for the record. Ms. McElroy requested that the Board hold a workshop
to discuss the County’s analysis. Debra Mays, of Jones Homestead appeared before the
Board to discuss her concerns regarding this Airport being a single runway, noting
Franklin County has three runways. She also discussed the desire for the County to

provide a design for the public to view, inquiring if one runway will be enough, whether
there will be a tower and/or hangers, or will St. Joe Company rent to the public. Following
discussion, County Administrator Hammond stated the Airport will be open to the public,
discussing the County previously reaching out to Franklin County on a joint venture for
expansion of their airport but Franklin County was opposed to this idea. He reported the
County is proposing a 6,500 feet runway and would be a backup emergency landing for
Tyndall Air Force Base, noting the issue is cost to add additional runways. Chairman
Quinn called for additional public comment. Robert Branch, of Port St. Joe appeared
before the Board to inquire if Tyndall Federal Air Force Base had given their blessing to
this project. County Administrator Hammond stated he hopes Tyndall will provide funding
and their blessing, noting Tyndall’s stage to comment will come with the FAA (Federal
Aviation Administration) permit. Mr. Branch discussed his concerns regarding the
proposed airport conflicting with the rifle range. County Administrator Hammond stated
the airport site is miles from the rifle range and it will not be affected. Mr. Branch asked
the Board to think about this project before voting. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. There being no further public comment, the motion passed unanimously, as
follows:

POLICY – PROHIBITING EXCESSIVE FORCE NON-VIOLENT DEMONSTRATIONS
Referencing page 4 of the packet, County Administrator Hammond reported that the
Board has no choice since this is a requirement from HUD (Department of Housing and
Urban Development). Chairman Quinn inquired if staff provided a copy to Sheriff Harrison
to review. County Administrator Hammond stated the County does not have the authority
to tell the County Sheriff no, reading the proposed policy for the public. Sheriff Harrison
stated the Sheriff’s Office has a use of force policy that covers all that. Chairman Quinn
called for a motion to move forward with the policy. Following discussion, OMB Director
Herring stated the County already has this policy, noting the change is adding the
definition. She reported that staff has notified HUD that the Gulf County Sheriff’s Office is
its own agency, but this policy is required in order for the County to receive grant funding.
Chairman Quinn again called for a motion for the Board to move forward. Commissioner
Farrell so moved. Commissioner Rich seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for
public comment. There being no public comment, the motion passed 4 to 1, with
Commissioner McCroan voting no.

OIL EXPLORATION DEADLINE
County Administrator Hammond stated it is not on the agenda therefore, the Board cannot
vote, but Mrs. Mays would like to discuss the oil drilling due to it being time critical.
Deborah Mays, of Gulf County Citizens Coalition for a Health Future appeared before the
Board to discuss the oil exploration in the river basin of Apalachicola. She noted the Board
has three weeks past the time that Florida D.E.P. puts a public notice in the Calhoun
County newspaper to respond. Mrs. Mays reported the drilling company (Clearwater) will
be at the Calhoun County Commission meeting tomorrow, noting the Board may want to
send a representative to this meeting. She provided the Board with a newspaper article
from the Tallahassee Democrat regarding quotes from Representative Jason Shoaf and
Senator Corey Simon <copy provided to the Clerk for the record> regarding this subject
matter. County Administrator Hammond reported Calhoun County voted unanimously to
support this project, inquiring why is Calhoun County meeting tomorrow. Mrs. Mays stated
it is a Board meeting but unsure whether they are bringing in Clearwater (drilling
company) or just has Clearwater on the agenda. Commissioner Rich discussed his recent
inquiry regarding whether the City of Port St. Joe had a contingency plan in the event of
a spill or train derailment crossing the Apalachicola River, and if the City of Port St. Joe
had a backup plan for their water supply, voicing concerns about possible contamination.

TRAFFIC – BACK BEACH ROAD
Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns regarding the traffic in District V,
inquiring about the timeframe for D.O.T. (Department of Transportation) to build the new
Back Beach Road. County Administrator Hammond stated the County will be the bad
guys when construction at WindMark reaches 755, noting the governing law states in the
DRI that issuing building permits will have to stop. He reported that under the DRI
WindMark is about 200 homes away from reaching the maximum amount. County
Administrator Hammond noted that the bypass cut-through road coming off the Cape will
help locals, also discussing his concerns regarding the four-lane road that was turned into
a two-lane road coming through town (U.S. Highway 98). He stated he has seen traffic
backed-up from the light to the Methodist Church and the County is not even at peak
season. County Administrator Hammond discussed staff having conversations with
D.O.T. and the Back Beach Road Project is in their court. Commissioner Rich discussed
his concerns regarding a few intersections (Stone Mill Creek and County Road 386) in
Wewahitchka deemed dangerous and with continued traffic will continue to cause
problems. County Administrator Hammond reported the County has begged D.O.T. to
install caution lights at both of these intersections, noting Chipley (D.O.T.) hates caution
lights. Following discussion, Commissioner Husband discussed sending a letter to D.O.T.
voicing their concerns. After further discussion, County Administrator Hammond stated
the priority of the County is to four-lane U.S. Highway 71, or in lieu of four-lane do passing
lanes to alleviate some of the problems. E.D.C. Director Jim McKnight appeared before
the Board to discuss a recent meeting with D.O.T., noting D.O.T. is not going to four-lane
U.S. Highway 71, but a passing lane is a possibility. He noted the County would like to
see the new Back Beach Road, and the Florida Gulf Coast Parkway initially planned.
E.D.C. Director McKnight encouraged the Board to express these needs with State
Legislators. County Administrator Hammond recommended asking the D.O.T. District
Secretary Phillip Gainer to come down to look at these needs. The Board had no
objection. Commissioner Husband requested that if D.O.T. District Secretary Gainer was
unable to attend, then ask if he could send a representative.
There being no further business, upon motion by Commissioner Farrell, second by
Commissioner Rich, and unanimous vote, the meeting did then adjourn at 4:53 p.m., E.T.

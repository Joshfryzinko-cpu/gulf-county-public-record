TRASH PICKUP – BCC WASTE SOLUTIONS
Mike Cox, with BCC Waste Solutions, appeared before the Board to discuss complaints
received regarding trash pickup, noting customers need to have their cans with the
opening facing the road and a shut lid. He stated customers with an abundance of trash
can call and request a second trash can for $8.00 a month. Chairman Quinn inquired how
many times the truck (driver) will pick up additional trash bags before they stop service at
that household. Mr. Cox stated most of the time the driver will give them two weeks, noting
the driver will take pictures before servicing the location and then send the pictures to

dispatch. Following discussion, Chairman Quinn discussed his concerns regarding
notifying the customer before stop service. Commissioner Rich discussed Mr. Cox’s good
customer service and willingness to work with customers. He reminded the public to place
their second (trash receptacle) can four feet apart so the automatic arm can grab the trash
can. Mr. Cox reminded the public the cans need to be open facing the road, not handle
facing.

PANCARE
Sean Golder and Katty Thorp, of PanCare appeared before the Board to discuss the
health services provided by PanCare, reporting the main medical branch is in Port St. Joe
at the old Citizens Bank building. He stated the building is being fitted for optical and
pharmacy as well as medical and dental care. Mr. Golder reported there is a dental site
housed at the Gulf County Health Department in Wewahitchka and there is a mobile unit
primarily for Gulf County serving behavioral health outreach. Commissioner Farrell
inquired about the hours and dates for the optical services. Mr. Golder stated PanCare
does not have concrete dates at this time but is taking appointments as received.
Commissioner Husband inquired about the dentist hours in Wewahitchka. Ms. Thorp
stated the hours are Monday through Friday with a dentist available two days a week and
a dental hygienist four days a week, noting they are looking to hire a full-time dentist.
County Administrator Hammond inquired about whether PanCare has the FQHC
(Federally Qualified Health Center) for the South-end of the County only. Mr. Golder
stated PanCare has FQHC for the whole County and shares responsibility with North
Florida from a medical standpoint for specifically the Wewahitchka zip code for medical.
Discussion followed regarding grant funding and services provided based on the grant
requirements.

BED TAX – SHORT-TERM RENTALS
Commissioner Husband presented an idea to the Board to consider adding a $1.00 per
night tax on short-term rentals, noting the County has 1,000 short-term rentals. He
discussed using this fund to assist in paying the volunteer fire departments, proposing
$25.00 per fire for each firefighter who attends and to use these funds to help with the
attrition rate, bunker gear, fire trucks and equipment, estimating the tax collection of
$219,000.00 annually. He discussed his concerns regarding the ISO (Insurance Services
Office) ratings, noting in his opinion the County has done a very good job in the past
placing and locating these fire departments to meet the ISO ratings. Discussion followed
regarding the proposal. County Attorney Novak discussed recent communications with
the FL Department of Revenue regarding the T.D.C. tax that is sunsetting this year, noting
the County advertised to hold a Public Hearing today but the Legislator changed the
statute to where it now is to be placed on the referendum. He stated staff will research
this topic further to see if it is possible and upon Board approval it will also be placed on
the ballot referendum. Commissioner McCroan suggested the proposed cost be $35.00
per fire call. Members of the Board discussed their support in this proposal. Upon inquiry
by Commissioner Farrell regarding whether the Board needs to put this item to a vote,
County Attorney Novak stated staff will bring this item back before the Board for
consideration once a determination in how to move forward is determined.

Rob Fisher, of Indian Pass appeared before the Board to discuss his concerns regarding
the proposed pilot program (septic system) in Indian Pass, noting community questions
have been provided to members of the Board prior to today’s meeting <copy of file with
Clerk for the record>. He thanked the staff and Board for their efforts to reduce the
pollution and negative environmental impact from existing septic tanks, noting the
community is hesitant in going with a single-source company, OnSyte Performance.
Following discussion regarding the neighborhood’s concerns about this startup company,
Mr. Fisher inquired if Indian Lagoon has been tested for pollutants. Commissioner Farrell
discussed the closures during the last twelve months, noting the longest closure last year
was 6-7 days due to the fecal matter in the Lagoon. He discussed the testing results,
offering to provide a copy to Mr. Fisher for his review. Following discussion, Chairman
Quinn inquired about possibly inviting the proposed company (OnSyte) to a workshop for
the public to discuss this further. County Administrator Hammond stated even though the
contracting is sole source the County will be the utility, reporting that Onsyte has a
presence in Florida. He discussed that the County has been looking to improve water
quality and this pilot program is not proposed to be mandatory, noting in the future
everyone on the Gulf, Lagoon or Bay will have to get off septic tanks. County Administrator
Hammond reported that the Northwest Florida Water Management District is paying for
this pilot project and N.W.F.W.M.D. and D.E.P. has put their stamp of approval on it.
Following discussion, Mr. Fisher suggested the Board move up the workshop date to
explain to the community as soon as possible.

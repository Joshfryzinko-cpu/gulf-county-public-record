JANUARY 15, 2025
WORKSHOP
HONEYVILLE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in workshop session with
the following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Phil
McCroan, and Commissioners Christopher McLemore, Lloyd Jack Husband, III, and
Randy Pridgeon.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Deputy Clerk Leanna Cooper,
County Administrator Michael L. Hammond, County Assistant Administrator Clay
Smallwood, III, Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Building Official Brad Bailey, Gulf
County E.D.C. Director Jim McKnight, Emergency Management Director/Fire Coordinator
Matt Herring, Gulf County E.M.S. Director Jody Daniels, G.I.S. Coordinator Clay Kennedy,
Human Resource Director Kaci Rhodes, Office of Management & Budget Director Sherry
Herring, County Planner Doug Crane, Public Works Director Mark Cothran, and T.D.C.
Executive Director Silvia Williams.
Chairman Quinn called the meeting to order at 8:05 a.m., C.T.
Chairman Quinn then opened the meeting in prayer and led in the Pledge of Allegiance
to the Flag.
COUNTY ISSUES
Commissioner Pridgeon discussed the Board and County staff coming together today to
provide updates on various topics, noting Gulf County will celebrate 100 years this year.
FLOATING DRY DOCK & ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT
Commissioner Pridgeon requested an update from E.D.C. Director Jim McKnight
regarding the floating dry dock and economic development. E.D.C. Director McKnight
appeared before the Board to provide an end-of-the-year report, <copy provided to the
Clerk for the record>, discussing the top employers within Gulf County, the ship projects
and job vacancies at Eastern Shipbuilding, and the opening of the GCI Annex. Upon
discussion by E.D.C. Director McKnight regarding an apprenticeship at Haney Vo-Tech,
Commissioner Pridgeon discussed the need for a trained workforce for Gulf County
students. E.D.C. Director McKnight discussed the floating dry dock and applying for
Triumph funds, noting it would be owned by the County and Eastern Shipbuilding would
lease the facility. Discussion followed regarding the Triumph grant. Commissioner
Pridgeon discussed his concerns regarding Triumph fundings that are not tied to the
school system are less likely to be granted. E.D.C. Director McKnight then provided an
update on Skyborne. Discussion followed regarding Skyborne and the water and road
projects for the new hanger facility (Costin Airport). E.D.C. Director McKnight then
concluded his presentation by discussing the objectives for 2025 and goals for 2026.

Commissioner McCroan discussed the floating dry dock being the driver for Gulf County.
Discussion followed regarding getting workforce training for the floating dry dock and the
need of affordable housing.
S.H.I.P. POLICY CHANGES
OMB Director Herring presented a recommendation from pages 9-10 in the Administrative
Packet <copy provided to the Clerk for the record> for the Board to review regarding
proposed changes to the S.H.I.P. policy. Discussion followed regarding income limits to
qualify for the housing programs. S.H.I.P. Director Lanier continued discussing with the
Board the recommended policy changes to the S.H.I.P. Policy. OMB Director Herring
discussed H.H.R.P. funding, Round I and II, noting Round I is closed and Round II has a
deadline of June 2025. Discussion followed regarding the hardship to qualify applicants
in the very-low and low income categories. Chairman Quinn requested OMB Director
Herring to explain what H.H.R.P. is to the public. OMB Director Herring discussed the
H.H.R.P. program. County Administrator Hammond discussed his concern regarding the
deadline approaching and applicants needing to get the remaining documentation by the
end of this month in order to close out by June 2025. Discussion followed regarding the
deadline and qualifying requirements. S.H.I.P. Director Lanier continued discussing the
recommended S.H.I.P. policy changes. OMB Director Herring concluded the remaining
recommended item changes. County Administrator Hammond noted the recommended
changes will be in the next Consent Agenda for Board approval.
VETERANS
Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn, V.S.O. Director Lanier reported Coffee with a Vet was
funded through the United Way and that funding is no longer available. She stated this
outreach event is not funded in her budget but has a desire to continue this outreach.
V.S.O. Director Lanier discussed attending spring and fall events and looking to do other
outreach efforts.
LIVING SHORELINE
Commissioner Pridgeon discussed the Apalachicola rocks and his interest in
implementing a living shoreline in Highland View near the old Butler’s restaurant site. He
stated this project is paid through grant funding. Commissioner McCroan discussed his
concerns regarding this area being a mud bottom, not a sand bottom. Commissioner
Pridgeon reported that the testing is free, done by FSU (Florida State University), to
determine if this site would benefit as a living shoreline project.
2025 BUDGET APPROPRIATIONS REQUEST
Upon inquiry by Commissioner Pridgeon, County Administrator Hammond stated staff has
some recommendations to present to the Board regarding the 2025 budget appropriations
request. He provided a request list for the Board to review <copy was not provided to the
Clerk for the record>, noting the appropriation request will be law enforcement salary,
breakwaters for Cape San Blas, airport, make up money from D.E.M. (Division of
Emergency Management), and a fire truck for Stone Mill Creek Fire Department.
Commissioner Pridgeon discussed his concerns regarding the new airport, noting he is
in support of the Port. Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns regarding the

airport contract with St. Joe Company, noting the economic driver is the floating dry dock.
After discussion by Commissioner Pridgeon regarding the airport in Franklin County,
Commissioner Husband discussed the proposed airport being good for emergency
services during hurricanes. County Administrator Hammond noted that Gulf County was
cut-off for five days after Hurricane Micheal, reporting that a helicopter could not land
because the landing site at the school was full of first responders and fire trucks, items
had to be dropped from the air at Cape San Blas, and the helicopter could not land at
Veterans’ Memorial Beacon Hill Park either due the staging area for Duke Energy. He
stated Gulf County is the only coastal County in northwest Florida that does not have an
airport. Discussion followed regarding Gulf County trying to team with Franklin County
and efforts to expand Costin Airport. County Administrator Hammond reported 95% of the
airport will be paid through Federal D.O.T. airport funds. Discussion followed regarding
economic growth, the airport working together with the floating dry dock, and the need for
a functioning Port Authority Board. County Administrator Hammond discussed holding a
workshop to allow County Consultant Yeager to provide an update on these projects,
noting that the Port Authority has not had a qualified quorum in several years. Discussion
followed regarding the Port Authority and the economic growth along the canal, and the
industrial area. Commissioner Pridgeion was in support of holding a workshop to discuss
the airport and the floating dry dock project.
The meeting did then recess at 10:07 a.m., C.T.
The meeting did reconvene at 10:22 a.m., C.T.
OLD GULF COUNTY COURTHOUSE
Commissioner Husband discussed efforts in trying to find an end-use of the Old Gulf
County Courthouse located in Wewahitchka, noting there are two agencies, City of
Wewahitchka and North Florida Head Start, that could take over the building. He stated
the top priority is to get this building on the historic registry, noting he would like to speak
to both agencies regarding any end use interest in the building. Commissioner McLemore
expressed his support in making the necessary repairs to the building. Commissioner
Pridgeon discussed using the Old Gulf County Courthouse to hold an office for T.D.C.,
office for the City of Wewahitchka and North Florida Head Start, and a museum.
Commissioner Husband discussed having a City and County partnership in making the
Old Gulf County Courthouse site a possible green area/town square.
ANIMAL CONTROL – HUMANE SOCIETY
Commissioner Husband discussed assisting the (St. Joseph Bay) Humane Society
regarding animal control. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed his concerns regarding
unadoptable dogs held at the shelter. Commissioner Husband discussed violent dogs and
the savings to the Humane Society by not housing unadopted dogs. County Administrator
Hammond reported that the Humane Society has dogs that have been at the shelter for
three years and the County policy is seven (7) days. He discussed having a discussion
with the Humane Society, the option to extend the time period, noting it is not humane for

the dog to reside in a shelter for three years. County Administrator Hammond discussed
the liability of adopting a dog that has bitten someone, his concerns regarding the amount
of money the County spends on animal control and funding the shelter (Humane Society).
County Administrator Hammond discussed his concerns when the Humane Society
refuses to accept an animal from County Animal Control, noting the County is paying the
Humane Society to accept these animals. Discussion followed regarding having a
workshop with the Humane Society and promoting a spay and neuter marketing
campaign through T.D.C.
IMPACT FEE STUDY
Commissioner Husband discussed the need for fire protection in low-line areas within the
County. He requested approval to visit Bay County to discuss their impact fee study
further. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed fire services, noting that every district needs
to have the same fire equipment tools.
FIRE
Commissioner Pridgeon discussed obtaining ISO rating for every fire district, inquiring if
this is a priority for the fire coordinator. Commissioner Pridgeon then discussed holding
training classes at the old White City Fire Department Station to help grow the County fire
departments. Discussion followed regarding ISO ratings. Commissioner McCroan
discussed the needed equipment and tools for each fire department and looking at
purchasing used fire trucks as a savings. County Administrator Hammond discussed his
concerns regarding getting ISO rating for every fire district. Discussion followed regarding
firefighting training and passing equipment to other fire departments.
ANIMAL CONTROL – HUMANE SOCIETY
County Administrator Hammond inquired of the Board’s next step regarding animal
control and the Humane Society. Chairman Quinn stated a workshop may not necessarily
be needed but have a Commissioner and County staff meeting with Humane Society
President Diana Burkett. Commissioners McCroan and Husband agreed.
RIVERINE – CREEK DEBRIS REMOVAL
Commissioner Husband discussed his concerns regarding debris left in creeks from
Hurricane Michael, inquiring if staff can apply for funding. Assistant County Administrator
Smallwood stated staff will ask for funding. County Administrator Hammond noted the
County did ask and received a small amount of funding. Assistant County Administrator
Smallwood noted a study was completed on Stone Mill Creek and the State is aware of
how much debris is there, stating staff can follow up. Discussion followed regarding the
original cleanup and various grant pots (resources) to assist in funding. Commissioner
Pridgeon discussed scheduling a meeting with the County’s lobbyist while attending
Legislative Day to discuss these issues. County Administrator Hammond noted that is
what staff typically does, reporting this year staff with meet with the D.O.T. Secretary
regarding opening the GCI Annex and the Foresty Camp. Discussion followed regarding
various funding projects and drainage issues throughout the County.

WORKCREWS
Commissioners McLemore discussed the need for at least two more workcrews, noting
that once the Annex opens there is hopes to increase the inmate workcrews.
PARKS & PARKS FUNDING
Commissioner McLemore discussed his concerns regarding various repairs needed in
parks in District I, inquiring if there are any grant funds available to assist with
maintenance repairs. County Administrator Hammond reported most grants will not pay
to maintain something that they have already paid for, noting the quality of the wood has
declined due to environmental changes. County Administrator Hammond discussed
needing direction from the Board in how to spend the park money. Discussion followed
regarding creating a priority list from each district. County Administrator Hammond
discussed the high utility costs to maintain various County parks. Commissioner McCroan
discussed the cost of a recent boardwalk at Salinas Park. Following discussion on the
need to create a priority park list, County Administrator Hammond requested each
Commissioner provide their top project within their district, Assistant Public Works
Director Hart will provide a cost estimate of each project, and then the Board can decide
the order of each park project.
ROADS, ROAD BOND & ROAD APPLICATIONS
County Administrator Hammond reported that, on average, the County has done a road
bond every 6.5 years, noting it has been 10 years since the last road bond. He stated the
Municipalities are interested in doing a road bond, and drafting a road list was discussed.
Discussion followed regarding the benefits of a road bond, contacting the Municipalities,
and drafting a priority road paving list.
UNDERWATER STRUCTURES & BEACH ENHANCEMENT
Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns regarding the underwater structures,
requesting an update on this project. Assistant County Administrator Smallwood reported
that N.F.W.F. (National Fish & Wildlife Foundation) has agreed to grant additional funds
to the County, noting the agreement will be ratified at the next Board meeting. He
discussed working towards obtaining a twelve-month permit. Discussion followed
regarding efforts to get Cape San Blas out of C.B.R.A. (Coastal Barrier Resources Act).
County Administrator Hammond discussed the appropriations requests, noting that staff
will present a list for the Board to consider at the February meeting.
WORKFORCE HOUSING
Chairman Quinn discussed coming up with a workforce housing plan. After discussion by
members of the Board regarding cost and building code updates, E.D.C. Director
McKnight stated he will present a proposal for the Board to consider at the next meeting
in February. County Administrator Hammond discussed his concerns on whether the
program is going to be tied to income requirements.
ROADS, ROAD BOND & ROAD APPLICATIONS
Chairman Quinn stated there are road paving needs within the City of Port St. Joe.

PARKS & PARK FUNDING
Chairman Quinn then discussed his concerns regarding not being able to apply for any
F.R.D.A.P. grants for parks in District IV due to all his parks are located inside the City
limits of Port St. Joe, requesting if there is an opportunity, then he would like to utilize
these funds.
OLD GULF COUNTY COURTHOUSE
Chairman Quinn inquired if the Old Gulf County Courthouse could be added to the
appropriations request, noting the County received funding from Congressman Dunn for
the Washington Gym. He also inquired if the Washington Gym could be added to the
appropriations request for additional funded projects. County Administrator Hammond
stated that both projects can be added, noting the Old Gulf County Courthouse would be
a closer match, but the Courthouse will be more than just changing windows and such.
Chairman Quinn recommended that the County reach out and ask.
PARKS & PARK FUNDING
OMB Director Herring stated that the City of Port St. Joe can apply for F.R.D.A.P. grants
for parks and discussion followed regarding this matter.
PRIVATE INSPECTORS – BUILDING DEPARTMENT
County Administrator Hammond discussed concerns regarding private inspectors, noting
the property owners are being cut-out. He discussed an incident in WindMark where there
was no insulation installed and another incident at St. Joe Beach there the structure had
to be torn down to the studs.
WATER SEWER COLLABORATION PARTNERSHIP
County Administrator Hammond stated that the County needs to meet with the City of
Port St. Joe to discuss collaborating or a partnership regarding water and sewer, noting
the County has received $30 million to do a sewer plant. He discussed teaming up with
the City of Port St. Joe on water to save the citizens money. County Administrator
Hammond discussed holding a meeting with the City of Port St. Joe to discuss this topic
along with roads, fire department, etc.
COUNTRY CLUB UPDATE
Assistant County Administrator Smallwood discussed the County Country Club Golf
Course, noting membership and revenue is up. He also discussed the irrigation project
and the new pro-shop on site.
T.D.C. TAX – 5TH PENNY
Commissioner Pridgeon asked if the County reached the $10 million threshold for the
T.D.C. 5th penny, if that would then give the County more flexibility in spending. County
Administrator Hammond discussed the possibility of there being different rules, but not
the flexibility that the County has heard. Discussion followed regarding spending flexibility
and checking with the lobbyist regarding the rules.

LEGISLATIVE DAY
County Administrator Hammond reported that Legislative Day is March 18th and 19th.
There being no further discussion, the meeting adjourned with a closing in prayer at 11:42
a.m., C.T.

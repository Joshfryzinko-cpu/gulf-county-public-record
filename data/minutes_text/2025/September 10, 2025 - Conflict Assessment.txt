SEPTEMBER 10, 2025
CONFLICT ASSESSMENT MEETING
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in a conflict assessment
meeting with the following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman
Phil McCroan, and Commissioners Christopher McLemore, Lloyd Jack Husband, III, and
Randy Pridgeon.
Present representing the City of Port St. Joe were: Mayor Rex Buzzett, City
Commissioners Steve Kerigan, Brett Lowry, and Scott Hoffman, City Attorney Clinton
McCahill, City Manager Jim Anderson and, City Clerk Charlottee Pierce <City
Commissioner Eric Langston was not present>.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Deputy Clerk Leanna Cooper,
County Administrator Michael L. Hammond, County Assistant Administrator Clay
Smallwood, III, Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Deputy Administrator Silvia Williams,
Building Official Brad Bailey, Code Enforcement Officer Robert Knowles, Human
Resource Director Kaci Rhodes, Office of Management & Budget Director Sherry
Herring, Mosquito Control Director Austin Horton, County Planner Doug Crane, and
Public Works Director Mark Cothran.
Chairman Quinn called the meeting to order at 10:01 a.m., E.T.
Chairman Quinn then led in the Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag.
GULF COUNTY, FLORIDA RESOLUTIONS 2025-43 & 2025-44 IN RESPONSE TO
CITY OF PORT ST. JOE ORDINANCES 618 & 619
County Attorney Novak noted that today’s Conflict Assessment Meeting was duly
advertised. He provided an overview of the timeline regarding this matter for the presence
court reporter. County Attorney Novak then provided an overview of the County’s
opposition to City Ordinances 618 (annexation) and 619 (land use change). He reported
the parcel ID is #04571-000R owned by H & K Holdings, LLC containing 2.5 acres located
on the north side of Industrial Road, noting surrounding parcels are zoned industrial.
County Attorney Novak presented an aerial map and a copy of the City’s advertisement
for the Board to review <copies were not provided to the Clerk for the record>. He noted
City Ordinance 618 is regarding annexation (parcel into the City limits), discussing the
County’s notice in opposition and legal challenge. County Attorney Novak stated that City
Ordinance 619 is regarding a land use change on same said parcel to a residential
classification. He noted the County’s argument is: 1) defective notice; 2) spot zoning; and
3) lacked the consistency of the comprehensive plan of the City of Port St. Joe. He noted
that if there is no resolution at today’s conflict assessment meeting, then the County will

seek a permanent injunction through the Courts. City Attorney McCahill discussed the
conclusion and the decision with the Judge’s decision (City Ordinance 616).
Commissioner Pridgeon discussed industrial use and the need for growth. Commissioner
Husband inquired what the density would be on this parcel if the annexation and land use
change were to take place. City Manager Anderson appeared before the Board to state
the density would be 15 units per acre. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Pridgeon, City
Manager Anderson stated that the parcel is plated as 12 lots. County Administrator
Hammond reported that it was a subdivision dating back to the Kenny’s Mill period, where
mill workers lived. He stated that since the County’s Comprehensive Plan adopted in
1992, retroactive 1990, it has been industrial and zoned land use industrial, noting the
area of Eastern Shipbuilding and the wastewater treatment plant was all part of Kenny’s
Mill, zoned industrial. Discussion followed regarding the zoning classification. Planner
Crane appeared before the Board to discuss the interest of the current property owner’s
desire to develop a subdivision for workforce housing, noting he did research this parcel
and found that the 2.5 acres is designated industrial land use. County Administrator
Hammond stated that the 2.5 acres was part of a larger parcel, Kenny’s Mill. City Mayor
Buzzett discussed his concerns regarding the need for workforce affordable housing,
noting the property owners’ desire is to build workforce housing for her employees.
Discussion followed regarding location, zoning, and growth. County Administrator
Hammond discussed the position of annexing this parcel with the City of Port St. Joe’s
guarantee that it will remain industrial use. Commissioner Husband discussed the zoning,
noting the City of Port St. Joe and the County has identified this area as industrial. He
discussed his concerns regarding keeping this area industrial for growth. Chairman Quinn
asked the City of Port St. Joe how much property is available behind 10th Street. City
Mayor Buzzett reported it is a 45-acre parcel that the City is working towards workforce
housing, noting only 25-30 acres is usable property. He discussed the agreement with the
County to allow 172 units. Chairman Quinn inquired if the City of Port St. Joe would be
willing to meet with the developer to discuss swapping 2.5 acres from the 10th Street
location for the 2.5 acres on Industrial Road. Commissioner Pridgeon noted the 2.5 acres
is across the street from the Port. Chairman Quinn expressed his desire to help if all
parties are interested in swapping property. Commissioner McCroan was in support of
this proposal. City Mayor Buzzett noted there is no road leading to the 45 acres. Chairman
Quinn discussed adding the new road to the upcoming road bond list. City Manager
Anderson discussed the road paving projects for the 45-acre site. Discussion followed
regarding the workforce housing project behind 10th street. City Commissioner Kerigan
discussed his concerns regarding the need for workforce housing for growth. Chairman
Quinn again inquired if the City of Port St. Joe would consider swapping if the developer
(property owner) was interested. City Mayor Buzzett and City Attorney McCahill discussed
drawing up a proposal. Discussion followed regarding annexing the property on Industrial
Road. Commissioner McCroan stated the Board does not have an issue with annexing
the parcel into the City, noting the disagreement is changing the land to residential.
Following discussion, Commissioner McCroan discussed the County purchasing the land
from the developer (Industrial Road property) and help the City of Port St. Joe facilitate
the workforce housing project behind 10th Street. Upon inquiry by City Mayor Buzzett,
County Administrator Hammond stated the County would offer to purchase the property
on the Industrial Road, but the City of Port St. Joe would offer 2.5 acres to the developer

from the 45-acre parcel behind 10th Street. City Mayor Buzzett discussed having the
attorney work up a proposal and then approach the developer (property owner). County
Administrator Hammond inquired when the City of Port St. Joe would like to meet again.
County Attorney Novak recommended the Board recess this meeting and bring back a
proposal and invite the property owner to attend. After discussion, County Administrator
Hammond discussed working with the City of Port St. Joe on density.
The meeting did recess at 10:55 a.m., E.T. on September 10, 2025.
The meeting did then reconvene on September 22, 2025, at 3:02 p.m., E.T.
The following members of the Board were present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., and
Commissioners Lloyd Jack Husband, III, and Randy Pridgeon <Vice Chairman Phil
McCroan and Commissioner McLemore was not present>.
Present representing the City of Port St. Joe were: Mayor Rex Buzzett, City
Commissioners Scott Hoffman, City Manager Jim Anderson, City Clerk Charlotte Pierce,
and City Attorney Clinton McCahill <via telephone>. <City Commissioners Eric Langston
and Steve Kerigan were absent.>
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Chief Deputy Leanna Cooper,
Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Deputy Administrator Silvia Williams, G.I.S.
Coordinator Clay Kennedy, and Office of Management & Budget Director Sherry Herring.
Chairman Quinn led in the Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag.
County Attorney Novak noted today is a reconvening of the Conflict Assessment Meeting
from September 10, 2025. He stated for the record this meeting was posted and
advertised in the newspaper and the County has no objection to the fact that the City of
Port St. Joe did not meet the Statutory deadline regarding procedural requirements. He
reported that a 5-point summary letter was submitted to the City of Port St. Joe and
property owner, Kaye Haddock after the September 10, 2025 Conflict Assessment
Meeting, noting the County would purchase the parcel from the owner at a negotiated
price plus closing fees, the City of Port St. Joe would possibly convey to H & K Holdings,
LLC (Kaye Haddock) comparable acreage within in the 46 acres within the City of Port
St. Joe (located behind 10th Street), the previously approved density by the County would
be unaffected by this additional density, if an agreement was reached, and upon recording
of the deed the County would withdraw the litigation and the parcel would remain as an
industrial tract. City Mayor Buzzett provided a summary of an email that City Attorney
McCahill provided to County Attorney Novak; noting there is no additional property at the
10th Street location that is buildable to convey to Ms. Haddock and government property
cannot be given to a private individual. County Attorney Novak inquired if the City has

viewed proposing a public private partnership. City Attorney McCahill noted Ms. Haddock
is not interested. County Attorney Novak discussed locating acres from the tract located
off 10th Street, providing an aerial of the proposed location <copy was not provided to the
Clerk for the record>. Members of the Board discussed the purpose of the City tract near
10th Street. Kaye Haddock, owner of H & K Holdings, LLC appeared before the Board to
discuss her intent when she purchased the parcel on Industrial Road, noting her
contractor visited the Planning Department and was told the parcel was mixed/use
commercial property and that she could build workforce housing. She stated she is
currently going through the process annexing the property into the City of Port St. Joe.
Ms. Haddock stated that she would consider selling the property to the County at a fair
market price. Discussion followed regarding the meeting with the Planning Department
about the zoned property and workforce housing. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed his
concerns in keeping the area on the Industrial Road an industrial zone. City
Commissioner Hoffman discussed his concerns regarding the new information provided
by Ms. Haddock regarding the zoning classification from the Planning Department.
Discussion followed. Ms. Haddock reported that she had a person in Tallahassee to
review the maps and it was marked as mixed/use commercial. County Attorney Novak
noted in 1930’s it was mill homes; however, the County is looking at the property from
when the comprehensive plan was adopted. City Attorney McCahill discussed annexing
the property into the City and come to an agreement. Commissioner Husband inquired if
Ms. Haddock would be interested in commercial-type development. Ms. Haddock stated
she would consider it. City Mayor Buzzett discussed his concerns regarding the private
landowner’s rights to build on her property and the zoning. He discussed using the area
for rental property for the maritime academy. County Attorney Novak discussed the
projects forthcoming in this industrial area. He stated the County is not at fault, noting the
County is trying to preserve the character of this property for the preservation of the entire
area. County Attorney Novak discussed the potential of the County purchasing the
property from Ms. Haddock. Discussion followed. Ms. Haddock discussed her concerns
regarding the need for affordable workforce housing, noting she would be open for the
County to purchase the property at market value. County Attorney Novak stated if there
is no resolution today with the City of Port St. Joe, then the County will notice the City and
both attorneys will attend a mitigation session, and the County can work up a proposal to
Ms. Haddock. City Mayor Buzzett discussed agreeing to the County purchasing the
property, annexing the parcel into the City and the City would zone it industrial. County
Attorney Novak discussed being approached by surrounding property owners, noting the
precedent will be set. Following discussion, County Attorney Novak noted the County
would not be able to purchase the property without an appraisal. City Commissioner
Hoffman discussed his concerns regarding County Attorney Novak’s comments that the
County was not at fault; agreeing that he does not want to postpone this meeting.
Chairman Quinn thanked Ms. Haddock for attending today’s meeting, noting he has

questions regarding what Ms. Haddock was told by the Planning Department and he will
be asking to review the maps.
There being no further discussion, the meeting did then adjourn at 3:58 p.m., E.T.

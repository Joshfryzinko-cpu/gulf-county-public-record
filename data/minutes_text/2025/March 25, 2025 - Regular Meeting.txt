S.H.I.P. UPDATE – APALACHEE REGIONAL PLANNING COUNCIL
Kwentin Eastberg, of Apalachee Regional Planning Council appeared before the Board
to provide an S.H.I.P. update, introducing staff members and presenting the Board with
the A.R.P.C. policies and procedures regarding the S.H.I.P. process <copy provided to
the Clerk for the record>. He discussed the application process and timelines to assist in
moving applications along more efficiently. Discussion followed regarding applicants’
status. Upon motion by Commissioner McCroan, second by Commissioner Husband, and
unanimous vote, the Board gave Mr. Eastberg additional time to speak. Mr. Eastberg
noted the encumbered deadline for Fiscal Year 2022-23 is June 30, 2025. Discussion
followed regarding the remaining funds available in FY2022-23. County Administrator
Hammond discussed the possibility of Gulf County receiving additional funding for S.H.I.P.
if the Renaissance Bill passes. Pat Hardman, a member of Long-Term Recovery,
appeared before the Board to discuss the desire to streamline, noting there are many
residents that need S.H.I.P. assistance but are illiterate or don’t have access to computers
or don’t really understand how to do the application process. She stated there needs to
be a presence by A.R.P.C. in the County. Mr. Eastberg noted the goal is to have a staff
member come to Gulf County weekly or biweekly.

PUBLIC HEARING – PROPOSED ORDINANCE / LAND USE MAP AMENDMENT

Attorney Novak read the proposed ordinance by title, noting this is the first reading.
Planner Crane noted the proposed ordinance will be sent to the State for review and
comments before holding the final hearing. He stated the two additional parcels, owned
by Steve Newman, have been included in the proposed ordinance as approved by this
Board. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed the effects leading up to drafting this proposed
ordinance and the purpose is to designate an industrial corridor. He discussed removing
a couple of the parcels from the proposed ordinance to remain agriculture that run along
the waterway to provide a buffer. Following discussion, Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. Christy McElroy, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to discuss the intent
of the proposed ordinance, looking at all the information including the buffer zone. She
discussed her concerns regarding the County having the infrastructure and roads for
future growth. Planner Crane noted this is a comprehensive plan amendment and in the
Comprehensive Plan there is a future land use map that reflects future growth within the
County. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Deborah Mayes, of Gulf County
Citizens Coalition appeared before the Board to inquire if the agriculture parcels near the
waterway have a 300’ buffer zone and therefore it cannot be used for residential areas.
Commissioner Husband noted his understanding is that nothing permanent can go in the
Corps of Engineer easement without the Corps permission. Rob Fisher, of Indian Pass
appeared before the Board to inquire if the Corps spoilage right-of-way is the same as a
street right-of-way. Commissioner Husband discussed the right-of-way is the edge of the
water and based on the centerline of the canal and the spoil easement is taken from the
center line of the survey and offset from the center line on either side of the canal.
<Chairman Quinn directed the Clerk to give Mr. Fisher two additional minutes.> Mr. Fisher
continued to discuss the right-of-way and buffer zone. Robert Branch, of Port St. Joe
appeared before the Board to discuss his reasons for appearing before the Board at
yesterday’s meeting and questions he presented. Chairman Quinn called for any
additional public comment. There was no further public comment. County Attorney Novak
reported this is the first Public Hearing and once staff receive comments back from the
State, then the second and final hearing will be advertised. Chairman Quinn then closed
the Public Hearing to the public.

HOWARD CREEK BOAT RAMP IMPROVEMENTS, PHASE II
County Administrator Hammond stated staff is ready to move forward on the second
phase of the Howard Creek Boat Ramp, noting the funds are available and the Board has
previously approved to advertise for sealed bids. He reported that the design is to install
a second boat ramp and place a floating dock between them.
TRAVEL – TALLAHASSEE
County Administrator Hammond discussed upcoming travel to Tallahassee to meet with
D.O.T. Secretary and staff in the Governor’s office. He noted staff is waiting for the final
release of the State budget.
WATER MAIN – WHITE CITY
David Smith, of White City appeared before the Board to discuss his concerns regarding
a water main in White City, noting the contractor has cut and repaired all the roads coming

up U.S. Highway 71. He reported that the repairs have not been made on Sealy Drive,
requesting Commissioner Pridgeon’s assistance in getting the road repaired. Mr. Smith
also discussed his concerns regarding the road on Sealy Drive collapsing and the need
for striping. Commissioner Pridgeon noted staff will look into the matter regarding Sealy
Drive.
PIPE REPLACEMENT – 10TH STREET PARK
Christy McElroy, of Panhandle Transparency Partnership appeared before the Board to
present project documents and letters from FL Department of Environmental Protection
regarding the pipe replacement at 10th Street Park <copy provided to the Clerk for the
record>. She discussed her concerns regarding work being performed without a D.E.P.
permit and the need to protect the County’s wetlands.
PARKS
Robert Branch, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to present copies of deeds and
parcel information regarding purchases of property for parks <copy provided to the Clerk
for the record>. Commissioner McCroan requested the Clerk hold Mr. Branch’s time.
Following discussion, Chairman Quinn then directed the Clerk to back Mr. Branch’s time
to 2 minutes and 30 seconds. Mr. Branch discussed the documentary stamps paid on the
deed. He then discussed a property near 10th Street Park where the County purchased
it, sold it to William Ramsey, Sr., and then purchased it back from Mr. Ramsey. He
discussed his concerns regarding the funds spent to purchase land in Jones Homestead
and near 10th Street for parks. Commissioner McCroan reported the lot on the corner of
Jones Homestead and U.S. Highway 98 was purchased for $39,000.00.
ST. ANDREWS BAY AND ST. JOE BAY ESTUARIES
County Administrator Hammond noted the Board decided back in 2020 not to join the St.
Andrews Bay nor the St. Joe Bay deal, stating the Chairman is not a member.
INVOICE – DEAD LAKES PARK
Kenny Strange, of Kenny Strange Electric, appeared before the Board to request
assistance with an unpaid invoice. He reported that in 2022 the Board leased Dead Lakes
Park to CPWG to manage the RV Park, noting he was hired for electrical work. Mr.
Strange stated the current amount due is $57,000.00, inquiring if the County can help
with getting payment. Discussion followed. County Attorney Novak stated he will assist
Mr. Strange in getting paid.
LAND USE CHANGE – WATERWAYS PROTECTION
Deborah Mayes, of Jones Homestead appeared before the Board to request the Board

Property Appraiser’s Office regarding her request, noting the Planning Department does
not draw any of the maps. County Administrator Hammond stated that before a building
permit is issued a survey is required, identifying where those lines are.

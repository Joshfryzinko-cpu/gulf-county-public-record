MARCH 24, 2025
WORKSHOP
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in a workshop session
with the following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Phil
McCroan, and Commissioners Christopher McLemore, Lloyd Jack Husband, III, and
Randy Pridgeon.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Deputy Clerk Leanna Cooper,
County Administrator Michael L. Hammond, County Assistant Administrator Clay
Smallwood, III, Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Building Official Brad Bailey,
Emergency Management Staff Assistant Tim Wood, Grant Administrator Erin Kennedy,
Grant Administrator Laura Beech-Armstrong, Human Resource Director Kaci Rhodes,
Office of Management & Budget Director Sherry Herring, County Planner Doug Crane,
Public Works Director Mark Cothran, and Assistant Public Works Director Raymond Hart.
Chairman Quinn called the meeting to order at 9:00 a.m., E.T.
Chairman Quinn then opened the meeting with prayer and led in the Pledge of Allegiance
to the Flag.
LARGE SCALE MAP AMENDMENT – FUTURE LAND USE MAP AMENDMENT
Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn regarding today’s workshop, County Administrator
Hammond noted staff is present to take questions and public comment. Commissioner
Pridgeon discussed the County initiated land use change and the concerns of his
constituencies regarding this future land use map amendment. He then discussed the
County being in support of jobs and a diverse economy, noting Gulf County cannot solely
depend on tourism. Commissioner Pridgeon suggested the Board name it, proposing Gulf
County Industrial Corridor. Discussion followed regarding the limited areas that remain
within the County for new businesses. Commissioner Pridgeon inquired what type of
businesses would be considered for this area. Planner Crane reported that businesses
could range from moving of goods, manufacturing of goods, noting it could be anything
with the distribution, manufacturing, or moving of goods can go in this proposed area.
Discussion followed regarding the desire to localize the industry businesses.
Commissioner Pridgeon inquired how the County can protect the two waterways in this
area. Planner Crane reported there currently is a 50’ (foot) wetland setback due to being
south of the Intercoastal Waterway. He stated that depending on the type of industry there
are setback restrictions in the LDR (land development regulations). Following discussion,
Commissioner Pridgeon again inquired if the Board had an interest in calling this area the
Gulf County Industrial Corridor. Commissioner Husband inquired if it legally must have a
name and if the Board was to name it would there be any repercussions. County Attorney
Novak answered no to both, noting there is nothing keeping the County from naming it

nor restricting the County from using it. Chairman Quinn opened to the public, calling for
public comment. Robert Branch, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to inquire
about today’s Public Notice, noting it was advertised showing all the parcels and now it
does not show the parcel numbers. County Attorney Novak noted the Chairman can title
the meeting notice however he wants there is no legal requirement for the parcel numbers
to be listed. Discussion followed regarding the Public Notice regarding today’s workshop
meeting. Mr. Branch stated if the County has a desire to name it, he recommends calling
it Kenny Mills Road. Discussion followed regarding the proposed airport and
infrastructure. County Administrator Hammond noted this is not about the airport, stating
the airport does not need a land use change. Chairman Quinn noted the parcel numbers
are listed for tomorrow’s Public Hearing. County Administrator Hammond stated these are
two separate things, 1. the Public Hearings on tomorrow’s Agenda has the parcel
numbers listed as required; 2. Commissioner Pridgeon requested a workshop for the
public to have more input on this matter, which does not require the parcel numbers to be
listed on the meeting notice. Commissioner Pridgeon stated the maps are available for
review at the back of the meeting room, noting the GIS maps are not viewable on power
point. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Debroah Mays, of the Gulf County
Citizens Coalition appeared before the Board to thank the Board for holding this meeting,
inquiring what type of high intensity industry would require 150’ buffer zone. Planner
Crane discussed any industry that would potentially have contaminants to the waterways.
Mrs. Mays discussed a letter submitted to members of the Board from the Gulf County
Citizens Coalition <copy was not provided to the Clerk for the record> asking the County
place a 250’ (foot) buffer zone and two LDRs that prevents any dredging through the
buffer zone or pipelines routed to the Port. She stated the Coalition does not oppose
industry and manufacturing but wants it to be balanced and protect the environment. Mrs.
Mays requested that at tomorrow’s first reading (Public Hearing) the Board agree to
rewrite the proposed ordinance to include a buffer zone and no dredging in the Gulf
County Canal and no pipelines to the Port. She also asked the Board what their vision is
for these lands, if they don’t agree to rewrite the proposed ordinance. Commissioner
Pridgeon noted he is unaware if the Gulf County Canal can be dredged. Discussion
followed, noting the Corps of Engineers owns the Canal. Planner Crane noted the
proposed ordinance is not the place for any of Mrs. Mays’ asks, stating this is a
comprehensive plan amendment that is submitted to the State to change the
Comprehensive Plan. He reported the place for her ask is in the LDR (land development
regulations). Planner Crane discussed his concerns regarding amending the LDR and
businesses going through the variance process if the Board makes the requested
changes. He stated that the way the rules stand, the Board will have to vote on any
industry that wants to come in to have access to the Canal through their development
order. John Ehrman, of Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to request that the
Board look at the buffer, discussing his concerns regarding the need of a buffer. Sarah
Hodgson, of St. Joe Beach appeared before the Board to inquire what the setback back
is in the LDR from the waterway. Planner Crane reported it is 50’ (feet). Discussion
followed regarding the land and its current allowable land usage. David Smith, of White
City appeared before the Board to discuss the purpose of this land use change to
encourage industry to come to Gulf County, inquiring if there is any industry expressing a
desire to locate in this area. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed the recent businesses

that have opened on Industrial Road since Hurricane Michael. Chairman Quinn called for
any additional public comment. Butch Kline, of Cape San Blas appeared before the
Board; suggesting they name this area Gulf County Opportunity Park.
There being no further discussion, upon motion by Commissioner McCroan, second by
Commissioner Husband, and unanimous vote, the meeting did then adjourn at 9:47 a.m.,
E.T.

NOVEMBER 25, 2025
WORKSHOP
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in a workshop with the
following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Phil McCroan,
and Commissioners Christopher McLemore, Lloyd Jack Husband, III, and Randy
Pridgeon.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Chief Deputy Clerk Leanna
Cooper, Deputy Clerk Casi Campbell, County Administrator Michael L. Hammond,
Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Deputy Administrator Silvia Williams, Emergency
Management Director/Fire Coordinator Matt Herring, Gulf County E.M.S. Director Jody
Daniels, G.I.S. Coordinator Clay Kennedy, Human Resource Director Kaci Rhodes,
Office of Management & Budget Director Sherry Herring, Mosquito Control Director Austin
Horton, County Planner Doug Crane, Public Works Director Mark Cothran, Assistant
Public Works Director Raymond Hart, Sheriff Mike Harrison, and Interim T.D.C. Executive
Director Warren Yeager, Jr.
Chairman Quinn called the meeting to order at 8:00 a.m., E.T.
Chairman Quinn led in the Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag.
GULF COUNTY T.D.C.
Deputy Administrator Williams provided a presentation packet <copy provided to the Clerk
for the record> to the Board from a recent T.D.C. meeting, providing the Board a status
update regarding tourist tax collections and short-term rental services. Commissioner
Pridgeon discussed his concerns regarding tracking short-term rentals and bed (tourist)
tax to ensure that all is being collected. He inquired if there are other avenues the County
can pursue to make sure short-term rentals are paying the bed tax. Discussion followed.
Deputy Administrator Williams reported that the Tax Collector’s Office is receiving bed tax
from approximately 1,800 properties. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed working with the
City of Port St. Joe regarding the short-term rental properties in WindMark. County
Administrator Hammond recommended the City of Port St. Joe implement the County’s
short-term rental registration policy. He discussed his concerns regarding the false alarm
calls taxing the taxpayers and the possibility of imposing a fine for repeated calls to the
same location. Commissioner Pridgeon requested the topic of short-term rentals be
added to the possible upcoming workshop with the City of Port St. Joe.
Commissioner Pridgeon discussed his concerns with golf carts, noting the matter is
different on the south end of the County verse the north end. He reported that in the City
of Port St. Joe has a golf cart policy that requires owners to obtain a decal, encouraging
the County before January 2026 to draft a policy regarding golf carts in the tourist corridor.

Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns regarding golf carts at the Cape and
being driven on the Cape San Blas Road. Sheriff Harrison discussed his concerns
regarding how to regulate golf carts when visitors bring them from out of state. Discussion
followed. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McCroan regarding the new State law, Sheriff
Harrison reported the age limit to drive a golf cart requires the driver to possess a valid
driver’s license. Discussion followed regarding the City of Port St. Joe’s golf cart policy
and the County adopting a proposed ordinance regarding golf carts on the tourist corridor.
Commissioner Husband discussed his concerns regarding requiring visitors to register
golf carts during their short visit, noting the ‘back beach road’ (Gulf Coast Parkway) would
resolve a lot of these issues. Commissioner McCroan requested that the golf cart rental
companies be included in the workshop. County Administrator Hammond discussed
separating the golf cart usage between beach driving verses regular driving, noting there
was a violation at Salinas Park. Discussion followed.
Upon inquiry by County Administrator Hammond, Emergency Management Herring
appeared before the Board to discuss the number of false alarm calls, noting majority
have been in the WindMark area.
Public Works Director Cothran discussed staff performing inspections and short-term
rentals registering, noting through these investigations 200 more homes have been found.
Commissioner Pridgeon thanked T.D.C. for keeping the bathrooms clean.
GULF COUNTY E.M.S.
E.M.S. Director Daniels presented a presentation to the Board <copy provided to the
Clerk for the record> regarding an overview of the Gulf County Emergency Medical
Services (E.M.S.). He provided an overview of the operations, new vehicles and medical
equipment, staffing schedule, call volume, transport, and special projects. Commissioner
Husband inquired of the total miles on an ambulance in a year. E.M.S. Director Daniels
reported an ambulance typically runs 50,000 miles per year. Commissioner McLemore
inquired if the County has a contract with Bay Medical (Ascension Sacred Heart Bay) to
transport patients to Panama City. E.M.S. Director Daniels stated the County does not
have a contract, noting the County does not handle all of their emergency transports.
County Administrator Hammond reported that the County does receive payment for those
transport services. Discussion followed regarding nonemergency transport services.
E.M.S. Director Daniels continued to discuss staffing, recruitment and training. He
reported that the County does have landing zones within the County. Upon inquiry by
Commissioner McLemore regarding combative patients, E.M.S. Director Daniels
discussed working with local law enforcement on site, noting if there is a medical need,
staff try to transport the combative patient. Discussion followed regarding staff safety with
combative patients.
The meeting did then recess at 9:07 a.m., E.T.
The meeting did reconvene at 9:20 a.m., E.T.

Upon inquiry by Commissioner Pridgeon, E.M.S. Director Daniels discussed the desire to
move towards three (3) captains per shift, noting having a 24-hour a day captain will
provide better coverage for the County. Discussion followed regarding coverage and
staffing. Commissioner Pridgeon noted he liked the idea that captains can assume the
role on an ambulance when needed. He also discussed E.M.S. using the heavy rescue
truck from Beaches Fire Department Countywide for vehicle accidents. E.M.S. Director
Daniels noted there is room at the Port St. Joe ambulance building to park the heavy
rescue truck. Commissioner Pridgeon proposed moving a unit to the community service
center at St. Joe Beach. Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns regarding the
call volume, noting an ambulance was stationed at the South Gulf Fire Department at the
Cape during peak season. E.M.S. Director Daniels discussed the calls and response time
during the period the ambulance truck was stationed at the Cape. Commissioner
McCroan discussed his concerns regarding a unit stationed at St. Joe Beach and the
response time for calls to Cape San Blas. E.M.S. Director Daniels noted two trucks
(ambulances) are stationed at the Courthouse Complex, discussing the call volume and
constant movement of the ambulances. Discussion followed regarding location options to
station ambulances. County Administrator Hammond noted the County will lose $1.5
million in E.M.S., stated the County has not had to pay for a new ambulance in years. He
reported that the County’s largest call volume is Sacred Heart Hospital and the nursing
home (Shores Nursing and Rehab Center). Commissioner Pridgeon discussed his
concerns regarding the growing population in Gulf County. Upon inquiry by Commissioner
McCroan, E.M.S. Director Daniels stated the heavy rescue truck would be an asset for
the County. Commissioner McLemore discussed the mileage on the two newest
ambulances, inquiring about the schedule for obtaining a new ambulance. E.M.S. Director
Daniels reported that a new ambulance truck was ordered over a year ago with an
anticipated arrival date of summer 2026. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Pridgeon
regarding budget planning, County Administrator Hammond stated the planning begins in
January, noting equipment is staged out. Discussion followed regarding the fleet.
Chairman Quinn thanked E.M.S. Director Daniels and his staff for their hard work,
encouraging staff to continue applying for grants.
There being no further business, and upon motion by Commissioner McCroan, second
by Commissioner Husband, and a unanimous vote, the meeting did then adjourn at 9.49
a.m., E.T.

PROPOSAL – HABITAT CONSERVATION PLAN
County Administrator Hammond discussed receiving proposals to update the HCP
(Habitat Conservation Plan), noting the first HCP completed in 2007 and the second which
was finalized in 2017 were never approved by FL Fish & Wildlife Commission. He
requested permission from the Board to update the HCP by receiving proposals.
Commissioner Husband inquired if it would be more cost effective to join the two plans
than to start over. County Administrator Hammond agreed that it would be a savings to
combine the two plans. Discussion followed regarding merging the two Habitat
Conservation Plans. Commissioner McCroan motioned to approve. Commissioner
McLemore seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Coastal
Community Association President Pat Hardman appeared before the Board to encourage
the Board to get an HCP in place. She discussed her concerns regarding restoration,
resiliency, and building an environment for wildlife. Commissioner McCroan noted the
matter is not the product, but FL Fish and Wildlife Commission is concerned of being sued
by environmentalists. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being none, the
motion passed unanimously. County Administrator Hammond stated he spoke with
Congressman Patronis regarding the matter, noting the Eglin beach property needs to be

conveyed to the County just like all the other former military bases or Corps of Engineer
parcels.

RESOLUTION – CHALLENGE CITY ORDINANCES #618 & #619
County Administrator Hammond stated the property (2.5-acre tract on Industrial Road) is
industrial property and has always been industrial property, discussing users reviewing
the real text. Discussion followed regarding the conflict assessment meeting held on
September 22, 2025. He requested permission from the Board to have the parcel
appraised and if both parties can come to an agreement, to purchase the property from
H & K Holdings, LLC (Parcel ID #04571-000R). County Attorney Novak provided an
update regarding the Conflict Assessment Meeting that was held on September 10, 2025
and reconvened on September 22, 2025, noting the County would look to purchase the
2.5-acre tract on Industrial Road and the City of Port St. Joe would provide comparable
land behind 10th Street. He reported that upon further review the City of Port St. Joe feels
they cannot provide the comparable land, noting the property owner is not interested in
building affordable housing on the tract behind 10th Street. County Attorney Novak
discussed the County purchasing the 2.5-acre tract on Industrial Road to potentially
negate the annexation and spot zoning challenge that the County has filed in court. He
noted no resolution was reached at the Conflict Assessment Meeting, providing highlights
from that meeting. County Attorney Novak then presented a proposed resolution for
consideration approving and directing administration to commence legal action to oppose
City Ordinances #618 and #619. Commissioner McCroan noted the property is in
proximity of two (2) industrial seafood plants, wastewater treatment plant, and several
other industrial businesses. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed the comprehensive plan
and how the Board tried to negotiate with the City of Port St. Joe. Commissioner Husband
noted it has been the intent of the Board, over the years, to purchase any waterfront
property, stating this is the gateway to a seawall with potential development for future
jobs. Chairman Quinn discussed his concerns regarding efforts by the County and the
County being blamed. County Attorney Novak read the proposed resolution for the Board
to consider. Discussion followed. County Attorney Novak requested a vote on the
resolution and a second motion to authorize administration to work on the purchase of
the property. Commissioner McLemore made a motion to move forward. Commissioner
Pridgeon seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Deborah
Mays, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to inquire what the County has spent
over a million dollars on. County Attorney Novak noted the costs to design the floating dry
dock, the projected designs of the floating dry dock and the airport, and the seed money
for Eastern Shipbuilding. Chairman Quinn called for any additional public comment. Chief
Deputy Clerk Cooper called clarification of the motion. County Attorney Novak noted the
first motion is for the adoption of the proposed resolution. There being no further public
comment, the motion passed unanimously, as follows:

STATUS UPDATE – OYSTER HARVEST LAWSUIT
County Attorney Novak discussed the Apalachicola Bay being closed to recreational
oyster harvesting through an executive order. He reported that FL Fish & Wildlife
Commission is recommending reopening in some areas, noting Indian Lagoon is not one
recommended for reopening. County Attorney Novak discussed the litigation between the
County and the FL Fish & Wildlife Commission regarding oyster harvesting in Indian
Lagoon, requesting Board direction on how to move forward with the lawsuit. County
Administrator Hammond stated staff’s recommendation is to drop the lawsuit and work
towards reopening Indian Lagoon for recreational only by using the political side.
Commissioner Pridgeon stated he is in agreement of dropping the lawsuit, noting that FL
Fish & Wildlife Commission will reopen the Apalachicola Bay. He discussed his concerns
regarding the closing of oyster harvesting and the research performed by FWC,
suggesting that the County form a fisherman’s association. County Attorney Novak
requested Board approval to allow himself and County Administration to move forward.
Chairman Quinn called for a motion to move forward with dropping the oyster lawsuit.
Commissioner Pridgeon made the motion. Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment. The motion
passed unanimously.

INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT – ROAD BOND LOAN
County Administrator Hammond reported that the City of Port St. Joe rejected the road
bond (loan) interlocal agreement, requesting Board approval to negotiate a separate
agreement with the City of Wewahitchka who will be participating in the road bond (loan)
project. He stated that the current interlocal expires in 2031, therefore in 2032 each
Municipality will receive their percentage of the gas tax funds. County Administrator
Hammond noted the interlocal agreement with the City of Wewahitchka will have the
same percentage, but starting in 2032 the City of Wewahitchka will remit approximately
$40,000.00 to the County annually for the duration of the new road bond (loan). Chairman
Quinn discussed his concerns regarding the City of Port St. Joe not signing the interlocal
agreement and the roads that need to be paved in District IV. County Administrator
Hammond asked for a motion, noting that in the past both Cities agreed to road bonds.
He stated in 2032 each City will receive their portion of the gas tax funds, and the County
will need an interlocal agreement with the City of Wewahitchka that they will remit their
portion of gas tax funds to the County to participate in the road bond (loan). Commissioner
McCroan discussed the potential the City of Port St. Joe would have received in road
paving funds if they would signed the interlocal agreement. Commissioner McLemore
motioned to move on with the City of Wewahitchka. Commissioner Husband seconded
the motion. After discussion by members of the Board, County Attorney Novak requested
to include in the motion to rescind the interlocal agreements that were submitted last
month. Commissioner McLemore agreed to add this to his motion. Commissioner
Husband stated his second stands. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There
being no public comment. The motion passed unanimously.

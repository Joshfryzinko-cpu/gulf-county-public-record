FEBRUARY 18, 2025
CONFLICT ASSESSMENT
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioner met this date in a conflict assessment
meeting with the following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman
Phil McCroan, and Commissioners Christopher McLemore, Lloyd Jack Husband, III, and
Randy Pridgeon.
Present representing the City of Port St. Joe were: Mayor Rex Buzzett, City
Commissioners Eric Langston, Steve Kerigan, Brett Lowry, and Scott Hoffman, City
Manager Jim Anderson, City Clerk Charlottee Pierce and City Attorney Clinton McCahill.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Deputy Clerk Leanna Cooper,
County Administrator Michael L. Hammond, County Assistant Administrator Clay
Smallwood, III, Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Gulf County E.D.C. Director Jim
McKnight, Emergency Management Director/Fire Coordinator Matt Herring, G.I.S.
Coordinator Clay Kennedy, Human Resource Director Kaci Rhodes, Office of
Management & Budget Director Sherry Herring, Mosquito Control Director Austin Horton,
County Planner Doug Crane, Public Works Director Mark Cothran, Assistant Public Works
Director Raymond Hart, Sheriff Mike Harrison, and T.D.C. Executive Director Silvia
Williams.
Chairman Quinn called the meeting to order at 9:00 a.m., E.T.
Chairman Quinn opened the meeting with prayer and led in the Pledge of Allegiance to
the Flag.
GULF COUNTY, FLORIDA RESOLUTION 2025-11 IN RESPONSE TO CITY OF PORT
ST. JOE ORDINANCE 616
County Attorney Novak discussed the notice of the conflict assessment meeting and the
adoption of County Resolution No. 2025-11, presenting corresponding documents to the
Board for review <copy was not provided to the Clerk for the record>. He noted that when
there is a conflicting government agency between City and County there are provisions
under the Florida Statute that require the City and County to meet to try and resolve the
issue before going to court. County Attorney Novak stated the purpose of today’s conflict
assessment meeting is regarding Ordinance 616 adopted by the City of Port St. Joe,
noting the County adopted Resolution No. 2025-11 to trigger the conflict resolution as an
effort to avoid going to court. Discussion followed regarding the order of events for today’s
meeting. County Attorney Novak stated for the public the reasoning for today’s meeting
is in regards to City Ordinance 616 that potentially voluntary annexes approximately 113
acers, Parcel ID #04268-000R, along Premier Drive adject to the Industrial Road. He
discussed the parcels surrounding this parcel and the timeline of events leading up to

today’s meeting, reading aloud the Legislative language regarding a conflict resolution
procedure and its definitions. County Attorney Novak then provided the County’s legal
arguments. E.D.C. Director McKnight appeared before the Board to state that the
proposed project is not consistent with what existed in the past nor future plans for Gulf
County. He discussed past use being an industrial area, noting from a study completed
by Duke Energy, this area is the number one business/economic development site for
Gulf County. Discussion followed regarding various businesses that viewed the area in
bringing their business to Gulf County. E.D.C. Director McKnight presented a proposed
plan from a businessman <copy not provided to the Clerk for the record> interested in
moving to Gulf County. Following discussion, County Attorney Novak then provided the
conflict of nature, the summation, and then offered comments from the Board and staff.
After discussion regarding the proposed business plan, County Administrator Hammond
discussed the density in the County, noting the City of Port St. Joe has a higher density
and that is a draw for developers to go to the City and ask for annexation. He stated this
is all about land use, expressing his concern of the land use change to residential will kill
future light industrial. Discussion followed by members of the Board. City Mayor Buzzett
stated this parcel of land has sat dormant for 25 years and a proposal was presented to
the City of Port St. Joe. He stated a large-scale amendment will have to be submitted to
the State for approval. He then discussed the parcels surrounding the proposed annex
parcel, noting this is the last parcel not in the City limits that is surrounded by the City.
Mayor Buzzett stated the industry for south Gulf County is tourism, noting there will not
be another smokestack. He encouraged the Board to drop their opposition to this
voluntary annexation. Commissioner McCroan discussed the realization that Gulf County
will not go back to smokestacks and tourism is critical to Gulf County, noting a benefit for
Gulf County as a whole, is the access to the water and using a floating dry dock scenario
business to diversify the economy. He discussed his concerns regarding the proposed
residential housing on a site that was a chemical plant for 70 years, inquiring whether an
environmental study has been performed. City Mayor Buzzett discussed his concerns
regarding the traffic from Eastern Shipbuilding and the need for workforce housing.
Chairman Quinn inquired who would be living in the condos at the proposed marina,
noting an average schoolteacher will not be able to afford to live in these condos. Mayor
Buzzett discussed these condos would most likely be second homes, noting the income
from tap fess and ad valorem taxes. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed his concerns
regarding the purchase of the industrial property and diversifying the economy, noting that
the County cannot always depend on tourism. Discussion followed regarding ad valorem
taxes and infrastructure. City Commissioner Lowry discussed various joint projects
between the City and County and the working relationship between the two. Chairman
Quinn discussed his desire to come to a conflict resolution and supporting the City of Port
St. Joe with future projects. County Administrator Hammond stated the philosophy of the
County is that this site is industrial and needs to remain industrial, noting the developer
will play the game of going to the City for more density. Discussion followed regarding the
need for light industry to remain in this area. After further discussion, City Commissioner
Langston discussed his concerns regarding the constituencies’ opinion and the
opportunity to bring more income to the City of Port St. Joe, noting that the Board does
not know what the developer will do with this parcel of land. <Commissioner Husband left
the meeting at 10:22 a.m., E.T.> City Commissioner Kerigan noted this meeting is to

discuss coming to a conflict resolution regarding the voluntary annexation. He stated this
is a good move for the City of Port St. Joe and its constituents. City Commissioner Kerigan
then discussed the steps that the developer will have to go through to determine the land
use of this parcel. <Commissioner Husband returned to the meeting at 10:24 a.m., E.T.>
Upon inquiry by Commissioner Husband, County Attorney Novak stated today’s meeting
was an opportunity for both parties to express themselves, noting there is no vote of the
Board nor public comment at this conflict assessment meeting as each Board has
upcoming meetings and decisions will be finalized at that time. City Commissioner Lowry
discussed coming to a resolution to annex the parcel and the City of Port St. Joe
maintaining the land as industrial. Commissioner McCroan expressed his support in a
contingency that the parcel remains industrial use. Discussion followed by members of
the County Board in support of the property remaining industrial. City Mayor Buzzett noted
he would not want to tie the hands of the City of Port St. Joe on options of the land or the
developer. County Attorney Novak stated to the Board that he will provide more legal
instruction at the next Board meeting, noting the dialog and questions today were great,
but the window is short. He stated the City of Port St. Joe can adjourn, go in recess, or
ask questions at their upcoming hearing, noting there is no requirement by the City of
Port St. Joe to make a decision at their March 4, 2025, public hearing. County Attorney
Novak stated for the record that there has never been any communication with the
property owner that he nor County administration is aware of.
There being no further discussion, the meeting did then adjourn at 10:52 a.m., E.T.

EGLIN
County Administrator Hammond reported that Eglin (Air Force Base) closed the beaches
(near Cape San Blas) without any warning or reasonable excuse, noting Eglin is not
willing to be reasonable. He stated staff have reached out to the offices of Senator Scott,
Congressman Patronis and Congressman Dunn regarding the matter. Jim Norton
appeared before the Board to provide a historical summary from when he worked for
Congressman (Allen) Boyd during the exchange of the old Coast Guard property, also
called the old D3 site. He discussed the property bypassing BLM (Bureau of Land
Management) and being granted to Eglin Air Force, stating an agreement between Eglin
and Gulf County was established to allow beach driving, obtain the lighthouse and the
keeper’s quarters. Mr. Norton discussed his support on beach driving, stating the Air
Force is in breach of the contract. County Administrator Hammond discussed the Coast
Guard barracks, noting that most were destroyed by Hurricane Opal in 1995 and the
County attempted to obtain this property, but the Air Force wanted it. Following
discussion, County Administrator Hammond noted that one of the reasons for the closure
was not a true account, noting the County is a good steward. He discussed the County
obtaining this property and protecting it for a passive park. County Administrator
Hammond discussed various other Coast Guard properties within the County that were
transferred from BLM to Gulf County. Following discussion, County Administrator

Hammond noted Eglin mentioned that FWC (Fish and Wildlife Commission) has not
accepted the County Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP). He stated the higher-up’s (FWC)
did not accept the Habitat Conservation Plan presented in 2017, but the County has been
enforcing it. Discussion followed regarding obtaining a copy of the contract. Chairman
Quinn called for public comment. Todd Nierh appeared before the Board to discuss his
concerns regarding the closure and the need to educate the public about protecting the
environment. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to extend his time. Upon motion by
Commissioner Husband, second by Commissioner Pridgeon, and unanimous vote, the
Board granted Mr. Nierh additional time to speak. Mr. Nierh continued to discuss his
support of beach driving and reopening that portion of the beach to the public. Chairman
Quinn called for any additional public comment. John Ehrman, of Cape San Blas
appeared before the Board to discuss the need to review the contract and the ability to
enforce the Ordinances. Chairman Quinn again called for public comment. There was no
further public comment. County Administrator Hammond noted staff will continue the
pressure until the County receives a good outcome. He stated the contract is between
the Coast Guard and the Air Force, noting the County has a license agreement with Eglin.
Sheriff Harrison appeared before the Board to discuss the volume at the Cape and his
staff covering and enforcement. Commissioner McCroan discussed his concerns
regarding this matter. County Administrator Hammond discussed the issue with children
driving golf carts. Commissioner Pridgeon discussed witnessing a 6-seater golf cart going
over the Highland View Bridge, noting the Board needs to hold a workshop to discuss a
plan going forward regarding golf carts and vandalism. Commissioner McCroan
discussed working on getting it back open and then discussing the other issues. A person
from the public inquired if you can walk around the gate. County Attorney Novak noted
the gate is closed, but with the County’s license agreement it goes to the mean highwater
line. He discussed the County being the stewards of the property and enforcement, but
unable to due to the recent closure. Following discussion, Jim Norton appeared before
the Board to state the property that the Air Force owns, the military claims ownership into
the water. He cautioned the County before allowing the public to go walking (in the
shoreline). Mr. Ehrman inquired why the County cannot have the military patrol their
beach. Craig Williams, from Congressman Dunn’s office appeared before the Board to
discuss the efforts to get the beach open as soon as possible.
ROAD BOND
County Administrator Hammond stated staff is recommending the Board agree to a tax-
exempt bank loan for 25 (twenty-five) years rather than executing a road bond. He
reported the positives are: 1) lower issuance cost; 2) quicker to access; 3) less
complicated; 4) the disclosure requirements are much less; 5) no need for a rating
agency; 6) competitive interest rate; and 7) the flexibility. Discussion followed regarding
the calling of bonds. County Administrator Hammond discussed closing on or before
October 1, 2025. He stated that staff paid an outside source to draft the ordinances, and
the Florida Department of Revenue notified the County regarding these proposed
ordinances, noting the County will draft these proposed ordinances the way D.O.R.
recommends. County Administrator Hammond stated that he does not want to hold the
Public Hearing at the September meeting due to the timeline. County Attorney Novak
stated the County has revised the 6th (sixth) Cent Ordinance (#2025-04) and staff will

advertise the D.O.R. amendments in it and advertise for the two Public Hearings, noting
it may require the Board hold a special meeting in September.
INTERLOCAL AGREEMENTS FOR ROAD PAVING
County Administrator Hammond discussed presenting an interlocal agreement to both
Municipalities (regarding the bank loan for road paving), requesting Board approval to
enter into negotiations for an interlocal agreement with both Cities to bring back to the
Board for consideration at the next meeting. Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn, County
Administrator Hammond requested Board approval to the County executing a tax-exempt
bank loan in lieu of a road bond and permission to submit the interlocal agreements to
the Cities. Discussion followed regarding closing and issuance of the funds.
Commissioner Pridgeon inquired about the Municipalities not accepting the interlocal, and
whether the County will still move forward. County Administrator Hammond confirmed,
noting the projected funding is $9.5 million of new money, with the City of Port St. Joe,
based on the interlocal agreement, is intitled to 21% and City of Wewahitchka is intitled
to 10%. He noted if the Cities do not agree to the interlocal, then the Cities will receive
the default percentage of the 6th cent (gas tax). Upon inquiry by Commissioner Husband
regarding the motion, County Administrator Hammond noted the motion requested will
allow him to do exactly what is laid out in this package; selecting the low bid, terms set,
and interest is locked. Commissioner Pridgeon motioned to accept the bank (loan) versus
the bond. Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn inquired if
Commissioner Pridgeon wanted to include in his motion to allow County Administrator
Hammond to negotiate the interlocal agreements with the two Cities. Commissioner
Pridgeon discussed moving forward with or without the Cities agreeing to the interlocal.
Discussion followed regarding the default rate and the proposed interlocal. Chairman
Quinn inquired again if Commissioner Pridgeon was in agreement to include this in his
motion. Commissioner Pridgeon agreed. Chairman Quinn called for public comment.
There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously.

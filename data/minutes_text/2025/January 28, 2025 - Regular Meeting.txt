– Guarantee & Appropriate a Salary to the Gulf County Tax Collector, as
follows:
RESOLUTION NO. 2025-08
WHEREAS, Section 145-022, Florida Statues provides that the
Board of County Commissioners, with the concurrence of the County
Official as provided for and in said Section shall be resolution guarantee
and appropriate a salary to the County Official in an amount not to exceed
that specified in said Chapter; and

WHEREAS, the Tax Collector has requested and concurred with the
Board of County Commissioners that said Tax Collector be placed on a
salary as provided in said Section; and
WHEREAS, the said Tax Collector will pay all fees collected by said
Office to the Board of County Commissioners of Gulf County as provided
for in said Section;
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED;
1. That the Board of County Commissioners of Gulf County, Florida pays
the Tax Collector of said County a guaranteed salary as provided for in
Section 145.022, Florida Statues.
2. That said Board will provide funds according to the budget as submitted
by said Tax Collector and approved by the Board, with will include each
and every item of expenditures necessary to operate said Office,
including the salary of the Tax Collector.
3. The said Board will pay over said funds to the Tax Collector as provided
by law or upon monthly requisition.
4. That a copy of this Resolution be filed with the Florida Department of
Banking and the Auditor General.
ADOPTED this 28th day of January 2025
(End)

APPROPRIATIONS REQUEST
County Administrator Hammond presented page 9 of the packet; being an
appropriations request. Commissioner Pridgeon asked that this topic be tabled.
Commissioner Pridgeon then discussed a workshop held at the Honeyville
Community Center where the Board discussed the floating dry dock and the
airport. He requested the Board wait to submit the appropriations request until after
members of the Board can hold a meeting with the Port Authority to discuss the
potential of a marine tech center at the floating dry dock site. Commissioner
Husband discussed tying an education element to the request, suggesting the
proposed technical school be placed at the airport site, not at the Port. E.D.C.
Director McKnight appeared before the Board to discuss the current proposal.
Following discussion, Commissioner McCroan noted he is in support of specialty
trades. County Administrator Hammond stated an easy use of Triumph funds is for
education, noting it does not hurt to ask for State funding also. He discussed
getting Franklin County to join and share their pot of money for the floating dry
dock project. Commissioner Pridgeon stated he cannot support the airport request
at this point (included in the appropriations request). Following discussion about
adding a State-of-the-art Emergency Management Center, County Administrator
Hammond discussed reaching out to the consultant to add this in the plan and
getting a grant to build emergency services at the airport, noting D.O.T. will not
fund the building of emergency services. Following discussion regarding Federal

funds, keeping the area industrial/mixed commercial, and the location,
Commissioner Pridgeon inquired if Tyndal Air Force Base is in support of the
proposed airport. County Administrator Hammond reported no letter of objection
has been filed with the FAA, noting there will be no interference by Tyndal Air Force
Base. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Pridgeon regarding a buffer along the canal
(Intercoastal Waterway), County Administrator Hammond discussed land use
change and parcels already in use. Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn regarding the
recommendation on page 9, County Administrator Hammond discussed adding the
Emergency Operations Building to the master plan in year three or four.
Commissioner Husband inquired if there was enough room at the airport site for
an E.O.C. building and a technical training center. County Administrator Hammond
stated yes, noting there is 1,000 acres at this location. County Consultant Yeager
appeared before the Board to state he is working with the consultant (CHA
Consulting, Inc.) on design, suggesting the Board hold a workshop in the next few
weeks for a presentation. Commissioner McCroan noted the $10 million
appropriations request for the breakwaters project. County Administrator
Hammond stated the water is in the Trust Fund. Discussion followed regarding
funding for moving the water plant. Commissioner Husband motioned to approve
as shown on page 9. Commissioner McLemore seconded the motion. Discussion
followed by members of the Board to add to the request an E.O.C. building and a
technical school. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Debrah Mayes, of
Jones Homestead appeared before the Board to discuss her concerns regarding
the airport, St. Joe Company donating the land to the County, use of D.O.T. funds,
and inquiring whether St. Joe Company will run the airport. County Attorney Novak
discussed the FOB and functioning as an airport, noting the County owns the land
with a 50-year lease to St. Joe Company. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. Francine Vellanti, of Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to
discuss her concerns regarding the airport, noting she is not in support. Chairman
Quinn called for any additional public comment. There being none, the motion
passed unanimously.

GRANT INFORMATION – CAPE SAN BLAS
President of Coastal Community Association, Pat Hardman appeared before the Board
to report that CBRA (Coastal Barrier Resources Act) should not be the blame for not
receiving Federal funds per FWC, noting Federal funding is available because the
restoration had already been done to the barrier line, and the second reason you can get
federal money without CBRA being the holdup is if anything is done for resiliency of
wildlife corridors, habitat, structures, and endangered species. She noted the Cape has
three endangered species, mice, turtles and shore birds. Ms. Hardman reported that FWC
has found two grants the County can apply for immediately, noting a FWC representative
is willing to assist the County with the grant language regarding CBRA. Assistant County
Administrator Smallwood discussed applying for a FWC grant. County Administrator
Hammond noted the County is aware of the F.E.M.A. exceptions, reporting that F.E.M.A.
has rejected the County’s requests for the beach renourishment project. He noted with
the new administration, the County will try again but the other funding is a no, unless the
Board approves it. County Administrator Hammond continued to state this funding
requires the County to designate the area as habitat and restrict public use and beach
driving. Discussion followed regarding applying for funds. County Attorney Novak
discussed the 15 years the County has been trying to get out of CBRA.

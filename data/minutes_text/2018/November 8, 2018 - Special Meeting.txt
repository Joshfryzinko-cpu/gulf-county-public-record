BUDGET REDUCTIONS
County Administrator Hammond presented to the Board an expenditure and revenue
reductions report <copy provided to the Clerk for the record>. He stated that on October
1, 2019, without any changes, the County will have a deficit of $3.2 million; reporting that
to soften the blow of extremely hard decisions next year, the County needs to make some
decisions this year. County Administrator Hammond proposed that the Board implement
the following: 1) a 4% (four percent) reduction for all Constitutional Officers and Board of
County Commissioners budgets, except outside contracts; 2) outside agencies would be
cut 10% (ten percent), except for the Florida Department of Health, which would stay at
4% (four percent); 3) allow the County Administrator lee-way on certain specific budgets
under the Board’s line item, such as Courthouse utilities or the Medical Examiner
Contract. He stated the bottom line would be a reduction of $800,000.00; reporting that
the County is not anticipating laying off any current employees, but the County is not filling
vacant positions or rehiring employees that retire or quit. County Administrator Hammond
requested a vote of the Board to cut the Constitutional Officers and Board budgets by 4%
(four percent) across the board and all other outside agencies, other than the Florida
Department of Health, by 10% (ten percent), effective immediately. Commissioner Rich

made the motion. Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion for discussion. After
discussion by members of the Board, Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There
being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously. Upon request by County
Administrator Hammond, Commissioner McDaniel motioned to allow County
Administrator Hammond the authority to make further cuts, as he deems necessary, to
all Board and Administrative Budgets. Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment, the motion
passed unanimously. County Administrator Hammond requested Board approval to allow
the County, and potentially the City of Port St. Joe, to make a joint application for: 1) stop
gap funding up to ten (10) years for lost ad valorem tax rate; and 2) request for the
Triumph Board to pay for the water and sewer tap/impact fees for residents that are
setting up temporary housing and for businesses. Commissioner McCroan made the
motion. Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion. After discussion, the motion passed
unanimously.
HURRICANE DAMAGE COUNTY BUILDINGS
County Administrator Hammond reported that Risk Management Director Lowry has
several forms that the Chairman needs to sign regarding vehicles and a fire truck that
were damaged by Hurricane Michael <copy provided to the Clerk for the record>. He
requested a motion from the Board to junk these vehicles. Commissioner McDaniel so
moved. Commissioners Rogers seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously. County
Planner Collinsworth provided a damage report to the Board regarding County buildings;
suggesting that staff create a list of all the buildings, placing them in priority of which ones
needs to be worked first, as the insurance money comes in. County Administrator
Hammond discussed the damage at the Old Gulf County (Wewahitchka) Courthouse;
reporting that the County currently has a lease with North Florida Child Development. He
recommended the Board: 1) terminate the lease immediately; 2) cut the power (to the Old
Gulf County (Wewahitchka Courthouse); 3) cord-off the building and get those working in
the building out; and 4) still offer to transfer the (Wewahitchka Courthouse) building to
that non-profit. Commissioner McDaniel requested that the Board postpone a decision to
allow for further review. He then motioned to delay a vote on this matter until the regular
November meeting. Commissioner Rich seconded the motion. Commissioner McCroan
discussed his concerns regarding people being in an unsafe building. Commissioner
McDaniel agreed to modify his motion. After discussion regarding the condition of the
building, Chairman Quinn inquired of Commissioner McDaniel’s modified motion.
Commissioner McDaniel agreed to modify his motion to table this matter until the regular
November meeting, but to go forward with vacating the tenants. Chairman Quinn called
for public comment. Veterans' Service Officer & S.H.I.P. Administrator Paul appeared
before the Board to request that Commissioner McDaniel add to his motion to cut off the
electricity. Commissioner McDaniel reported that the main power supply is located in the
basement which is under water. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being
no further public comment, the motion passed unanimously.

DEBRIS REMOVAL
County Administrator Hammond reported that there are two (2) different debris removal
groups, one (1) is a State controlled group for State Roads 71, 22 and 98, and DRC and
Ashbritt have all County roads, including private roads. County Assistant Administrator
Yeager called Danny Demidio with Ashbritt to appear before the Board to provide an
update. Danny Demidio, of Ashbritt, appeared before the Board to provide a debris
removal update. Allen Bush, of Metric Engineering appeared before the Board to discuss
the matter regarding houses being pushed to the right-of-way for pickup. County
Administrator Hammond reported that the County has been told that if it is in the right-of-
way, then it could be picked up, but the County has also been told it is illegal for someone
that has a house that is damaged to hire a contractor to tear the house down and then
place it on the right-of-way; stating this is illegal and the County will not be reimbursed by
F.E.M.A. (Federal Emergency Management Agency). He stated that is what the County
has been going with and telling residents; requesting Mr. Bush to tell the County if this
information is correct. Mr. Bush stated that this matter is part of the hold up; reporting that
F.E.M.A. does not like to pay for things that have already been paid for once. He stated
that if a homeowner received funds from their insurance company to have the structure
demolished and a contractor pushes the debris to the right-of-way, this could be ineligible
for reimbursement from F.E.M.A. Commissioner McCroan inquired if the contractors
currently have a place to haul the debris. Following discussion, Mr. Bush discussed
approaching F.E.M.A. to ask for a specific and clear answer (regarding contractors
placing structure debris on the right-of-way). Commissioner McCroan again inquired if a
contractor has a place to dump the debris. County Planner Collinsworth discussed his
concerns regarding some of the agreements with contractors include debris removal and
others do not. County Attorney Novak discussed the differences between right-of-way
and right-of-entry; stating that a right-of-entry requires an instrument allowing contractors
to come onto private property. After discussion by County Attorney Novak, County
Administrator Hammond stated that staff will request an answer from F.E.M.A, but until
the County receives an answer; a contractor dumping debris on the side of the road is a
felony. Mr. Bush requested that debris not be placed in black bags, as these items will
not be picked up due to not being able to see what is inside these bags. Chairman Quinn
inquired as to where contractors go to dispose of the debris. County Administrator
Hammond stated that they would have to haul it to Blountstown or to an approved lay-
down yard; reporting that the County is not in the landfill business and Waste Pro is
supposed to take it to the transfer station, but the charge is $65.00 per ton and $9.00 per
ton at the landfill in Calhoun County.

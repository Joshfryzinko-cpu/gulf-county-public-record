ORDINANCE – LEAVE NO TRACE
County Administrator Hammond discussed the complaints received regarding leave no
trace over the last two (2) weeks; stating he needs direction from the Board. After

Recreational Director Traylor appeared before the Board to provide a status report
regarding trash and debris collected from the beaches and parks over a period of time.
He also discussed concerns regarding the enforcement of this ordinance. T.D.C.
Executive Director Godwin appeared before the Board to state that if the Board wishes
for the T.D.C. staff to enforce the (Leave No Trace) Ordinance, then there needs to be
discussion regarding staffing or contracting this service out. After discussion, County
Administrator Hammond recommended taking out about 30-40% (thirty-fourth percent) of
the unenforceable area and narrowing the scope. After discussion by County Attorney
Novak and upon recommendation by County Administrator Hammond, Commissioner
McDaniel motioned to advertise to hold a Public Hearing at the next regular Board
meeting to consider changes to the Leave No Trace Ordinance. Commissioner Rich
seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Pat Hardman,
President of the Coastal Community Association appeared before the Board to discuss
concerns regarding the Leave No Trace Ordinance; stating for the record that the Coastal
Community Association has never opposed beach driving. She also stated that they do
support the enforcement of a leave no trace (ordinance). Upon motion by Commissioner
McCroan, second by Commissioner McDaniel, and unanimous vote, the Board agreed to
extend Ms. Hardman’s time to speak. Ms. Hardman discussed some of the problems and
the need to find solutions. Upon request by County Administrator Hammond, County
Attorney Novak discussed the executive order that provides further direction on House
Bill 631. After further discussion, Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Randy
Pridgeon, of Beacon Hill appeared before the Board stating that the new Law (HB 631)
creates confusion. John Fadio, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to discuss his
concerns regarding County staff not being able to get on the beaches until after the turtle
patrol has gone through. Upon motion by McCroan, second by Commissioner Rogers,
and unanimous vote, the Board extended Mr. Fadio’s time to speak. Mr. Fadio continued
discussing his concerns regarding staff getting on the beach sooner to enforce leave no
trace. County Attorney Novak read aloud the Governor’s Executive Order. Chairman
Quinn called for public comment. Patrick Foy, of Indian Pass appeared before the Board
and inquired about the redlining of the proposed changes to the Ordinance and who will
have input to the redlining. County Attorney Novak stated that County Administrator
Hammond will consult with staff and the concerns from each Board member and bring
back a draft at the next meeting for consideration. Mr. Foy inquired if there could be public
input before the redlining is completed. County Administrator Hammond suggested the
Board hold a workshop prior to the regular Board meeting to obtain public input. After
discussion, Chairman Quinn called for additional public comment. There being no public

comment, the motion passed unanimously. Commissioner McCroan motioned to hold a
workshop a week before the regular Board meeting to discuss Leave No Trace.
Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment.

ORDINANCE – RECREATIONAL VEHICLE
County Administrator Hammond requested direction from the Board regarding the RV
(Recreational Vehicle) Ordinance; reporting that staff has received complaints. He stated
that this Ordinance has had five (5) declarations and no enforcement; reporting that
people are not complying with the permits or the evacuation orders. County Administrator
Hammond discussed his concerns regarding Oak Grove, the sections within the County
that are excluded under the RV (Recreational Vehicle) Ordinance, and the exemptions.
County Administrator Hammond continued to discuss the Oak Grove Subdivision and the
exemptions within the Ordinance; recommending that the Board go back to the original
Ordinance, which was one (RV) per lot. Discussion followed. Upon inquiry by
Commissioner McCroan, County Attorney Novak reported that under the present
Ordinance, within a mile of the coast, effective open water areas, excluding Simons
Bayou, Highland View, and Oak Grove, within a mile of the coastline RVs (recreational
vehicles) are prohibited for anyone for more than two (2) stays of a seven (7) day period
over the course of one (1) year, if you purchased your property after January, 2016. If the
property owner purchased the property prior to January, 2016, then they can obtain a
permit from the County and leave the RV (recreational vehicle) on the property year
round, and it can be passed down to the next generation through deed, as an exception.
After discussion by County Administrator Hammond, Planner Collinsworth recommended
that the Board revert to one (1) (recreational vehicle) per parcel. After discussion by
members of the Board, County Administrator Hammond recommended that the Board
hold a public hearing to strike this Ordinance and go back to the County’s Comprehensive
Plan and LDRs (Land Development Regulations) which allows one (1) per parcel,
Commissioner McDaniel motioned to hold a Public Hearing at the next regular Board
meeting to abolish the RV (Recreational Vehicle) Ordinance. Commissioner Rich
seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Linda Sertich, of
Beacon Hill appeared before the Board to discuss keeping the Ordinance. Chairman
Quinn called for any additional public comment. There being no public comment, the
motion passed unanimously.

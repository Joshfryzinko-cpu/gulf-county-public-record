APRIL 11, 2018
JOINT WORKSHOP
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in a joint workshop with
the Port St. Joe City Commissioners with the following members present: Chairman
Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Jimmy Rogers, and Commissioners Ward McDaniel
and Commissioner Phil McCroan <Commissioner David C. Rich was absent>.
Port St. Joe City Commissioners and Staff present were: City Mayor Bo Patterson, City
Commissioners Eric Langston, David Ashbrook, Brett Lowry and Rex Buzzett, City
Manager Jim Anderson and Clerk Charlotte Pierce.
Others present were: County Attorney Jeremy Novak, Clerk Director of Finance &
Management Rhonda Woodward, Clerk Budget & Finance Officer Sherry Herring,
Deputy Clerk Leanna Roberts, County Administrator Michael L. Hammond, Deputy
Administrator Kari Summers, Central Services Director Lynn Lanier, Gulf County E.D.C.
Director Warren Yeager, Jr., Parks & Recreational Director Billy Traylor,
Dewberry/County Engineer Clay Smallwood, III, Public Works & Mosquito Control
Director Mark Cothran, Sheriff's Department Deputy Chase Harvey, T.D.C. Executive
Director Kelli Godwin, and Veterans' Service Officer & S.H.I.P. Administrator Joe Paul.
Chairman Quinn called the meeting to order at 6:00 p.m., E.T.
Chairman Quinn opened the meeting with prayer, and led the Pledge of Allegiance to
the Flag.
RESTORE UPDATE
E.D.C. Director Yeager began by stating his discussion will mainly address funds that
earmarked for the City projects. He discussed Pot I Projects; stating the County’s five
(5) year plan has been submitted to the U.S. Treasury for review. He reported that
following the review, the County can begin making application for the projects. E.D.C.
Director Yeager stated that the County’s priority project is beach restoration. Projects for
the City include: (1) Beacon Hill Septic to Sewer; a $2.6 to $2.7 million dollar project
with $500,000.00 funded through Pot I, and the remainder from Pot III; (2) $1 million
from Pot I for Port St. Joe Sewer rehab along Long Avenue, and within District IV.
E.D.C. Director Yeager discussed timeframes and the need to meet again to discuss
their project priority needs. He then noted there is a little over $2.3 million planned to go
toward Port St. Joe sewer upgrades and rehab from Pot III. E.D.C. Director Yeager
explained the differences with Pot I and Pot III funds and who has approval authority
over each.
SEWER PROJECTS – GULF AIRE/SEASHORES & JONES HOMESTEAD

E.D.C. Director Yeager noted dollars committed to the Jones Homestead Sewer and
Gulf Aire projects; requesting City Manager Anderson for an update. City Manager
Anderson reported that the Jones Homestead Project is a Legislative appropriation in
the amount of $250,000.00, through D.E.P. (Department of Environmental Protection);
stating the City has already gone out for bids on material, and for the purchase of 65
(sixty-five) grinder stations. After discussion, City Manager Anderson reported this
project is scheduled to begin the summer of 2018. E.D.C. Director Yeager stated the
County is going to see if they can help residents get on the system by alleviating some
of the expense through Pot III money. Upon inquiry by Mayor Patterson, E.D.C. Director
Yeager reported that there is $1 million in Pot I and $2.3 million in Pot III for (Port St.
Joe) sewer rehab, and about $2.1 million for Beacon Hill septic to sewer. Upon inquiry
by City Commissioner Buzzett, E.D.C. Director Yeager discussed the phases for each
project and the sequence for the release of funds. Following discussion, County
Administrator Hammond reported that the quicker money is going to be from Pot I;
noting the money currently in the County’s account, $250,000.00 has been set aside for
the Gulf Aire purchase if the City of Port St. Joe decides to do that. He stated that
moneys from Pot III could be years out. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McCroan, City
Manager Anderson reported that the City of Port St. Joe applied to D.E.P. (Department
of Environmental Protection) for a $2.7 million sewer project that would include Long
Avenue and a new lift station; stating that on the water side, with materials and the City
trying to do the work themselves; about $500,000.00, and the road bond money. He
reported that the City already has a commitment on the water side; stating the City has
a 60/40 grant to loan from the SRF (State Revolving Fund), $300,000.00 on the grant
and $200,000.00 on loan for water, but they do not have the money or commitment on
the sewer as of yet.
ROAD BOND CLOSEOUT
County Administrator Hammond reported, based on discussions with Bond Counsel, the
money has to be committed to the project and moving forward by June 30, 2018. He
noted possible penalties and negative bond ratings when bond funds are not spent
within the appropriate timelines. Commissioner McCroan asked if starting the
engineering qualified for starting the project. County Administrator Hammond reported
that according to Bond Counsel, the City of Port St. Joe must be under contract by June
30th. City Commissioner Buzzett asked what happens to the $1.1 million if the City of
Port St. Joe is unable to get under contract. County Administrator Hammond noted, in
the past, the County would have to send back any interest earned on that money, and
the unspent proceeds would be applied toward the bond principal. Upon further inquiry
by City Commissioner Buzzett, County Administrator Hammond stated that Bond
Counsel has not given an actual answer as to what we have to do in this instance other
than it will be in violation of Federal Law, and it will affect the County’s and City’s ability
to borrow money in the future. He also stated the County is in hopes the City would pick
other projects to use it on before the deadline. Following inquiry by Commissioner
McCroan regarding possible other projects, Clerk Budget & Finance Officer Herring
appeared before the Board to state that if the funding is not available to pursue the
project as desired due to the timeframe, then the direction would be for another qualified
project within the covenants of the bond. City Manager Anderson asked if the City could

go ahead and get the funds and place them in a non-interest bearing account until such
time as they were able to secure adequate funds to complete the project (Long
Avenue). Clerk Budget & Finance Officer Herring reported that would not be permissible
under the bond; the City must be committed and under contract within the next few
months. Discussion continued regarding the condition of Long Avenue and possible
solutions to get a project under contract. County Administrator Hammond stated the
County needs the City’s plan quickly that follows the rules of the bond. City Manager
Anderson discussed portions of Reid Avenue or its alleyway as a possible solution.
DOWNTOWN REDEVELOPMENT AGENCY – 30 YEARS ENDING 2019
County Administrator Hammond reported that the CRA (Community Redevelopment
Agency) under the PSJDRA (Port St. Joe Downtown Redevelopment Agency) will end
2019; stating the County would object if the City of Port St. Joe tried to extend or do a
new CRA. He stated that this year, the County’s commitment was $167,000.00. Upon
inquiry by City Commissioner Buzzett, County Administrator Hammond stated that past
Boards have been on record in opposition, and after speaking with the current Board
members individually, there is a consensus to oppose the CRA/DRA extension. County
Attorney Novak discussed the laws to opening up discussions to extend the CRA and
the steps the City would take if it was their desire to extend it. City Commissioner
Buzzett noted the City nor the County paid into the CRA for the first ten (10) years.
County Administrator Hammond agreed, but noted that up to 1997, the area didn’t
generate much of anything. County Attorney Novak stated the CRA, for renewal, must
abide by the law as to the necessity. He noted there must be a real evaluation for
necessity; having a plan that the City and County would look at and do an analysis of
the data.
10TH STREET PARK
Chairman Quinn discussed the proposed plan with the 5th (fifth) penny; stating instead
of building a sports complex near the college there was a plan to use these funds to
enhance the 10th Street Park, not to harm property values; stating nothing has been
done except to put a plan to paper. Commissioner McDaniel discussed the Tourist
Development Fifth (5th) cent bed tax adopted for Parks and Recreation. He stated the
County does not own the 10th Street Park, they are only here to support it. City
Commissioner Lowry reported the City Board voted 5-0 to move forward with working
on a plan for the 10th Street area, but the City Board does not agree with closing two (2)
city roads. Upon inquiry by City Commissioner Buzzett, County Attorney Novak stated
that $980,000.00 is the estimated budget for this project. County Administrator
Hammond reported that $8,000.00 has been spent on planning. City Commissioner
Buzzett discussed the conceptual plan of the park, noting that $1 million would not be
enough. He stated they need to just improve the existing footprint. County
Commissioner McCroan discussed briefly the history of the park, noting there was no
intent to cause a disruption, but only to improve the facility. City Commissioner
Ashbrook expressed concerns over future maintenance cost; noting it would be great to
have a nice park, but not if you can’t maintain it going forward; agreeing the best thing
to do is improve on what they have. Commissioner McCroan stated the piping under the
ballfields must be replaced regardless of any park improvements. City Manager

Anderson stated the City is working with the Water Management District and
Department of Environmental Protection on possible funding ($1 million) for the pipe
replacement; noting they should know something in the Spring (2019). County
Commissioner Rogers asked that they not forget about the Stack House and the
unused park across the street from it, as they develop their plan. County Attorney
Novak talked about the T.D.C. funds; reiterating they are not ad valorem tax dollars and
the need for both Commissions to identify and recognize that. He noted their primary
use is to attract people to Gulf County so that it serves a lawful purpose for the use of
those funds. County Attorney Novak advised that the County Commission has the final
say and determination on the lawful use of those funds and the City would have to
satisfy that requirement to be able to use the T.D.C. funds. After further discussion,
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Christy McElroy, Caroline Williams, A.W.
Rogers, Beverly Maddox and Jill Bebee (presented a hand-out to the Board and a
petition <copy of both on file in the Clerk’s Office>), Tommy Lander, Robert Branch,
Clare Morris, and Robert Farmer all appeared before the Board to discuss their
concerns regarding the proposed 10th Street Park Project.
There being no further business, the meeting did then adjourn at 7:29 p.m., E.T.

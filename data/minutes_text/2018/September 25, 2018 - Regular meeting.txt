12. Resolution – FL Department of Health (Fee Schedule * FY2018-19), as follows:
RESOLUTION NO. 2018-42
WHEREAS, it is the duty of the Board of County Commissioners of
Gulf County, Florida to establish fees for services provided by the Florida
Department of Health in Gulf County; and
WHEREAS, fees for services may be waived for those below 100%
of the current poverty level and fees may be waived at the discretion of the
County Health Department Administrator for those experiencing financial
hardships, except as noted in the fee schedule; and
WHEREAS, any client of the Florida Department of Health in Gulf
County and their subcontractors shall not be denied tuberculosis treatment,
sexually transmitted disease treatment, pregnancy testing, HIV/AIDS
counseling and testing, or communicable disease control services for failure
or inability to pay a prescribed fee, regardless of income; and
WHEREAS, the County Health Department Administrator may
negotiate additional fees for services with other providers, agencies or
entities in order to provide education, lab services, vaccinations, or other
services provided at worksites, community health fairs, or in conjunction
with grant awards; and
WHEREAS, the County Health Department Administrator may
minimally increase/add a fee during the course of the year, upon written
notification of the Board of County Commissioners of Gulf County;
THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the Board of County
Commissioners of Gulf County, Florida hereby adopts the FY2018-19
Environmental Health and Medical Clinic fee schedules for the Florida
Department of Health in Gulf County.

THIS RESOLUTION ADOPTED this 25th day of September, 2018

WINDMARK PIER NORTH – ST. JOE COMPANY
E.D.C. Director Yeager reported that D.E.P. (Department of Environmental Protection)
has been working on the design of the Windmark Pier (North); stating that there is not
enough funds in the request (N.R.D.A. Project) to build the pier. He stated that D.E.P.
suggests the County either; (1) leave the funds and try to find different pots of money; or
(2) turn down the $1.3 million and reapply through N.R.D.A. and request the $3.5 million
that is expected to cover the cost of this project. E.D.C. Director Yeager recommended
that the Board release the $1.3 million back into the N.R.D.A. (Natural Resource Damage
Assessment) Program and then submit another application for $3.5 million for the full
funding cost of the pier. Commissioner Rogers made the motion. Commissioner McCroan
seconded the motion. After discussion, Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There
being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously.

PROBLEMS WITH THE BOCC
Roland Wilson, of Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to give County Administrator
Hammond a partial apology; reporting that he did go back six (6) weeks in the newspaper
and did find one County public notice. He discussed his concerns regarding the County
not placing public notices in the newspaper. Mr. Wilson asked for an update regarding
County-wide voting; stating that he would like to see something done to progress County-
wide voting. He inquired about what can be done to get two (2) meetings per month, as
in the past; requesting that one (1) of those meetings be held in the evening at 6:30 p.m.
to allow the people an opportunity to attend a meeting. County Administrator Hammond
stated, regarding the County advertisements, the County meets the same time every
month all year round for regular meetings. He reported that it is impossible due to the
deadline of a weekly paper to get special meeting board notices in; stating that the County
live streams the meetings, the public can view the meetings on the County’s website, and
the Clerk has a huge emailing list that is sent out to notify the public of special meetings.
County Administrator Hammond reported that the minutes stop being printed in the paper
because they stopped being free and the cost of full pages in the paper would cost the
County thousands’ dollars a month to put minutes in the paper; stating that the minutes
are available on the County’s website. He stated that the County hired a law firm, the
County spent $80,000.00-$90,000.00 to do a study, and the report showed that the
County would lose the challenge; reporting the Board voted unanimously not to pursue
after receiving the report. Commissioner McCroan inquired if the case would have to go
back before the Judge that signed the decree, County Attorney Novak stated that the
jurisdiction and order to Gulf County is through Federal Court; reporting in order to amend
that order it would have to go back through the same process under the Federal Courts.

LOCAL STATE OF EMERGENCY – HURRICANE IAN
County Administrator Hammond reported that Emergency Management Director Herring
has a 2:00 p.m. update regarding Hurricane Ian and after the update, he will be asking
the Board to declare a Local State of Emergency. Emergency Management Director
Herring appeared before the Board to report that the next advisory will be in one hour,
stating that at the 2:00 p.m. advisory it was discussed that this is a major hurricane making
landfall Thursday or early Friday between Tampa and Steinhatchee (Florida). He stated
that it is time that the Board consider declaring a Local State of Emergency. County
Administrator Hammond reported that T.D.C. has picked up the mobi-mats on the Cape
and Indian Pass and are removing the restroom facility at Indian Pass Boat Ramp. He
stated that he sees no need to do any evacuations but thinks it prudent to move if you are
in the RV (recreational vehicle) Park at the end of Indian Pass or across from the Indian
Pass Raw Bar. County Administrator Hammond reported that based on historical
knowledge it appears that Indian Lagoon will overrun the road and backup catching water.
He recommended that the Board not do any evacuation (orders) that would require people
to move their RVs from the RV Zone/Protection Zone, but prudence would dictate that in
those two RV Parks, that they get out on their own. County Administrator Hammond

reported with the projected route of the storm, Gulf County’s biggest problem will be
surge, especially at Indian Pass, on the outside beaches. Discussion followed regarding
the projected category 4 hurricane. County Administrator Hammond recommended the
Board call for a Local State of Emergency, reporting that the Governor (Ron DeSantis)
and U.S. President (Joe Biden) have issued a State of Emergency for all 67 Counties.
County Attorney Novak read the proposed local state of emergency for the Board to
consider. Commissioner Rich so moved. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being none, the motion passed
unanimously (4-0). County Administrator Hammond stated that if the Board has no
objection, he will leave it up to Emergency Management Director Herring to mobilize
E.O.C. to go to a different category. He requested that the Chairman recess this meeting
in case staff needs to call the Board back. Discussion followed regarding erosion at the
Cape and Stumphole. Commissioner McCroan thanked Public Works for working on
getting the ditches cleaned out at Jones Homestead and Indian Pass. County
Administrator Hammond reported that Emergency Management Director Herring will
provide another update at tomorrow’s regular Board meeting. Chairman Quinn called for
public comment. Coastal Community Association President Pat Hardman appeared
before the Board to inquire if staff is anticipating that Indian Pass Road will be washed
over, and a detour will be needed; requesting staff to look at placing some rock at the
road at Indian Summer Cottages (detour route). Chairman Quinn called for additional
public comment. There was no further public comment.
The meeting recessed at 4:10 p.m., E.T.

BEACH DRIVING PERMITS – GULF COUNTY TAX COLLECTOR
County Administrator Hammond stated that the Gulf County Tax Collector notified him of
an issue with fake beach driving permits, reporting that people are purchasing one permit,
making color copies, and leaving them in rental houses for customers to use. He
discussed his concerns regarding this matter, suggesting the Board agree to canceling
the permit and banning the homeowner from purchasing another permit for a period of
time. County Administrator Hammond requested direction from the Board regarding this
matter, inquiring if the Board would like to review the option of limiting the purchase of
out-of-county permits in the future. Discussion followed regarding the fake permits,
penalties, limited permits issued to out-of-county, and a three-tier fee system. County
Administrator Hammond stated that he will bring back a recommendation for a three-tier
system and penalty phase for the Board to consider. Members of the Board discussed

their concerns regarding the fake beach driving permits. Upon inquiry by Commissioner
McCroan, County Attorney Novak discussed the enforcement mechanism in the current
Beach Driving Ordinance (#2022-13), stating it is the same as driving on the roads with
fake tags, therefore the County has the Florida law in that regard, but can also withhold
the beach driving. Discussion followed regarding the proposed three-tier system and the
rules regarding a beach driving permit purchase.

FUNDING – GULF COUNTY LIBRARY
Commissioner McDaniel requested that County Administrator Hammond clarify how the
money is distributed for the Library. County Administrator Hammond reported that for at
least the last thirty (30) years, Gulf County has not ever written a check to the Port St.
Joe or Wewahitchka Libraries, stating the County has a multi-County contract with Bay
County Public Library System. He stated that the employees of the Libraries have never
been Gulf County employees, reporting the County funds approximately $109,000.00 in
County tax funds and receives a State matching grant for a total of $160,000.00 to
$170,000.00 per year to this library system. County Administrator Hammond also
reported that the maintenance of the Wewahitchka building, is owned and maintained by
the City of Wewahitchka, and Gulf County owns the Port St. Joe building as it is on Gulf
County property. He stated that Gulf County provides maintenance, (property) insurance,
replaced the roof after Hurricane Michael, janitorial services, and cuts the grass at the
Port St. Joe Library. Commissioner McDaniel discussed the role of Friends of the Library,
stating that this organization has nothing to do with the County.

OLD GULF COUNTY COURTHOUSE - WEWAHITCHKA
Commissioner McDaniel stated for the record that the last filing cabinet was removed
from the Old Gulf County Courthouse located in Wewahitchka. He discussed reaching
out to the City of Wewahitchka, Historical (Society) group to try and find a home for this
building. Commissioner McDaniel discussed the prior lease agreement with North Florida
Head Start and the agreement being dissolved due to Hurricane Michael. He reported
that the Old Gulf County Courthouse was the first roof replaced after Hurricane Michael.
Commissioner McDaniel stated that he had a team go up last week to inspect the building,
requesting a status report of their findings. County Administrator Hammond stated that
he, Assistant County Administrator Smallwood, Public Works Director Cothran, and
Assistant Public Works Director Hart went inside and discovered there are no windows
open in the Old Courthouse, excluding the jail, no leaks, the drop ceiling in the Courtroom
is exposed but is completely dry, and there is a door on the Southside that needs to be
replaced as the windows were knocked out. He stated that they shut the door between
the Courtroom and the back part (the jail), reporting the back part is a disaster and didn’t
venture in the old jail section. County Administrator Hammond noting that some of the
windows (old jail section) are open and there is a hole in the top where the (radio) tower
went through and discussed looking at engaging engineers to draft a plan on the removal
of the old back part of the building. Commissioner McDaniel requested the support of the
Board, that the County reach out, calling Mrs. Johnson to appear before the Board. Ann
Johnson, President of the Wewahitchka Historical Society stepped forward.
Commissioner McDaniel then requested assistance from Mrs. Johnson in collecting
letters of support for the Board. Chairman Quinn discussed getting with the County’s grant
department. Mrs. Johnson stated the desire of the Wewahitchka Historical Society is to
help the County, agreeing to collect letters of support. Commissioner McDaniel called
Jack Husband, of Southeastern Consulting Engineers, Inc. to step forward. As Mr.
Husband appeared before the Board, Commissioner McDaniel asked him to work with
Assistant County Administrator Smallwood on the old jail section of the Old Gulf County
Courthouse and bring a plan back to the Board of what it would take to remove the back
section. Mr. Husband asked permission from the Board to allow Assistant Public Works
Director Hart to be included in this project. Commissioner McDaniel had no objection. He
discussed his concerns regarding the electrical system, reporting that it will have to come
up to code. Commissioner McDaniel also requested Mr. Husband to work on a plan for
the electrical. He then reached out to Grant Administrator Rhodes to look for any grants
for funding. Grant Administrator Rhodes agreed, stating that she will get something
together. Commissioner McDaniel then again requested support of the Board. Chairman
Quinn discussed grant options, suggesting that Commissioner McDaniel reach out to the
County’s Representatives for funding also. County Administrator Hammond discussed
drafting a cost estimate, which will include remediation, and then the Board would be able
to request funds from the Legislators. Commissioner Farrell made a motion of support.
Commissioner McCroan discussed the need for a plan for the long-term use of this
building, requesting that this be included in the proposal. Following discussion and upon
inquiry by Chairman Quinn, Commissioner Farrell stated his motion is to do a letter of
support. Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously.

STATUS REPORT – CODE ENFORCEMENT
Upon request by Commissioner Rich, Building Official Bailey provided a status report
regarding the blighted properties within Gulf County, reporting that 55 unsafe structures
have been torn down, 44 of those property owners complied after receiving notice from
the County, and the County went out for bid on the remaining eleven, which the contractor

did the work and liens have been placed on these properties. He stated that five more
have been added this month, reporting there are some homes in St. Joe Beach that are
still battling insurance and the County has given a lead-way, but it is coming down to
where something must be done. County Administrator Hammond stated that the City of
Wewahitchka is receiving a monthly report and this report can be provided to the Board
for review as well. He recapped the process, stating that the County has prequalified
contractors, each time a case is presented to the Special Magistrate, all prequalified
contractors have the opportunity to submit a sealed bid, and the opening (of the bids)
takes place in the Board room with an invitation open to the bidders to attend. County
Administrator Hammond discussed his concerns regarding the ones the County is tearing
down because the taxpayers must carry this bill for about eighteen months before it is
placed on the tax bill and then the sale of the certificate, stating that the threat of tear-
down seems to be working for the majority.

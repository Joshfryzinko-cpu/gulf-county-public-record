Chairman Quinn called the meeting to order at 5:01 p.m., E.T.
2022-23 BUDGET OVERVIEW
OMB Director Herring noted that all members of the Board have been provided with the
budget book for the tentative budget, which was adopted at the last Hearing, reporting
that this is the County’s Final Budget Hearing. She stated: 1) the tentative aggregate
millage rate is 6.8934, which is 13.76% above the current year aggregate roll-back of
6.0596; 2) the tentative aggregate millage rate includes the Countywide millage rate at
6.5 and each Dependent Fire District at .5 mills; and 3) the tentative M.S.T.U. voted debt
service millage rates as, Gulfside Beachfront at 0.6786, Gulfside Interior at 0.5689, and
Bayside at 0.5723. OMB Director Herring also stated these are the maximum millage
rates and this Hearing will require a roll call vote for the proposed resolutions. She
reported that the primary reasons for the ad valorem tax revenue increases are: 1)
Sheriff’s Office; 2) Clerk’s Office; 3) County Jail (Detention Facility); 4) FRS Retirement
Rate Cost; 5) Insurance Cost; 6) Employee Pay Raises; 7) Capital Outlay Projects; 8)
E.M.S. Services; and 9) Road and Street Facilities. OMB Director Herring then reported
that this budget book was posted on the County’s website, as required by TRIM, and also
the Final Hearing was advertised in The Star (newspaper) on September 22, 2022. She
stated that staff is requesting for this Final Budget Book be increased, as follows: 1)
General Fund by $55,200.00, affecting the Gulf County Sheriff’s Office law enforcement
contracts and expenditures; 2) Fund 103, affecting the State Ad for Mosquito Control,
Cash Carry Forward revenues and expenditures; 3) Fund 123, County Development
Fund, affecting Cash Carry Forward revenues, reserves and expenditures; 4) Fund 124,
Hospital Fund, affecting Cash Carry Forward revenues, reserves and expenditures; 5)
Fund 205, in the amount of $60,080.00, for Cash Carry Forward revenues and

expenditures; 6) line item adjustments in General Fund to account for the changes to the
Elected Officials Salary; 7) decrease change in funding Animal Control Services from the
City of Wewahitchka that will need to off-set Cash Carry Forward; and 8) any final line-
item adjustments that will not affect the total fund. OMB Director Herring also stated that
Gulf County Sheriff Harrison has a request to carry forward his funding, in the amount of
$83,000.00 from this year’s budget to next, reporting that $53,000.00 of these funds will
be for the purchase of new vehicles that were delayed due to production and $30,000.00
to be used towards the purchase of a new drone due to conflicting legal opinions this
purchase has not yet been made. She then requested Board approval, stating that these
adjustments will go into the final budget. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to approve
it. Commissioner McDaniel so moved. Commissioner Rich seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment, the motion
passed unanimously. OMB Director Herring reminded the Board that if there are any
changes to the proposed budget, she will need those offsetting changes. County
Administrator Hammond stated that the combined animal control and (St Joseph Bay)
Humane Society budget now exceeds $200,000.00 and he will have a recommendation
at tomorrow’s regular Board meeting regarding the City of Wewahitchka. Chairman Quinn
then opened the Public Hearing to the public. There being no public comment, Chairman
Quinn closed the Public Hearing to the public. Chairman Quinn inquired of the Elected
Officials present for comments; there were none.

2022-23 BUDGET OVERVIEW
County Administrator Hammond stated that by far this is the largest budget that this Board
has ever adopted, reporting that less than 20% of that is ad valorem taxes. He reported
that County Assistant Administrator Smallwood will provide the Board with an update at
tomorrow’s regular Board meeting on the number of grants received since Hurricane
Michael. County Administrator Hammond stated that he is proud of this budget, it’s a very
conservative and prudent budget but they were able to provide County employees a
significant pay raise, fund the Constitutional Officers’ request, and able to cut taxes. He
also stated that a member of the Board will need to volunteer to serve on the Budget
Committee (as Commissioner McDaniel will no longer be serving).

There being no further business, and upon motion by Commissioner McDaniel, second
by Commissioner McCroan, and unanimous vote, the meeting did then adjourn at 5:19
p.m., E.T.

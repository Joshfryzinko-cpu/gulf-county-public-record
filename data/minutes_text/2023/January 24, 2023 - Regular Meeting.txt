PROPOSED LDR CHANGES
County Administrator Hammond directed the Board to page 4 of the packet, noting staff
recommendations regarding changes to the LDR (Land Development Regulations): 1)
limit future density to one (1) unit per acre when changing from agricultural to residential;
2) eliminating high quality wetland credit for subdivision density; 3) requiring additional
off-street parking spaces for homes with greater than four (4) bedrooms; 4) send this to
the Planning Board (P.D.R.B.) for the next Public Hearing to take comment from the public
and add or subtract from these recommendations; and 5) place immediate moratorium
on land use changes from agriculture until the end of March 2023. He stated that if the
Board proceeds today, this will go before the P.D.R.B. at their February Public Hearing,
and recommendations will be made to the Board at the regular Board meeting in
February. County Administrator Hammond stated that if the Board chooses to move
forward at the February meeting, then staff will advertise for LDR and Comprehensive
Plan changes in March 2023. He stated this is a staff recommendation. Chairman Quinn
called for a motion to move forward with the LDR recommendations. Commissioner
McCroan so moved. Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn
called for public comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously (4-0).

COMPREHENSIVE PLAN AMENDMENT
Planner Crane discussed the proposed comprehensive plan amendment to add a
property rights element, which is required due to a new legislative mandate effective July
1, 2021, HB59. He stated Growth Management requires all local governments to have a
property rights element included in their Comprehensive Plan. County Administrator
Hammond reported HB59 is a State requirement. Planner Crane stated a copy of House
Bill 59 has been included in Board’s packet for review <copy on file with the Clerk for the
record> and a memorandum that outlays the four (4) items that are required by the State.
He reported that the County already considers the items in the decision-making, but now
these elements will be incorporated in the County Comprehensive Plan, reading aloud
the four elements as requested by the Chairman. Chairman Quinn then called for a motion
to add these amendments to our Comprehensive Plan. Commissioner Farrell so moved.
Commissioner Rich seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment.
There was no public comment, the motion passed unanimously (4-0). Sheila Homes, from
the audience requested to be acknowledged to speak. Chairman Quinn asked for County
Attorney guidance. County Attorney Novak inquired if she objects to the Quasi, stating

that she can come up to speak. Sheila Homes, of WindMark appeared before the Board
to inquire if the timing of this change has anything to do with the natural gas facility that
has been planned and talked about in the area. County Administrator Hammond stated it
does not, reporting the County can only amend the Comprehensive Plan twice a year and
there was not enough time for this change at the end of last year, so it is taking place this
month. He reported this is a formal requirement regarding private property rights
mandated by the Legislator and has nothing to do with LNG (liquified natural gas). Tom
Coates, of Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to request clarification on the
private property of the St. Joe Paper Company, and if there is a difference between
residential and commercial regarding the property rights element. County Administrator
Hammond said no, discussing the Florida Legislator is talking about taking, the Board has
to take in account before changing the rules what it is going to do to the property owner.
Chairman Quinn called for additional public comment. Rob Fisher, of Indian Pass
appeared before the Board to inquire if these elements will bootstrap the Board so they
cannot change regulations in the future. County Administrator Hammond said no, but the
Board has to take into account what it will do to the property owner and if it is ‘a taking’,
then the government will have to compensate the property owner. He reported that
amendments to the County Comprehensive Plan do not affect either City (Municipalities),
stating that this Board can not do anything about the St. Joe Company property as it is
inside the city limits. County Attorney Novak stated that these property right elements is
already State law. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. Pat Hardman, of Cape San
Blas appeared before the Board to discuss her concerns regarding the Board putting
themselves in a box, stating South Gulf County needs a plan to manage the growth.
Planner Crane encouraged the public to read House Bill 59, stating that it is asking in the
County Comprehensive Plan that the Board and staff will consider these four items in the
decision-making; it does not lock the Board down to anything. He reported that every
County and City in the State of Florida is required by July 2024 or by the next
Comprehensive Plan amendment to adopt at a minimum these four items and there is
nothing in these elements that locks the Board down to anything. Chairman Quinn called
for any additional public comment. There was no further public comment. Commissioner
Farrell inquired of a need for a vote. Chairman Quinn stated that the vote was carried
before the public appeared before the Board. Chairman Quinn called for public comment.
There being none, the motion passed unanimously (4-0).

PUBLIC HEARING – COMPREHENSIVE PLAN AMENDMENT
County Attorney introduced a proposed ordinance regarding the amendment of the
Comprehensive Plan for a small-scale land use map amendment for Parcel ID #01037-
020R. He reported this Public Hearing has been advertised and this is the first reading.
County Attorney Novak then read the proposed ordinance by title, stating the final reading
will be held on February 28, 2023, during the Board meeting. Chairman Quinn opened to
the public for any concerns regarding the proposed comprehensive plan amendment for
Parcel ID #01037-020R. There was no public comment. County Attorney Novak reported
the second reading has already been advertised.
PUBLIC HEARING – COMPREHENSIVE PLAN AMENDMENT
County Attorney then read a proposed ordinance by title regarding amending the
Comprehensive Plan to add Chapter 13, property rights element. He then stated that in
conjunction with this proposed ordinance, staff will then transmit the proposed ordinance
for Parcel ID #02734-012R, #02734-020R, #02734-021R, #03324-000R, #03343-005R,
#03362-000R, #03362-050R, #03363-050R, and #03368-050R to the State for reading.
He stated once read by the State, staff will receive further guidance and approval from
the State to move forward, noting the property rights element will be submitted in
conjunction. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There was no public comment.
County Attorney Novak stated this is listed as Quasi-Judicial (process), requesting the
Board waive the Quasi-Judicial with no comment and then go through the normal Public
Hearing process. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to waive Quasi. Commissioner
McCroan so moved. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. There being no public
comment, the motion passed unanimously (4-0). Chairman Quinn opened to the public,
calling for any questions or concerns regarding these Comprehensive Plan property
rights. There was no public comment. County Attorney Novak reported these will be
submitted to the State. Planner Crane stated the County is required, on any large-scale

map amendment or text changes, to submit them to the State within 10 days of the first
reading. He reported the State has 30 days to respond, giving the County 180 days to
make the recommended corrections and hold a second reading. Upon direction from
County Attorney Novak, Chairman Quinn then called for a motion to send it up to the
State. Commissioner Farrell so moved. Commissioner Rich seconded the motion. There
being no public comment, the motion then passed unanimously (4-0).

SEVEN REQUESTS – CR 30A
Francine Vellanti, of Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to present a written
request <copy provided to the Clerk for the record>, asking the Board to revisit the
previous seven requests submitted to the Board. She said she will be contacting the new
representative Simon (Senator Corey Simon) regarding her requests. Ms. Vellanti stated
her new subject is on the second letter, allowing time for the Board to read it, noting that
she was under the impression she was grandfathered in. She discussed the training she
has received regarding beekeeping and marketing honey, requesting Board approval to
continue her work (mobile vendor activity). Upon inquiry by County Attorney Novak, Ms.
Vellanti stated she hopes the Board will work with her before attending the State
Representative (delegation) meeting on January 31, 2023. Chairman Quinn inquired of
her requests to Mr. Shoaf’s office, Ms. Vellanti reporting that Mr. Shoaf’s office has
brought no results. Discussion followed regarding the meeting on January 31st with
Representative Shoaf and Senator Simon in the Board room. County Attorney Novak
stated that these requests have been brought before the Board and the Board has
responded. Discussion followed regarding the requested items on her list. County
Administrator Hammond discussed speaking with Ms. Vellanti last week regarding
receiving a ticket, after being warned, for selling products on County right-of-way and in
a County park, noting the County has an Ordinance in place since 1995 that states you
cannot sell anything on County right-of-way or County parks without a permit. He stated
that he cannot give her a permit to violate this Ordinance, the County has special permits
for events, noting the horses are insured and have a special permit to be on County
property. County Administrator Hammond stated if Ms. Vellanti wants to request a special
permit from the Board she can, but he does not have the authority, as County
Administrator to break this Ordinance. County Attorney Novak inquired if Ms. Vellanti has
obtained a County business license. Ms. Vellanti stated she is not required and was
inspected two years ago, reporting that she is under the cottage foods and beekeeping
rules. County Attorney Novak discussed the County adopting an Ordinance two years
ago regarding occupational licenses, noting if Ms. Vellanti is exempt, then it needs to
provide proof to the County. Ms. Vellanti requested permission to sell on County property,
asking if the Board needs time to think about it. Commissioner Farrell said no. He then
moved to deny permission to sell on County property or right-of-way. Commissioner Rich
seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There was no public
comment and the motion passed unanimously (4-0). Ms. Vellanti requested that the
County take it out of the brochure as an advertisement though T.D.C. County Attorney
Novak stated that if the County has an unlicensed photograph of Ms. Vellanti in the
County tourist guide, then staff will instruct T.D.C. to have it removed.

LAND ACQUISITION – STATE BUFFER PRESERVE
County Administrator Hammond discussed a project regarding a trade with the State
Buffer Preserve to obtain property that is contiguous with the County Country Club Golf
Course property which is needed for parking, future expansion, and a possible event
center. County Consultant Butler appeared before the Board to discuss the need for
additional parking for the Golf Course and has identified a piece of land, owned by the
State and controlled by the Buffer Preserve, immediately adjacent to the Golf Course
parking lot back to the west side entrance between Country Club Road and the Golf
Course. He noted the County does not own any land available to swap near the Buffer
Preserve, stating the County will need to purchase land comparable to the land from the
Buffer Preserve for the swap. County Consultant Butler stated there is privately owned
land near the Golf Course and the Buffer Preserve, but the State will have to approve it.
He then discussed negotiating with the private landowner to purchase the property, not
to exceed $250,000.00. Commissioner Husband asked if the uplands and wetlands have
been all worked out. County Consultant Butler reported there are wetlands on both
property sites, noting this is all subject to an appraisal and survey. Discussion followed
regarding property location and the proposed land swap. Upon inquiry by Chairman
Quinn, County Administrator Hammond recommended the Board allow staff to make the
purchase up to $250,000.00, contingent upon the County receiving approval from the
State. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to move forward. Commissioner Rich motioned
to approve. Commissioner Husband seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for
public comment. There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously.

PURCHASE – E.M.S. UTILITY VEHICLE
E.M.S. Director Daniels discussed the need for an E.M.S. utility vehicle to assist in
transporting patients from special events (football games, parks, etc.), stating he has
received some quotes but is waiting on a few more. County Administrator Hammond
discussed the cost being approximately $14,000.00-$15,000.00 for the unit and possibly
$4,000.00 for the additions. E.M.S. Director Daniels stated the quote received yesterday
was higher than $20,000.00 total. County Administrator Hammond discussed purchasing
two utility vehicles, one for each end of the County, and if the Board approves this
purchase, these vehicles will be put in place by next football season. Commissioner
McCroan inquired if these utility vehicles are capable of beach access, asking if they are
four-wheel drive. E.M.S. Director Daniels stated the specifications are four-wheel drive.
County Administrator Hammond discussed the capability of being at the football game or
a festival is due to prestaging, noting these utility vehicles will be housed at County
facilities. He discussed his concerns regarding timing, noting if the utility vehicle was not
already at Salinas Park it would be difficult to use on the beaches. Commissioner Rich
discussed the need for two designed trailers for transportation, supporting the idea to
purchase. Commissioner McCroan inquired where the utility vehicles will be housed.
County Administrator Hammond stated there are plenty of pole barns at the Courthouse

Complex, but currently the North end situation is a little more difficult due to the City of
Wewahitchka housing their firetrucks at the Wewahitchka E.M.S. building. E.M.S. Director
Daniels reported there is shelter in the back and once the fire trucks are removed the
utility vehicle will be stored in one of the ambulance bays. Discussion followed regarding
an open trailer or closed trailer. County Administrator Hammond stated he will get with
Assistant County Administrator Smallwood and E.M.S. Director Daniels to come up with
a way to house these vehicles. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to move forward with
purchasing two. Commissioner Farrell so moved. Commissioner Rich seconded the
motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment, the
motion passed unanimously.
NO WAKE ZONE – INTERLOCAL WATERWAYS
Public Works Director Cothran discussed an email from FWC (Fish & Wildlife
Commission) regarding the shape, square verses rectangle, of the No Wake Zone signs.
He discussed the process of obtaining permits to install the No Wake Zone signs for FWC
to enforce. Public Works Director Cothran stated the signs were installed March 2023,
and assumed FWC was enforcing. He stated the County then received an email from
FWC which noted that from the 24 signs installed one sign did not have the Ordinance
number affixed; therefore, FWC stated they would not enforce any of the signs. Public
Works Director Cothran reported that Public Works then went out to locate the one sign
and place the Ordinance number on it. He stated the most recent email from FWC stated
the signs are still not in compliance because they are square shaped, not rectangle. Public
Works Director Cothran noted the language in the permit packet states the markers need
to be uniform, reporting that all the signs are the same size and color. Upon discussing
his concerns regarding this three (3) year and four (4) month process, Public Works
Director Cothran then requested direction from the Board. Commissioner McCroan
reported this is not coming from the local officers, it is coming from Tallahassee,
discussing his concerns regarding the matter. Commissioner Rich suggested the Board
allow County Administrator Hammond to draft a letter to FWC and if that does not work,
then schedule a meeting where one or more of the Board members go to Tallahassee
and meet with the director. Members of the Board discussed their concerns regarding
safety. County Administrator Hammond requested permission of the Board to write a letter
requesting a meeting with the director. The Board had no objections. Chairman Quinn
called for a motion to put together a letter. Commissioner McCroan so moved.
Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment.
There being none, the motion passed unanimously.

CITY OF PORT ST. JOE – BEACHES SEWER SYSTEM
County Attorney Novak discussed the City of Port St. Joe acquiring the Beaches Sewer
System and the County presenting the funds for this purchase. He reported the County
is now in the process of obtaining the reimbursement through Consortium funds, noting
a condition from any Federal grant funds is that any subrecipient acknowledges, accepts,
and signs a subrecipient agreement. County Attorney Novak stated this agreement was
hand delivered to the City of Port St. Joe for execution on September 27, 2023, and as of
today, the County has not received the signed agreement back from the City of Port St.
Joe. He discussed his concerns regarding this matter as being time sensitive. County
Administrator Hammond discussed his concerns regarding the County funding the City of
Port St. Joe $750,000.00 and for the County to be reimbursed, the City must sign this
subrecipient agreement. He stated the Board should request that the City of Port St. Joe
not wait until their first meeting in November, which will be after the County’s deadline,
but to hold a special meeting before the end of October to approve this subrecipient
agreement. County Attorney Novak reported the letter has been drafted, requesting
permission to send it after adding County Administrator Hammond’s language regarding
time is of the essence with the date. County Attorney Novak discussed the City not
honoring the previous executed MOU (memorandum of understanding), noting there was
a commitment and pledge. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to send the letter
requesting a special (meeting) so we can get it signed and the County can get our money
back. Commissioner Farrell inquired if he needed to abstain. County Attorney Novak
stated yes. Commissioner Rich discussed his concerns regarding the City not signing this
agreement and the deadline. Chairman Quinn again called for a motion to send a letter
to the City (of Port St. Joe) requesting a special meeting so the City can sign (the
agreement) and the County can be reimbursed. Commissioner Husband so moved.
Commissioner Rich seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment.
There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously 4-0, with Commissioner
Farrell abstaining <FORM 8B on file with Clerk>.

200 AMP SERVICE – ADDISON TRAILER PARK WEWAHITCHKA
Robert Sutton, of Wewahitchka appeared before the Board to discuss a recent meter fire
at Addison Trailer Park (located in Wewahitchka) and his concerns regarding the RV
Ordinance taking away his right to choose between a mobile home (200 amp) or an RV
(100 amp) meter box on this lot, noting his State operating permit lists three mobile homes
now and allows a choice. He discussed obtaining a permit from the Building Department
and his concerns regarding the restrictions that are hindering his ability to do business by
appealing to both mobile homes and RVs (recreational vehicles). Mr. Sutton stated the
County RV Ordinance is in conflict with his State operating permit, which allows both
mobile homes or RVs. He requested the Board to resolve this matter by allowing him to
have the original 200-amp service replaced. Building Official Baliey reported that Mr.
Sutton, over a year ago, came in the office to request power for an RV, noting the mobile
home park is the old Addison Trailer Park, established in 1973 which is a non-conforming
mobile home park so through time of loss of trailers in the park anything new has to
comply with the County’s current standards whether it is a new mobile home or an RV.
He stated Mr. Sutton came to obtain a permit to install an RV and with that rule it allows
for a 100-amp service. Mr. Sutton stated he first asked for a 200-amp and received a
permit for a 200-amp service and was later informed that he could not do that, noting the
remainder 14-lots in the trailer park have 200-amp service. Following discussion, Mr.
Sutton stated the park was originally allowed 15 trailers, but since converting the lots over
to RVs, the State operating permit has changed (to three trailers). County Administrator
Hammond stated if Mr. Sutton comes in today to request a mobile home permit a 200-
amp service will be issued, noting the County rule is mobile home permits are separate
due to laws and inspection requirements. He reported the County’s law supersedes State
permits, noting Mr. Sutton is grandfathered in but when it blows away, burns or is torn
down the new rules apply. County Administrator Hammond stated if Mr. Sutton wants to
put in a new mobile home that meets rules, then a 200-amp service can be issued, but
he cannot receive a 200-amp service for an RV under the current County rules. Mr. Sutton
discussed wanting the option to choose. County Administrator Hammond reported a
mobile home permit is different than a RV or power pole permit, noting there are different
rules and regulations. Mr. Sutton requested an exception. Following discussion,
Commissioner Farrell motioned to deny the request. Commissioner Husband seconded
the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being none, the motion
passed unanimously. Mr. Sutton discussed his concerns regarding the County regulating
the amperage and the (Gulf Coast) Cooperative placing restrictors on 100-amp service.

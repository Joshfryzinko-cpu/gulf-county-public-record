Chairman Quinn then led in the Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag.
2023-24 BUDGET OVERVIEW
County Administrator Hammond discussed the parks money, noting it is difficult for staff
to do carry forward this early. He suggested parks money remain in the assigned district
pot and rollover like the Fire Departments’ funds. The Board had no objection.
County Administrator Hammond reported the County is still waiting on the State to provide
the Constitutional Officers’ salary changes, but all other line items are included in the
proposed budget.
OMB Director Herring provided a few key statements, as follows: 1) tentative aggregate
millage rate is 6.5883, which is 11.55% above the current year aggregate rolled back rate
of 5.9064; 2) tentative aggregate millage rate is made up of 6.2% Countywide and each
Fire Control District at .5 mills; and 3) tentative M.S.T.U. voted debt service millage rate
is .5502 for Gulfside Beachfront, .4758 is Gulfside Interior and Bayside is at .4706. She
stated these are the maximum millage rates that the Board may adopt, and a roll call vote
is required at today’s hearing. OMB Director Herring reported the primary reasons for the
ad valorem tax revenue increases are: 1) FRS retirement cost; 2) insurance cost; 3) pay
raises; 4) capitol outlay; 5) Sheriff’s Office; 6) County services; 7) County Jail; 8) E.M.S.
service; and 9) 9-1-1 services. She noted that each Commissioner has received a
tentative budget, and it is also posted on the County’s website; reporting this Budget

Hearing was advertised in the local newspaper, The Star, last Thursday. OMB Director
Herring stated page 4 of the budget book provides a listing by fund and the Board may
amend the budget at tonight’s hearing, but any increases will need to be offset with those
line-item adjustments. She requested Board approval of some net increases by fund, as
follows: 1) net increase of $58,775.00 in General Fund, affecting REC surtax revenue
estimates; 2) net increase in Fund 106, St. Joseph Fire District, of $70,000.00 due to a
change in cash carry forward estimate for both Beaches and South Gulf Fire
Departments; 3) net increase of $43,000.00 in Fund 107 for cash carry forward estimates
for Dalkeith Fire Department; 4) net increase of $87,235.00 in Fund 124 due to surtax
revenue from the revenue conference; 5) net increase in Fund 126 for $25,000.00 due to
revised Cash Carry Forward estimates affecting revenues and expenditures; 6) net
decrease of $570,000.00 in Fund 127, Disaster Fund due to revised calculation in Cash
Carry Forward; 7) net decrease of $25,000.00 in Fund 301 due to Cash Carry Forward
estimates; 8) net increase of $12,000.00 in Fund 404, Golf Course, due to Cash Carry
Forward revised estimates; and 9) to make line-item adjustments across all funds that will
not affect Ad Valorem. OMB Director Herring discussed the Constitutional Officers' salary
increases that were just posted by the State, noting these adjustments will be made
before the budget book is published. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to move forward
allowing OMB Director Herring to make those adjustments. Commissioner McCroan so
moved. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously. Chairman
Quinn opened the budget hearing by calling for public comment. There was no public
comment.

2023-24 BUDGET OVERVIEW
County Administrator Hammond thanked the Board for cutting taxes one more
year, discussing CRA eating up Bay County’s tax base. He reported that Gulf
County did not borrow money to fund projects but waited for funding before beach
renourishment and various projects. Commissioner Farrell discussed his concerns
as to what is going on in Mexico Beach and Panama City Beach, expressing
gratitude Gulf County waited for funding before proceeding with projects (after
Hurricane Michael).

There being no further business, and upon motion by Commissioner McCroan,
second by Commissioner Farrell, and unanimous vote, the meeting did then
adjourn at 5:24 p.m., E.T.

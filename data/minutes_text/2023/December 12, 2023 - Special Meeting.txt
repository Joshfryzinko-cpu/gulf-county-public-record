PUBLIC HEARING – BEACH DRIVING EMERGENCY ORDINANCE
County Attorney Novak noted this is a Public Hearing, properly advertised to consider a
proposed emergency ordinance, then reading the proposed ordinance by title for the
Board to consider. He stated the purpose for this proposed emergency ordinance is due
to this being the last Board meeting for this calendar year and the Gulf County Tax
Collector will be selling permits starting January 1, 2024. County Attorney Novak read the
highpoints of the proposed amended changes within the ordinance. County Administrator
Hammond suggested some additional changes to the proposed ordinance, as follows: 1)
$3.00 permit fee for Senior Citizens for County resident is a one-time administrative fee
but per vehicle, meaning if they change vehicles they will need to purchase a new permit
as the permit is sold based on the vehicle and not the person; and 2) under Section 6,
change the wording to proof of current insurance. Chairman Quinn opened the Public
Hearing and called for public comment. There was no public comment. County Attorney
Novak stated staff’s recommendation is for the Board to ratify through Ordinance.
Chairman Quinn again called for public comment. There being none, Commissioner
McCroan so moved. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Commissioner Farrell
inquired if Commissioner McCroan’s motion was adding the points by County
Administrator Hammond. Commissioner McCroan stated yes. Chairman Quinn called for
public comment. There being no public comment, the motion passed unanimously, as
follows:

STATUS REPORT – E.M.S. RESPONSE
E.M.S. Director Daniels discussed complaints received regarding timing of E.M.S.
responses, noting a lot of the complaints he believes is because the south-end is having
to respond to the north-end of the County due to a lack of availability of an ambulance on
the north-end. He stated some changes have been made since the complaints,
discussing some of the issues were caused by the change of Dispatch. E.M.S. Director
Daniels assured the public the County does have ambulances and they are responding
as quickly as possible. Commissioner Rich requested that E.M.S. Director Daniels work
with Dispatch, giving them more information so that dispatch can let the caller know in
case of emergency transport by car or wait for an ambulance. Commissioner Husband
discussed per capita the number of Gulf County ambulances per County population is
huge. E.M.S. Director Daniels noted the one thing working against Gulf County is distance
to a hospital, reporting Bay County’s turnaround time is quick but Gulf County, however,
does take a couple of hours as patients go to Panama City (Bay County) if critical. He
also stated as far as ambulances versus the 911 calls received, he believes the County
is sufficient. County Administrator Hammond discussed providing a breakdown during
budget time, if the Board so wishes, but the last study revealed the County had 6.5 times
more ambulances per capita than Bay County. He discussed trimming and still being at a
$500,000.00 loss and the problem with hiring due to lack of affordable housing in Gulf
County. Commissioner McCroan discussed E.M.S. Director Daniels doing a great job
stabilizing this department, noting that in the summer there is up to 20,000 people at the
Cape at any given time and when you send two (2) ambulances to the Cape you are
going to be limited. County Administrator Hammond reported the goal is, come spring
break, assuming the County can cover it, to put a swing truck (ambulance) in the tourist
corridor at high peak times.

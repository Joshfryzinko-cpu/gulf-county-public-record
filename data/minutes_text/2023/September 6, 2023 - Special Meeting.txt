SEPTEMBER 6, 2023
SPECIAL MEETING
PORT ST. JOE, FLORIDA
The Gulf County Board of County Commissioners met this date in special session with
the following members present: Chairman Sandy Quinn, Jr., Vice Chairman Phil
McCroan, and Commissioners David C. Rich, Jack Husband, and Patrick Farrell.
Others present were: Director of Finance & Management Leo Bebeau, Deputy Clerk
Leanna Cooper, County Administrator Michael L. Hammond, County Assistant
Administrator Clay Smallwood, III, Deputy Administrator Kari Summers, Building Official
Brad Bailey, Building Department Staff Trevor Seavy, Emergency Management
Director/Fire Coordinator Matt Herring, Grant Administrator Kaci Rhodes, Grant
Administrator Erin Kennedy, Human Resource Director Brett Lowry, Office of
Management & Budget Director Sherry Herring, Mosquito Control Director Austin Horton,
County Planner Doug Crane, Public Works Director Mark Cothran, Assistant Public Works
Director Raymond Hart, and Sheriff Mike Harrison.
Chairman Quinn called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m., E.T.
Chairman Quinn then led in the Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag.
ADMINISTRATIVE UPDATE & REPORT
From page 1 of his packet, County Administrator Hammond presented the proposed
capital plan for Fiscal Years 2024-2029 for the Board to consider; noting the need for
Board approval in anticipation of the Budget Hearing to follow. He stated staff has some
of these funds budgeted and reserved for future years. Upon inquiry by Commissioner
Husband, County Administrator Hammond stated the current plan approved by the Board
goes through 2023-24, this plan is just for reserved funds that are already in the Capital
Fund for Board approval for years 2024-2028. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to
move forward. Commissioner Farrell so moved. Commissioner Rich seconded the
motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment, the
motion passed unanimously.
County Administrator Hammond reported that the County Attorney worked out a deal for
the County to get out of the (Public Risk Management Health) Trust, stating there will be
no interruption with the change (in carriers) and the change will take place on October 1,
2023. He also stated that the County will be paying an administrative fee of $7.00 per
employee for so many quarters, noting basically the County is getting out (of the Trust)
for $12,510.00. County Administrator Hammond requested Board approval to allow him
to enter into that agreement and move forward with the new company (United Healthcare)
on October 1st. After clarifying the negotiated settlement amount by Commissioner
Farrell, County Administrator Hammond and members of the Board commended County

Attorney Novak for his work on the favorable resolution of this matter. Chairman Quinn
called for a motion to move forward with entering into that agreement. Commissioner
Farrell so moved. Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called
for public comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously.
Building Official Bailey reported on the County’s Local State of Emergency, staff visited
356 properties for RVs (recreational vehicles) from County line to County line and 107 did
not comply. He stated that out of those 107, 55 were in the County’s RV-restricted zone,
and one of those, which was Grandfathered in, will be recommended that they lose their
Grandfather-ship due to selling the property outside of the timeframe. Building Official
Bailey recommended to the Board that the 107 (property owners) receive a fine of up to
$500.00 and everyone inside the RV-restricted zone get a one-time warning letter, this
time, for their Grandfather-ship, and the one property owner will lose it because it was
sold. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Farrell regarding the location of the one that sold
(changing ownership), Building Official Bailey reported the property is located at St. Joe
Beach. County Administrator Hammond explained the Grandfather clause of the
ordinance and how it is applicable to ownership. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Husband
regarding the fine amount, County Administrator Hammond stated that up to this point,
the County has not fined anyone in violation of not evacuating RVs under the emergency
ordinance, noting the regular RV Ordinance gives a 48-hour timeline but the emergency
ordinance (order) supersedes the RV Ordinance. He suggested the Board impose a fine
somewhere in the middle of $500.00 and recommended these citations go before the
Special Master, so if the property owner doesn’t want to pay, they can appeal and plead
their case with the Special Master. Commissioner Rich discussed his concerns with the
RV Ordinance and expressed concerns regarding property owners’ rights. Chairman
Quinn discussed his support for the Special Master handling these to allow property
owners to plead their cases. Commissioner McCroan discussed concerns regarding the
ones who did the right thing and the need to enforce the rules; stating that people knew
the rules when they were allowed to place the RV (recreational vehicle) on their property.
Following discussion by members of the Board, Chairman Quinn asked for clarification
on what County Administrator Hammond is asking of the Board. County Administrator
Hammond stated he wanted their input and if it is good with the Board, Code Enforcement
will issue 107 citations, 55 warning letters, and one (1) revocation with the appeals going
to the Code Enforcement Special Master. Commissioner Farrell inquired if that needed to
be put in the form of a motion and whether to add the fine amount to the motion. County
Administrator Hammond stated it is at the Board’s discretion. Upon inquiry by
Commissioner Rich, County Administrator Hammond stated the recommendation is that
everyone gets a fine that did not comply, noting it can be appealed for special
circumstances to the Special Master and for the property owners in the RV exclusion
zone, what is covered by the RV Ordinance Beacon Hill, St. Joe Beach, Cape San Blas,
Indian Pass within a mile of the open water, that they get a one-time warning letter that
states this is one and only warning and next time they are subject to lose your
Grandfathered-in designation, also noting it will be an Administrative decision when it
comes and the Board can always override what the Administration does. He stated if a
property owner has a house, they can store an RV, just like you can store a boat, but if
you have a physical setup RV and is the only thing on that lot, then you must move it.

Chairman Quinn stated the County will issue 107 citations, 55 warning letters, and
revocation of the one that was statutorily lost (sold). Discussion followed regarding this
loss being due to a change in ownership, not due to the storm. Following additional
discussion, Chairman Quinn re-stated the motion to issue 107 citations, 55 warning
letters, and one (1) revocation with the fine being up to $500.00 and an appeal process
before the Special Master if the property owner has an issue. Chairman Quinn called for
a motion to move forward. Commissioner Farrell so moved but recommended setting the
fine for the first go-round at $250.00. County Administrator Hammond stated that is his
recommendation. Chairman Quinn inquired that instead of up to $500.00, the Board is
setting the fine amount up to $250.00. County Administrator Hammond stated his
recommendation is that they all get a $250.00 fine. Commissioner McCroan seconded
the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment,
the motion passed 4 to 1, with Commissioner Rich voting no.
DRIVEWAY PERMITS
County Administrator Hammond discussed kicking two (2) issues back to the Board, the
driveway permits and land-filling permits, not land-clearing permits. He stated the problem
is with property owners filling their lots and not respecting their neighbor’s property.
County Administrator Hammond stated the recommendation is that if they don’t have a
building permit and are not building or doing something in thirty (30) days, then the
property owners must stabilize it in some way to keep the dirt and run-off on their property.
PUBLIC HEARING – BUILDING DEPARTMENT ORDINANCE
Chairman Quinn opened the Public Hearing, calling for public comment regarding the
Building Department Ordinance and driveway permits.
DRIVEWAY PERMITS
Commissioner Husband discussed his concerns regarding the driveway permits,
discussing the alternative paving materials stated in the LDRs (Land Development
Regulations) on page 109, which in his opinion addresses this matter, but the gravel he
believes is not the problem, it is the lime rock and base below the gravel. He stated he
would like to keep gravel as a pervious material. Commissioner Husband also stated he
does not have an issue with a permit, as it will help the County regulate it (construction of
driveways), noting the base below is much more important in his opinion to keep it simple.
Commissioner McCroan inquired if Commissioner Husband was asking the County to
regulate the base underneath the gravel. Commissioner Husband said yes, asking if the
County could come up with a typical section for a driveway and state it can or cannot
have lime rock. Discussion followed regarding lime rock and pervious pavers. Chairman
Quinn called for public comment regarding driveway permits. There was no public
comment.
PUBLIC HEARING – BUILDING DEPARTMENT ORDINANCE
Commissioner Farrell noted the Board is discussing driveway permits, but the County
Administrator started off by discussing the topic of covering of vacant lands, stating these
are two separate issues. County Administrator Hammond agreed these are two separate
issues. Commissioner Farrell reported he is in support as this affects his District, noting

the State and North Florida Water Management want property owners to contain the water
on their property, addressing his concerns regarding this matter.
DRIVEWAY PERMITS
County Administrator Hammond discussed his concerns regarding lime rock on
driveways, noting Assistant County Administrator Smallwood has some
recommendations for the Board to consider. After discussion by Commissioner Husband
acknowledging this as a multi-level problem and stating that a driveway permit is a good
thing, Chairman Quinn called for public comment regarding driveway permits. There
being no public comment, Chairman Quinn closed to the public.
PUBLIC HEARING – BUILDING DEPARTMENT ORDINANCE
County Administrator Hammond noted both items are in the proposed ordinance, but he
is not aware of any other controversial changes that staff has but can be discussed if
needed. He stated the only controversial items in the proposed ordinance are the fill/clear
and driveway matters. Commissioner McCroan stated he would like to see Commissioner
Husband, County Administrator Hammond, and Assistant County Administrator
Smallwood work on the driveway portion. Planner Crane stated the common thing
happening is that property owners have a driveway with sand, gravel, or some other form
but as soon as the house is CO’d (Certificate of Occupancy issued) the property owners
put in a concrete driveway or pavers. He discussed his concern regarding the County not
being able to capture the modification or installation of driveways without requiring a
driveway permit; noting the purpose of adding a driveway permit is so the Building
Department could have knowledge of that so it can be calculated into the 30% and it stay
in compliant. Following discussion regarding the driveway permit, Chairman Quinn called
for public comment regarding the proposed ordinance. Pat Hardman, of Cape San Blas
appeared before the Board to discuss her concerns regarding the water problem and
property rights. She inquired if the County could provide driveway material options, and
agreed the County does need a permit for driveways and landfilling. Chairman Quinn
called for additional public comment. Donna Murry, of Indian Pass appeared before the
Board to discuss her concerns regarding a friend’s property that is experiencing sand and
runoff being washed onto her property from a joint neighbor. She inquired about what
recourse her friend might have. County Administrator Hammond stated it is a civil matter
and one of the things being discussed today is trying to get property owners to be
responsible and the proposal today is that if they do not have a building permit that within
30 days of doing the addition of fill, then the property owners will have to contain it either
with silk fence, hydroseed or sodding. Discussion followed regarding meeting standards
and a recourse process. Bruce Kuhse, of Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to
state he is in support of the concept of containing the stormwater runoff on an individual’s
property, inquiring about the thirty-day start date and who notifies the County the lot has
been cleared; asking how it is procedurally going to work. Building Official Bailey reported
when a permit is issued by building code, there is a permit box posted on the job site with
the permit in it. County Administrator Hammond clarified the intent is the land-filling part,
not the land-clearing part, discussing most of the properties at Indian Pass will require
HCP (Habitat Conservation Plan) and will have a clearing permit, and nearly all the Cape
and a lot of St. Joe Beach is that same way therefore, it narrows down the areas on the

south end and the beaches. Following additional discussion regarding the filling of land,
Chairman Quinn again called for any additional public comment. There was no further
public comment, Chairman Quinn closed the Public Hearing to the public. County
Administrator Hammond discussed needing further guidance from the Board regarding
this proposed ordinance, suggesting a meeting with each Commissioner individually
before next month’s final hearing. Commissioner Husband discussed his concerns
regarding regulations and location/area. After further discussion regarding property
owners keeping their dirt on their own property, Chairman Quinn stated County
Administrator Hammond will meet with each Board member with Assistant County
Administrator Smallwood and the Building Department.
There being no further business, and upon motion by Commissioner McCroan, second
by Commissioner Farrell, and unanimous vote, the meeting did then adjourn at 4:59 p.m.,

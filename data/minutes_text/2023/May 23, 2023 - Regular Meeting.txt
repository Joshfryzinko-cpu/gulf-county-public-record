Referencing page 1 of the Administrative Update and Report packet <copy provided to

STATUS UPDATE – H.H.R.P. ROUND I
OMB Director Herring directed the Board to page 2 of the packet, discussing the status
update report for H.H.R.P. Round I funding. She reported there are three applications that
are waiting to close, which has been factored into this report. County Administrator
Hammond noted in order to start Round II, Round I has to be closed out. He stated there
are some leftover amounts of money and the County will be asking for an extension and
to move these funds to Round II; reporting if it is not approved, then the recommendation
will be to return the remaining funds from Round I. OMB Director Herring stated that once
these three applicants are closed, then staff will reach out to the State for guidance if it
will be allowed to roll over unspent funds from Round I into Round II being that Round I
and Round II are two separate grants. County Administrator Hammond inquired if the
Board agreed; noting he does not expect the State to not allow the County to roll the
remaining funds forward. He then discussed his concerns regarding the strict guidelines
and the difficulty meeting the set aside. Chairman Quinn had no objection.
STATUS UPDATE – H.H.R.P. ROUND II
OMB Director Herring discussed the status report for H.H.R.P. Round II from page 3 of
the packet; noting the County has qualified and awarded two moderate applicants and
now have to spend $300,000.00 in low and very low immediately, and meet the set-asides
in order to fund these two moderate applicants. She discussed the lottery pool; requesting
guidance from the Board regarding an applicant that answers the call right away, qualifies
as low instead of very low, inquiring if the Board is okay with moving forward with the
applicant being moved to the low category or does the Board want staff to hold the
applicant until all the very low applicants are processed for qualification. OMB Director

Herring reported that staff is giving applicants thirty days to qualify and her
recommendation would be if the situation arises that an applicant applied as a very low
and now qualifies as low, and the other applicants didn’t respond and qualify within those
thirty days then staff can move forward with the applicant as a low application. Chairman
Quinn discussed allowing for the thirty-day respond and if the very low applicant does not
respond, but someone in the low responds within the thirty days. OMB Director Herring
reported every applicant in the very low and low category have been contacted by letter
and email giving them thirty days to respond if they are still interested and to provide the
needed information for qualification; stating from all the applicants, the County has only
received a response from two applicants and one of these applicants was very low but
now appears to qualify under the low category. County Administrator Hammond stated
OMB Director Herring is recommending first come, first serve. Commissioner McCroan
had no objection if the applicant can qualify in the low category. Discussion followed
regarding first-qualified applicants and allowing for the thirty-day response deadline. The
Board had no objection to the first come, first serve. County Administrator Hammond
referred back to page 1 of the packet, noting each District received $1 million and District
I was way ahead, the rules were changed for District III and V to try and catch them up
(dollar wise), stating part of the problem is when the County originally did the lottery,
applicants were classified by where they lived at the time but the majority are not
purchasing in their original classified District, this is the reason why the amount in District
I is higher than the others because most applicants can only afford to purchase in District
I and II. Discussion followed regarding the strict qualifications and the labor shortage for
rehab projects that has made it difficult to disburse these funds.

DEAD TREES – U.S. HIGHWAY 71
Commissioner Rich discussed his concerns regarding the danger of a dead tree south of
town on U.S. Highway 71 that needs to be cut. He asked for help from the Board regarding
this matter. Chairman Quinn inquired if the staff had a solution. Commissioner Rich stated
the tree is by CJ Lands property, on U.S. Highway 71, south of town, reporting there
needs to be immediate action. County Administrator Hammond discussed his concerns
that during Hurricane Michael, Metric would not approve cutting down leaning trees;
stating that D.O.T. does not want to cut trees, especially if it is close to the property line
and the County has cut more trees than any of the other agencies. He stated that he will
get Public Works to take care of it this afternoon; noting there are hundreds of dead trees,
especially on State right-of-ways. Discussion followed regarding dead trees needing to
be cut. County Administrator Hammond asked the Board to give him grace, then he will
put a crew on cutting dangerous trees. Chairman Quinn called for the County Attorney’s
opinion first. County Attorney Novak stated the County has a standing form, if there is any
questionable delineation between private property and public then Public Works Director
Cothran and his crew will have the property owner sign off on it. He stated in terms of
public health and safety, if we have these trees where it is clear on State right-of-way, we
go out and do it. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to move forward with that. County
Administrator Hammond stated there is no need for a motion, just tell him they want it
done and he will do it. Following discussion, Chairman Quinn said we need a motion.
County Attorney Novak stated a vote authorizing the Administrator to move forward based
on public health and safety. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to move forward.
Commissioner Rich so moved. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman
Quinn called for public comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously (4-
0). County Administrator Hammond noted when the County had to cut some of the
dangerous trees during Hurricane Michael, the County subcontracted the dangerous
ones; stating he doesn’t want County staff getting hurt, therefore, if it is something the
County cannot handle, then he will get Kim (H & H Trucking and Tree Service) or
somebody to at least top it and then the County will figure it out.

REGIONAL RURAL TRANSPORTATION PLAN – A.R.P.C.
Ben Chandler, of A.R.P.C. appeared before the Board to discuss the R.R.T.P. (Regional
Rural Transportation Plan) which consists of five Counties, noting this plan is to establish
a long-range, twenty-year horizon, of long-term and short-term projects. He reported this
is a partnership with D.O.T. and once completed this plan will provide full coverage for
District III throughout the region. Mr. Chandler discussed working with each County to
create an advisory committee, holding three members of each County which the County
will appoint two and A.R.P.C. will appoint one. County Administrator Hammond stated he
is excited about this plan, noting Gulf County has been under-serviced and the County’s
goal for at least thirty years is to see U.S. Highway 71 paved four-laned. Upon inquiry by
County Administrator Hammond, Mr. Chandler stated the five Counties under this plan
are Gulf, Liberty, Franklin, Calhoun, and Jackson. He discussed contacting the
Administrative Office to see who would be best to serve on this committee. County
Administrator Hammond inquired when the Board would need to appoint their appointees.
Mr. Chandler reported in the next few months is the goal, noting currently A.R.P.C. is
working on the public involvement plan and this will identify the process and criteria for
what the committee members will need to do.
RESTORATION OF ABANDONED CEMETERIES AT IOLA
Linda Davis and Ann Johnson, of Wewahitchka Historical Society appeared before the
Board, providing a letter and other documents for the Board to review <copy provided to
the Clerk for the record>. She stated per Florida Statutes 497.284, the Board has
jurisdiction over the white and black cemeteries located in Iola off Muscogee Road in
Wewahitchka. She reported that the current property owner is not maintaining these
cemeteries and requested the Board to officially authorize the Wewahitchka Historical
Society to access these abandoned historic cemeteries. Mrs. Davis discussed contacting
the property owner, Teal Timber, LLC, who has agreed to not plow the marked-off section
at this time. She discussed her concerns regarding the urgency as Teal is plowing very
closely to these cemeteries and their desire to restore both cemeteries. Chairman Quinn
called for a motion to extend. Upon motion by Commissioner Farrell, second by
Commissioner Rich, and a unanimous 4-0 vote, the Board extended Mrs. Davis additional

time to speak. Mrs. Davis reported the Society will need to obtain $1 million in liability
insurance before entering the property to clean and maintain these cemeteries;
discussing the presented proposed letter addressed to Teal Timber for the Board to
consider. She discussed the supplies needed, upcoming fundraisers, possible grants, and
requesting for donations, requesting support from the Board for the use of the County’s
equipment and staff. Mrs. Davis requested that the Board write a letter to Teal Timber
notifying them of the County’s decision and ask that they delay the plowing of this portion
of the land indefinitely; inquiring if the County had an insurance policy that would cover
the members of the Society. County Administrator Hammond stated he is in support of
saving the cemetery but is not sure what exactly the County can do; noting the million-
dollar liability is not astronomical as the County requires the same for park rentals. He
discussed the possibility of teaming with the University of Florida or the University of West
Florida and falling under their insurance. Discussion followed regarding Neal Timber as
the previous property owner. County Administrator Hammond discussed the Board writing
a letter to Teal Timber in support of these cemeteries and the Board’s desire to see these
cemeteries go to the Historical Society with easement access. County Attorney Novak
offered to meet with the Wewahitchka Historical Society to help and guide; noting this is
a public/private matter and yes, the Statute gives the Municipality/County the ability but if
the County elects to do it, then the County will set a precedent throughout the entire
County. He stated with blessing from the Board he will draft a letter, on behalf of the
Board, and work with the two private groups to enable access and coverage. The Board
had no objection.

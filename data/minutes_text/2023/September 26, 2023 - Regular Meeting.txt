FINAL PUBLIC HEARING – AMEND BUILDING DEPARTMENT ORDINANCE
County Attorney Novak noted the proposed ordinance amending the Building Department
Ordinance is in the Administrative Update & Report Packet for the Board to review. He
reported advertisements were placed in the newspaper on August 10th, August 24th, and
September 7th in addition to Public Hearings and the first reading of this proposed
ordinance. County Attorney Novak then read the proposed ordinance by title for the Board
to consider and discussed the highlights of the proposed amendments. Commissioner
Husband discussed the proposed amended language changes within the proposed
ordinance, stating he would like to have a few things added. Commissioner Husband
stated that he met with Building Official Bailey and Planner Director Crane as directed,
noting most of it is addressed but they added to the land clearing portion, clearing and/or
landfilling. Commissioner Husband noted that he and Planning Director Crane are under
the same opinion that pavers are pervious. Commissioner Husband requested to make
the pavers pervious and, in addition to this proposed ordinance, allow for a maximum
square footage of pavers that one lot can have to be the roof area of the house that is on
the lot. Upon inquiry by Commissioner Husband, Building Official Bailey confirmed there
would be no new permit fees for a new construction home. Commissioner Husband stated
gravel will still be pervious, indicating no change there. He stated that if an owner wanted
to do pavers there would be a TAC (Technical Advisory Committee) project review fee of
$500.00 and a $350.00 driveway permit fee under this proposed ordinance. Upon inquiry
by Commissioner McCroan about whether the County would regulate the base under the
pavers, Commissioner Husband confirmed that it would still be regulated and within the
30% so if they are not at 30% then nothing has changed but if they do want to go to the
pavers, they are regulated by the gravel below it based on the pavers manufactures
specifications and subject to TAC review. Discussion followed regarding the TAC
reviewing and regulating these paver projects. Planner Crane inquired for clarification that
pavers will be considered pervious after TAC review and that the TAC does have the
authority to deny any application for pavers, noting it is not an automatic approval.
Commissioner Husband stated that is correct. He reported that all pavers are considered
impervious until the TAC actually reviews them per manufacturers' specifications.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment, opening the Public Hearing to the public.
There was no public comment. County Attorney Novak stated staff has those
amendments and stabilization amendments as well. County Administrator Hammond
inquired about new constructions and if the County will require the driveway to be put in
before the CO (Certificate of Occupancy). Commissioner Husband stated yes, that will be
a requirement. County Attorney Novak stated it is the staff's recommendation to adopt
this proposed ordinance with the amended language. Chairman Quinn called for a motion
to move forward with the amended language. Commissioner Farrell so moved.
Commissioner Husband seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously, as follows:

VARIANCE REQUEST – JAMES & NANCY MAXWELL
Planner Crane introduced a variance request from James and Nancey Maxwell, Parcel
ID #03587-000R for the Board to consider. Chairman Quinn called for anyone wishing to
speak on this issue to step forward. James and Nancy Maxwell appeared before the
Board. Chairman Quinn inquired if Mr. & Mrs. Maxwell had any objection to waiving the
Quasi-Judicial Hearing. County Attorney Novak explained the option to waive the Quasi-
Judicial Hearing to Mr. & Mrs. Maxwell at which there was no objection to waiving.
Chairman Quinn then called for a motion to waive the Quasi-Judicial Hearing.
Commissioner McCroan so moved to waive. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion.
Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being none, the motion passed
unanimously. James Maxwell, of Beacon Hill introduced himself to the Board. Upon
inquiry by Chairman Quinn, Planner Crane stated the P.D.R.B.’s recommendation was a
4-0 vote to deny this variance request. Mr. Maxwell discussed the porch prior to the
damage caused by Hurricane Michael, obtaining a permit, and the inspection by the
Building Department. He requested the Board allow him to build the porch, including the
roof, like it was before Hurricane Michael. Commissioner Husband discussed the letters
presented by neighbors regarding this matter. Discussion followed regarding the request
to build using the same footprint. County Administrator Hammond discussed previous
variance requests that have come before the Board that were denied, noting if the
structure was substantially destroyed the request to rebuild in the same footprint has been
denied. He reported Mr. Maxwell can have the deck without a roof on it, as long as it is
under thirty inches (30”). County Administrator Hammond discussed the permit stating it
is good for six (6) months, noting if there is no inspection every six months, then the permit
expires. Upon inquiry by County Administrator Hammond, Building Inspector Bailey
reported Mr. Maxwell turned in a scope of work for insulation, windows, vinyl siding,
replacement of drywall, and roof repair. He stated during that time, a roof repair would
have meant replacing a piece of metal or two missing off the top, not a total roof rebuild.
Building Department Bailey also stated the County rules have been in effect since 1994
when the Comprehensive Plan was adopted, and this original porch was built twenty (20)
years ago without permits, noting if permitted back then it would have been caught that it
was in the setback. Discussion followed regarding setbacks and other property owners
within Beacon Hill who were denied variance requests. Commissioner Husband stated
there is room on the south side of Mr. Maxwell’s property to build a different room. Mrs.
Maxwell inquired about the location of the driveway if built on that side of the property.
Commissioner Husband stated the driveway could be shifted over or could come off of
Cowries Street. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to extend the time. Upon motion by
Commissioner McCroan, second by Commissioner Husband, and unanimous vote, the
Board extended time for Mrs. Maxwell to speak. Mrs. Maxwell discussed the location of
the porch and the neighbors having no objection to the obstruction of the beach view. She

discussed the need to repair the inside of the structure first before rebuilding the porch.
County Administrator Hammond stated the County has rules and the P.D.R.B. reviewed
this variance request and said no, noting the Board sets a precedent when these
variances are allowed. He stated that it must meet the hardship criteria on the property;
noting the Maxwell’s have other alternatives, therefore he does not see how the Board
can grant a side setback. Upon inquiry by County Administrator Hammond, Planner Crane
reported the request is an approximate twelve feet (12’) variance. After further discussion,
Planner Crane reported the permit was pulled for the repairs on November 14, 2018,
there was a section regarding a roof repair and after speaking with the Maxwells and
further research, there is no doubt in his mind that there was no damage to the existing
roof to the house therefore he fully believes when the Maxwells said repair roof they
meant to repair the roof to the porch, but the Building Department at that time did not look
at it as completely replacing the roof of that deck. He reported that, after the storm
(Hurricane Michael), the deck which is below 30” (thirty inches) that extends into the
footprint was the only thing that remained, noting all the roof structure was an aluminum
structure that was completely wiped out. Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn, Planner Crane
reported the P.D.R.B., by a 4-0 vote, recommended denying this variance. Chairman
Quinn called for a vote to waive the formal reading. Commissioner Farrell so moved.
Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. Upon inquiry by Mr. Maxwell, County Attorney Novak provided an explanation
regarding the reading of the two-page form, he then read aloud the four conditions in
which a variance can be granted. Chairman Quinn noted being that the reading was read
aloud there was no need for the motion, inquiring if Commissioner Farrell wanted to pull
his motion. Commissioner Farrell said yes. Chairman Quinn inquired if Commissioner
McCroan was okay with it. Commissioner McCroan said yes. Commissioner Husband
motioned to deny the variance. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman
Quinn called for public comment. There being none, the motion passed 4 to 1, with
Commissioner McCroan voting no.

BEARS – CAPE SAN BLAS
Mark Rosasco, of Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to discuss his concerns
regarding bears and the possibility of the County considering the use of a new type of
garbage can for bear-proofing. He presented a flyer to the Board to review <copy provided
to the Clerk for the record>, discussing the bear-resistant trash containers. Mr. Rosasco
requested the Board consider asking BCC Waste Solutions to offer these cans, noting the
State has granted funding to some Counties for these garbage cans. He also discussed
the Board asking the Fish and Wildlife Commission (FWC) to relocate these ‘garbage
bears’. Commissioner McCroan shared his concerns regarding the bears, stating the
County has tried and tried to get FWC to relocate the bears. Discussion followed
regarding the nuisance bears. Chairman Quinn stated staff will pass this information along
to BCC (Waste Solutions). Discussion followed regarding the bears, the cost of the can,
and the slowing of the trash route to unlock the cans. County Administrator Hammond
stated he would reach out to BCC (Waste Solutions) regarding this matter, noting it would
slow down the process immensely. Mr. Rosasco discussed his concerns regarding rental
companies not picking up the garbage in a timely manner once a bear has gotten into the
trash. Commissioner Rich invited Mr. Rosasco to attend the Legislative Delegation
meeting at 1:30 p.m., E.T. tomorrow to discuss his concerns regarding the bears. Upon

inquiry by County Administrator Hammond on whether the County wants him to contact
BCC (Waste Solutions) regarding these cans, Chairman Quin said yes and requested he
look into the grants as well.
FLOODING – 10TH STREET PARK
Robert Branch, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to present material for the
Board to review <copy provided to the Clerk for the record>, noting his main concern is
the safety and welfare of the citizens of Port St. Joe and surrounding areas due to
flooding. He discussed the presented picture of the stormwater canal between 10th Street
and 16th Street after Hurricane Michael and the flood zone in this area. He discussed his
concerns regarding stormwater and the flooding zone, noting his main concern is that the
Board is voting to make changes to this stormwater canal. Upon motion by Commissioner
Rich, second by Commissioner Husband, and unanimous vote, the Board extended Mr.
Branch’s time to speak. Mr. Branch discussed the Board voting to fund the altering,
covering, piping, and filling of this stormwater canal for the purpose of building a sports
complex in a high-risk flood zone, noting the Field of Dreams is to be used for this
purpose. Chairman Quinn inquired if Mr. Branch knew the pipes under the fields were
collapsing. Following discussion, Christy McElroy, of Port St. Joe appeared before the
Board to state that one of the City Commissioners is pushing not to repair the piping under
the field but to pipe the entire driven canal and according to this City Commissioner, it is
on record that Chairman Quinn, Commissioner McCroan and Assistant County
Administrator Smallwood are pushing this too. She discussed Florida Statute
380.093(093) regarding the resilient grant funds, noting not only is the County applying
for these grant funds, but the City of Port St. Joe is also. Ms. McElory reported there is a
tape that she will send to each member of the Board that describes what will happen to
Port St. Joe and being prepared. She discussed the flood maps updated by Northwest
Florida Water Management District and the importance of these maps. Upon motion by
Commissioner McCroan, second by Commissioner Husband, and unanimous vote, the
Board extended Ms. McElory’s time to speak. Ms. McElory continued to discuss the area
of concern as being a high flood risk, stating the community needs those trees along 10th
Street to 16th Street. Discussion followed regarding her concerns of the matter.
Commissioner McCroan stated he commends City Commissioner Hoffman for bringing
this back forward, reporting the (10th Street) park is in sad shape. He stated he supports
City Commissioner Hoffman for replacing that pipe under those ballparks. Following
additional discussion, Alan Wetzstein, of Port St. Joe appeared before the Board to
discuss his concern regarding flooding, noting this area is a green zone and the
community wants to ensure the water moves through quickly.

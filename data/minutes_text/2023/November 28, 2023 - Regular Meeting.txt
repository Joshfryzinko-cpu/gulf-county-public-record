the Flag.
CONSENT AGENDA
Chairman Quinn called for public comment regarding the Consent Agenda. There was no
public comment. Commissioner Farrell stated he will need to abstain from pages 57-58,
ratifying Amendment #1 Subrecipient Agreement being part of the ESAD Enterprises
purchase. Commissioner Husband then stated he will need to abstain from page 68,
(ratifying Modification #2 with D.E.M. on County Road 387) due to previously doing work
on this project, pages 71-72 (Monolith Construction invoice to repairs to GCSO Building)
due to working with this contractor, and page 101 (task order with Dewberry for South
Diana Street Bridge) as an existing client. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to accept
the Consent Agenda with Commissioner Farrell abstaining from pages 57-58 and
Commissioner Husband abstaining from pages 68, 71-72 and 101. Commissioner Rich
so moved with the abstentions. Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion.
Commissioner McCroan then stated he will need to abstain pages 71-72 (Monolith
Construction invoice to repairs to GCSO Building) then withdrew his second. Upon inquiry
by Chairman Quinn, County Attorney Novak stated the Board has a motion and second,
directing the Chairman to ask for the motion and second again acknowledging
Commissioner McCroan’s abstention from pages 71-72. Chairman Quinn inquired if
Commissioner Rich’s motion stands. Commissioner Rich stated yes. Commissioner
McCroan seconded the motion with the abstention on pages 71-72. Chairman Quinn

called for public comment. There being none, the motion passed 5-0, with Commissioners
Farrell, McCroan and Husband abstaining to the pages previously stated, as follows:

BEACH DRIVING ORDINANCE
County Administrator Hammond discussed pages 6-12 being the Beach Driving
Ordinance (#2022-13), noting the County has postponed changing the Ordinance but did
change the fee schedule a few months ago. He inquired if the Board wishes to change
the $3.00 lifetime 65 years and older to just County residents before going through the
process of amending this Ordinance. Following discussion, Commissioner McCroan had
no objection to allow a lifetime beach driving permit for County residents only that are 65
years of age or older. After further discussion, Commissioner McCroan motioned to
change the fee to $500.00 for out-of-county. County Administrator Hammond stated staff
needs a motion to: 1) change the out-of-county fee to what the County policy is; 2) add
the limit that was put in by policy to 200 maximum per year (out-of-county beach driving
permit); 3) permits being sold January 1st through December 31st; and 4) the exemption
for over 65 residents only. Commissioner McCroan stated that is his motion.
Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. County Attorney Novak discussed if this vote
passes, then staff can declare under the Statute an emergency ordinance which will
cleanup the policy already in place, advertise an emergency ordinance and ratify on

December 12, 2023, to meet the January deadline before the Tax Collector’s Office begins
selling permits under the new ordinance starting the new calendar year. Clerk Norris
inquired about the 65 and over on whether it was just residents or residents who are
taxpayers. County Administrator Hammond stated the motion is residents, so if you are
eligible for a residential permit, the $30.00 one, and over 65, then you can purchase the
$3.00 lifetime permit as long as you own that vehicle. He noted the permit will have to be
renewed every time they get a new vehicle. Discussion followed regarding residential
eligibility. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public comment,
the motion passed unanimously.

CURRENT PRE-DREDGING SEDIMENT REPORT DATA
Deborah Mays, of Gulf County Citizens Coalition for a Healthy Future appeared before
the Board to provide a presentation regarding the analysis of the 2014 and 2023 pre-

dredging sediment reports to date at the Port and their inaccuracies <copy provided to
the Clerk for the record>, discussing two (2) toxin chemical groups, Dioxin/Furans and
Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAH). She requested the Board to consider obtaining
accurate data by (1) getting a sample down to the full dredging plan depth, and (2) taking
a second sample deeper and analyze these samples separately so it will be known how
much is exposed to the marine environment, as well as how much will be going up on the
upland sites as the dredging spoil. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to extend. Upon
motion by Commissioner McCroan, second by Commissioner Husband and unanimous
vote, the Board extended Ms. Mays time to speak. Ms. Mays continued with her
presentation and requested that the Board require a complete dredging plan, not just a
permit, from F.D.E.P. to correct these sampling deficiencies and other deficiencies that
can be found in the agenda report. She further stated they would like to see (1) the
deficiencies corrected so actual trusted data can be had before moving ahead, (2)
mitigate the risk for contamination spread during dredging, and (3) if necessary, dredge
deeper and place protected clean sand back on top (capping) of the toxic layers. County
Administrator Hammond stated the only project the County is funding, or has funded,
none of that happened before 2017; noting the 2014 report is Port Authority data and has
nothing to do with the County’s project. He reported the project is less than one-half of
one percent of the total dredging project and it is just for the floating dry dock there at the
bulkhead. Upon inquiry by County Administrator Hammond, Ms. Mays stated she is not
opposed to dredging or redevelopment of the Port but would like to see that the County
receives the appropriate data so if there is dredging, the toxin levels will be known and
what will be exposed to the marine environment. Discussion followed regarding the
railroad, clean industrial pro-jobs, dredging, and soil samples. Assistant County
Administrator Smallwood discussed the dredging project, noting D.E.P. has been involved
from the beginning and approved the sediment plan. He stated the County is in the
permitting process and drafting a dredging plan that will have safeguards in mind that
they are alluding to. Discussing followed regarding testing directed by D.E.P.

DEAD TREES – GULF COUNTY
John Howell, of Weber Construction, appeared before the Board to discuss his contract
with the State regarding the removal of dead trees. He apologized to the Board,
discussing the challenges with tree removal in and outside of the right-of-way. He
reported that D.O.T. (Florida Department of Transportation) typically does not address
trees outside of the right-of-way, discussing his working relationship with the County.
Public Works Director Cothran reported since the last Board meeting Mr. Howell’s group
has taken down 77 trees along U.S. Highway 71 and 98 and the County has removed
approximately 20 trees. Commissioner Rich discussed his concern regarding public
safety. Following discussion, County Administrator Hammond discussed the possibility of
taking a harder stance on property owners that have leaning trees; those that are on
private property that are leaning towards the County or State right-of-way. He also
discussed coming up with a solution, maybe running it through the P.D.R.B. about a
proposed ordinance that property owners have to cut the trees if they are in the fall zone
of the road. Discussion followed regarding possible code enforcement issues.

UPDATE REPORT – EROSION SOUTH-END / CAPE SAN BLAS
Assistant County Administrator Smallwood stated that the Berm project is still on track for
construction in November 2024, reporting there is a meeting later today regarding the
permit application. He noted the County will still need to obtain a permit from the Army
Corps of Engineers. Commissioner McCroan discussed timing construction around turtle
season. Following discussion regarding the timeline, Commissioner McCroan expressed
his concerns regarding the erosion at the south end, requesting County Administrator
Hammond to discuss this matter. County Administrator Hammond discussed the set-
aside funds for this project, reporting that 800,000 cubic yards of sand is going to go north
of that project. He stated this will extend the life of future beach renourishment, noting
there will still be a need for beach renourishment. Commissioner McCroan discussed
other small Counties relying upon Federal Agencies to renourish beaches after a
hurricane or presentable storm. He asked County Administrator Hammond to provide a
quick explanation of what COBRA (Coastal Barrier Resources Act) is and the reason why
Federal funds are not expended. County Administrator Hammond reported that the State
gives approximately $.38 on the dollar for beach renourishment based on a formal
regarding beach access, parking, etc., but because Cape San Blas is erroneously in the
COBRA the County is not eligible for Federal funds.

WATER PRESSURE – INDIAN PASS
Commissioner McCroan requested an update regarding the matter of water pressure at
Indian Pass. County Consultant Butler appeared before the Board to report there was an
issue at Indian Pass last month, stating once all the parts are delivered these problems
will quickly be solved. He stated once the water starts pumping from White City, it will
solve a lot of the problems, noting all the water is being pumped from the wells. Discussion
followed regarding the new plant at White City coming online. County Administrator
Hammond discussed his concerns regarding subdivisions that have added irrigation
wells, stating there are discussions about having the developers run a separate supply
line for irrigation. He reported the water pressure also depends on how far the home is
from the water line, stating if it is a deep lot, especially on the north Cape, and 1,000 feet
from the County’s service line, then a booster pump is needed on that lot. County
Consultant Butler discussed his concerns regarding the water pressure traveling not only
long distances from the main supply line, but also traveling up a three-story house. County
Administrator Hammond noted the County has three separate projects going, the well
and plant in White City, booster station in Jones Homestead, and then a massive line that
will go across the railroad tracks and tie-in. He stated at the next phase, the booster pump
at Jones Homestead will tie into LUCI I.

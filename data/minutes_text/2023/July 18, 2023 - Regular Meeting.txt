AWARD BID #2223-28 – GROUP HEALTH INSURANCE PROGRAM
County Administrator Hammond requested a decision from the Board regarding health

appeared before the Board to discuss the coverage with United Healthcare. County

STATUS REPORT – SAUL’S CREEK ROAD
County Attorney Novak discussed working with various State agencies on getting it
(Saul’s Creek Road) dedicated and abandoned, noting the Board voted in the past to
abandon and relinquish any rights to it. He reported there have been issues regarding
private property owners having future access to their private property to ingress and
egress, noting one of the problems was with Deseret Ranches having a portion of the

PERVIOUS PAVER REVIEW – ALTERNATIVE PAVING MATERIALS
Planner Crane noted Windolf Construction and Hydro Pavers are present; stating the
request is to allow their paver system to be considered pervious under the LDR (Land
Development Regulations). He reported with a 3-0 vote, the P.D.R.B. recommendation to
the Board is to consider Hydro Pavers Paving System 100% pervious for driveways, pool
decks, and sidewalks, contingent upon County Engineer review and approval and upon
County Engineer approval that all projects will be permitted and inspected by the Gulf
County Building Department. County Engineer Smallwood stated that it is a good product
and he has reviewed the data, but it is not 100% pervious. Following discussion, Jeff
Parrish, Representative of Hydro Pavers appeared before the Board to discuss the paver
product and performed a demonstration of the product. County Engineer Smallwood
again stated it is not 100% pervious over the life, discussing coming up with a percent.
Upon inquiry by Chairman Quinn, County Engineer reported the recommendation from
the P.D.R.B. was 100% and he does not agree it is 100% but can come up with a percent
that the County can allow it to be considered as pervious. Commissioner Farrell stated it
would be prudent to set a number but also his recommendation would be to provide the
Building Department some latitude on site specific. County Administrator Hammond
discussed his concerns regarding stormwater, out-of-state developers doing big
developments on tiny lots, and property owners wanting to build 110% on a lot. He
cautioned the Board about establishing two different standards, a rich folk standard, and
normal folk. County Administrator Hammond requested that the Board allow County
Engineer Smallwood to provide a reasonable percentage, noting the County cannot have
a situation, unless the Board changes the rules, where you get 100% of the pervious area
is covered by a house and then keep adding and adding. He requested to allow County
Engineer Smallwood to come back at the next regular meeting with a recommendation
on the amount of impervious. Commissioner McCroan had no objection to the
recommendation by County Administrator Hammond. Mr. Parrish discussed the
comparison of the pavers and gravel. Deloris Windolf, of Windolf Construction, Inc.,
appeared before the Board in support of Hydro Pavers, stating they are not requesting
the County to change the 30% impervious surface ratio, which is any surface that rain
cannot go through, but asking for a viable solution that provides drainage, meets code
and is handicapped accessible for driveways, walkways, pool decks and patios. County
Administrator Hammond discussed the allowance of a three-story, 5,400 square foot on

that same lot, noting a property owner wants to build the biggest possible house, but the
simple solution is to build a smaller house or buy a different lot. Ms. Windolf restated that
they are looking for a viable solution through a product that can be offered to their clients
that drains as well as gravel but provides a hard surface. Commissioner McCroan
recommended that County Engineer Smallwood meet with the manufacturer and bring a
recommendation back before the Board. Chairman Quinn had no objection. County
Administrator Hammond discussed a possible variance on this particular lot, using this
product, but allowing County Engineer Smallwood time to come up with a percentage
going forward.

BUSINESS PERMIT ON THE BEACH – TREAT SQUAD
Coleen O’Malley, of Treat Squad appeared before the Board to provide material for review
<copy provided to the Clerk for the record>, requesting a permit to sell ice cream on the
County beaches and parks from a push-able ice cream cart. County Administrator
Hammond reported that the Board has a prohibition on County right-of-ways and parks
without special permission. He stated what is allowable is a commercial beach driving
permit on any beach that allows beach driving; the Cape, Indian Pass or Highland View
Beach, noting what would be extraordinary is if the Board allowed a permit to go on St.
Joe Beach or in a park that the County currently has a prohibition on. Commissioner
McCroan inquired if they could push the cart on St. Joe Beach. County Administrator
Hammond stated the Board would have to give them permission, noting the County
Ordinance states no businesses on the County right-of-way or parks and St. Joe Beach
is considered a park. Ms. O’Malley noted the County allows beach gear setup on all
County beaches, which is someone bringing chairs and umbrellas, setting up and taking
them down daily, beach picnics, and bonfires. Commissioner Rich stated he does not
have an issue giving special permission. Commissioner McCroan suggested separating
the parks out, discussing his concerns regarding the parks being overrun by vendors.
Upon inquiry by Ms. O’Malley receiving special permission in the parks during festivals,

County Administrator Hammond stated the County does not allow any commercial
activities in any County park without a special permit through the Board from T.D.C. and
insurance, but under a music festival, the business would fall under that event. He
reported that if Ms. O’Malley has a County business license and holds a County
commercial beach driving permit, then she would need special permission from the Board
to be on St. Joe Beach and Beacon Hill. Following discussion and upon inquiry by
Commissioner Farrell, County Administrator Hammond stated that with a County
business license and a commercial beach driving permit for the Cape beaches, Indian
Pass and Highland View it is covered, but for St. Joe Beach and Beacon Hill it would
require a special permit issued by the County for a non-motorized cart, not in parks, just
on the beach. Following further discussion, County Administrator Hammond stated the
County needs to be clear this is not on right-of-ways or parks due to liability and other
rules. Chairman Quinn stated he has no objection. County Attorney Novak discussed a
vote of the Board to authorize a special permit on St. Joe Beach and Beacon Hill, noting
there are other mechanisms in place for the Cape, Indian Pass and Highland View
beaches. He stated if it becomes a volume of requests then the Board can come back to
consider an ordinance. Chairman Quinn called for a motion to move forward.
Commissioner Rich so moved. Commissioner Farrell seconded the motion. Chairman
Quinn called for public comment. Gretchen Mayes, of Beacon Hill appeared before the
Board to inquire about the permanent white tents being set up and the ability to obtain a
permit from the T.D.C. to have your tent up for a week, noting the County’s Leave No
Trace Ordinance. Commissioner Farrell and County Administrator Hammond stated it is
part of the Leave No Trace Ordinance. County Attorney Novak discussed special events
and the outlet where a permit can be obtained from T.D.C. under the Leave No Trace
Ordinance. Chairman Quinn called for additional public comment. Francine Vellanti, of
Cape San Blas appeared before the Board to discuss her concerns regarding not being
allowed to sell her honey but the Board is granting Ms. O’Malley a permit. County
Administrator Hammond stated he does not have the authorization to break the law or the
Ordinance, noting the Board has the authorization to give variances or give permits. He
also stated she was told to ask the Board and the Board said no. Following discussion,
Commissioner McCroan stated the Board did not give Ms. O’Malley permission on the

FIRE CHIEF OF THE YEAR – BARRETT
Emergency Management Director Herring introduced State Volunteer Coordinator
Charlie Frank, FL Fire Chief Association Board President Chief Harold Theus, Assistant
Director of the State Fire Marshal Office John Gatlin, and Pace Fire Rescue Chief Robbie
Whitfield. Chief Theus appeared before the Board to present an award to Michael Barrett

award and the support within Gulf County. Commissioner McCroan congratulated Chief

and the benefit the volunteers do for Gulf County.
STATUS REPORT – GULF COUNTY E.D.C.
E.D.C. Director McKnight appeared before the Board to provide a status report regarding
economic development within Gulf County; reporting the County now has eye services
through PanCare at their new clinic located on Cecil G. Costin, Sr. Boulevard. He
discussed working with the Port Authority on a proposal to be submitted to the Port
Council to build the Bird Island Jetty to protect Eastern Shipbuilding operation, Port South
Marina, and the other areas beside Eastern Shipbuilding, in the amount of $12 million.
County Administrator Hammond reported the Gulf County Health Department made an
offer to the Eye Clinic regarding renting office space; noting the Board would have to
approve any contract that would come up using office space within the Health Department
Building. Mr. McKnight discussed the space available for rent at the Health Department;
noting medical or health would be the preference.

STATUS UPDATE – HURRICANE MICHAEL PROJECT
Referring to page 6 of the Administration packet (and Item #7 on the Agenda), Assistant
County Administrator Smallwood discussed various projects ongoing since Hurricane
Michael, totaling over $100 million. Upon inquiry by Commissioner McCroan, Assistant
County Administrator Smallwood reported the 30A and U.S. Highway 98 cut-through
(road) is listed under the American Rescue Plan; noting staff will need to sit down with
D.O.T. (Department of Transportation) to iron out the details for this road project. County
Administrator Hammond stated this project is fully budgeted, but D.O.T. wants the County
to install a turn lane and the County is not in agreement. Commissioner Farrell thanked
staff for carrying $109 million worth of projects for the County. Upon inquiry by
Commissioner Husband regarding the completion timeframe for Hardy Street in
Overstreet, Assistant County Administrator Smallwood reported the design phase is
ready and the pot of funds to be used required the County to update some policies, which
the County has done, therefore it is currently under State internal review. Upon inquiry by
Commissioner McCroan regarding Madison Street in Oak Grove, Assistant County
Administrator Smallwood stated plans should be ready after Labor Day and the
advertisement to receive sealed bids in September. County Administrator Hammond
noted this report does not include the $30 million the County has already completed.
Commissioner Rich requested staff to assemble a slideshow once these projects are
completed and present a presentation for the public.
ALTERNATIVE PAVING MATERIALS – BUILDING/PLANNING DEPARTMENT
Assistant County Administrator Smallwood directed the Board to page 7 of the packet,
recommending that: 1) any of the alternate paving materials would come through the
Technical Advisory Committee to approve and assign a ratio; and 2) start requiring
permits for driveways. Commissioner Husband inquired if gravel would be reviewed as
well. Assistant County Administrator Smallwood stated gravel does need to be reviewed
as well. Commissioner Husband discussed his concerns regarding the impact of this new
rule to include gravel. County Administrator Hammond stated their recommendation is

that part one (#1) will be effective immediately and part two (#2) be included in the
Building Ordinance; noting the first reading is today. He stated it would provide staff a
month to work out the details and timeline. Following additional discussion and concerns,
Chairman Quinn called for a motion to move forward with the recommendation. County
Administrator Hammond noted the recommendation is: 1) any of the alternate paving
materials would come through the Technical Advisory Committee, effective immediately;
and 2) start the process to include permit requirements for driveways in the Building
Ordinance. Chairman Quinn again called for the motion. Commissioner Rich so moved.
Commissioner McCroan seconded the motion for discussion. After discussion,
Commissioner Rich amended his motion to approve recommendation #1 and to table #2.
Commissioner McCroan stated his second stands. Chairman Quinn called for public
comment. There being none, the motion passed unanimously.
RED TIDE EVENT – LOCAL STATE OF EMERGENCY
County Attorney Novak discussed the red tide event and presented a proposed local state
of emergency proclamation <copy provided to the Clerk for the record> for the Board to
consider. He provided the high points within the proclamation. Chairman Quinn called for
a motion to move forward. Commissioner McCroan so moved. Commissioner Farrell
seconded the motion. Chairman Quinn called for public comment. There being no public
comment, the motion passed unanimously. County Administrator Hammond discussed
the County allowing local fisherman the opportunity to catch the fish before reaching the
beach shoreline; noting that FWC (Fish and Wildlife Commission) would rather pay the
County after the dead fish hit the beach, runs tourist off, and makes the public sick to
clean up and haul to the landfill than allow the fisherman to catch these fish before hitting
the beach. He reported it cost the taxpayers over $100,000.00 following the last red tide
event to clean up the fish once on the beach.
FIRST PUBLIC HEARING – BUILDING DEPARTMENT ORDINANCE
Pursuant to advertisement to hold a Public Hearing to consider amending the Building
Department Ordinance No. 2022-12. County Attorney Novak directed the Board to page
10 of the Administrator’s packet; noting this is the first reading of the proposed ordinance.
He discussed the high points recommended by staff. County Attorney Novak reported the
second reading will be held on September 26, 2023. Commissioner Husband inquired if
there was a minimum lot size that applies regarding the lot clearing or construction.
Building Official Bailey stated that for the clearing and filling, it is designed for areas that
are affected by neighboring subdivision. Members of the Board inquired if this needs to
be specified in the language (within the proposed ordinance). County Attorney Novak
stated there is the ability to make modifications following today’s discussion. County
Administration Hammond discussed holding a workshop to discuss this matter further.
Following discussion, County Attorney Novak requested for the purpose of the Public
Hearing that Chairman Quinn call for public comment. Chairman Quinn then opened to
the public, calling for public comment regarding the proposed ordinance. There was no
public comment. County Administration Hammond suggested holding the workshop on
September 6, 2023, at 4:00 p.m., E.T. The Board had no objection. Chairman Quinn
called for public comment. There being no public comment, Chairman Quinn closed the
Public Hearing to the public.
